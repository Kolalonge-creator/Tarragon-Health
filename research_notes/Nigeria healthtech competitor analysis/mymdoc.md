# MyMDoc (mDoc / mDoc CompleteHealth™, mymdoc.com)

**Research access note:** mymdoc.com is behind Cloudflare bot-protection and returned HTTP 403 / a Cloudflare block page to every direct fetch attempt (WebFetch, curl, and a real rendered browser session all hit "Sorry, you have been blocked" / "Attention Required! | Cloudflare"). No CAPTCHA-bypass was attempted, per research constraints. All marketing-site content below is therefore reconstructed from (a) Google's indexed snippets of mymdoc.com subpages, (b) the Google Play Store listing (which duplicates large portions of the site's own product copy), and (c) third-party citations/interviews that quote the company directly. Anything not independently confirmed is flagged as such.

**Identity note:** The company's legal/brand name is **mDoc** (also styled "mDoc Healthcare"); its flagship consumer product is **mDoc CompleteHealth™**, hosted at mymdoc.com. It is a Nigeria-based (Lagos-headquartered) chronic-disease/preventive-health/care-coordination platform — a direct structural competitor to Tarragon Health.

## Marketing website — headline/CTA copy, design, tone

### Takeaway
mDoc's public-facing messaging centers on self-care, coaching, and "virtual health companion" framing rather than a hospital/clinical brand voice; its site is organized around condition-specific landing pages (diabetes, hypertension, oncology, behavioural health, women's wellness, weight management) plus segment pages for employers and health plans/HMOs, mirroring a B2C+B2B2C go-to-market.

### Cited Findings

- Its stated vision (paraphrased from the About page) is to be the leading virtual health companion that instills confidence and empowers people to live healthier, more fulfilled lives; its stated mission is to ensure a healthier and more fulfilled world — [About Us | mDoc CompleteHealth™](https://mymdoc.com/about-us)
- The Google Play listing (which reuses site copy) opens with the line "Virtual Self-care, Health Coaching, Health Metrics, Webinars & Goal Tracking" as its subheadline — [mDoc CompleteHealth™ on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- Site/app copy frames eligibility around risk factors: living with a chronic condition, concern about abdominal weight, a parent/sibling with Type 2 diabetes, age 45+, or a history of delivering a baby over 4kg — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- The app/site cites a WHO-sourced statistic that chronic conditions account for over 28% of premature, preventable deaths in Nigeria, used as the platform's core justification — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- The site has distinct URL structure for condition verticals (diabetes, pre-diabetes, hypertension, oncology, behavioural health, women's wellness, weight-management-and-fitness) and separate B2B pages ("mDoc for Employer" at /employer, "Health Plans" at /health-plans, "Self Care Plans" at /self-care-plans) — [search-indexed mymdoc.com URLs, multiple queries]
- The site runs a blog/publications section (mymdoc.com/blogs, mymdoc.com/publications) covering topics like an African Digital Health Summit presentation on mDoc's AI approach, and a "Digital Mom Project" maternal-health piece — [mymdoc.com/blogs](https://mymdoc.com/blogs/innovations-in-digital-health-presenting-mdocs-ai-driven-approach-at-the-african-digital-health-summit-2024-07-17-13-07-70-703)
- A branded closing line used in the app-store description is "mDoc cares!" accompanied by green-heart emoji, and the description makes heavy use of emoji bullets (⚕️🏃👩‍⚕️📊💪📅🩺📓🗂️🔍) as a visual/tone device — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)

### Inferences
- The disclaimer-forward, "self-care support" (not "treatment") framing suggests a deliberate regulatory-positioning choice — mDoc markets itself as a coaching/adherence layer that complements, not replaces, clinicians, likely to stay outside stricter medical-practice regulation in Nigeria — similar in spirit to Tarragon's own "clinical review and escalation" (not "doctor-led") positioning discipline.
- The condition-vertical page structure (one URL per disease state, each presumably with its own hero/CTA) mirrors a common healthtech content/SEO pattern (landing pages per condition to capture organic search intent) — plausible but unconfirmed since the pages themselves returned 403.
- Segmenting the site into consumer, employer, and health-plan/HMO tracks indicates all three customer types (B2C, B2B employer benefit, B2B2C insurer) are actively marketed on the same domain, not siloed into a separate B2B site.

## Product functionality

### Takeaway
mDoc CompleteHealth™ is a coach-led (human coach + AI-assisted), subscription-based virtual self-care platform for chronic disease and general wellness, built around a structured "Propel Program," in-app health/diary tracking, and a network of physical "NudgeHub" touchpoints and provider-directory/tele-education products that sit alongside the consumer app.

### Cited Findings
- The flagship product, CompleteHealth™, is described as a "coach-led, multidisciplinary self-care experience" pairing members with dedicated coaches, covering diabetes, hypertension, obesity, cancer, pregnancy, and mental health — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- Core subscription benefits: a 24-week "Propel Program" with weekly coaching sessions; a dedicated health coach for personalized lifestyle action plans; a personal health dashboard to log blood pressure, blood glucose, exercise, etc.; weekly virtual exercise classes with a fitness coach; appointment booking with a multidisciplinary team (nutritionists, emotional-health coaches, etc.); and free blood glucose/BP tests at select in-person NudgeHub™ centers — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- Named in-app features: "My Diary" (health-metric logging feeding coach-created action plans), "Health Passport" (stores full medical history — conditions, medications, procedures, surgeries, providers), and "Symptom Tracker" (logs symptom type/intensity over time) — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- mDoc operates a "four-pillar" model beyond the consumer app: CompleteHealth™ (virtual coaching), NudgeHubs™ (physical community hubs offering BP machines, exercise classes, nutrition programs, tech support), NaviHealth.ai™ (a geo-coded directory of reviewed health services — facilities, labs, diagnostic centres, pharmacies — that lets women leave feedback), and the mDoc Quality Network / MQN™ (tele-education/training for healthcare providers) — [AI World](https://aiworld.eu/story/mdoc-provides-ai-driven-disease-support-across-nigeria); [PSI Member Spotlight description via search snippet]
- mDoc's AI layer is a chatbot/virtual coach named **Kem** (from "nkem," Igbo for "my own"), built on an LLM (reported as using GPT-4-class models), available 24/7, able to respond in English and Nigerian Pidgin, used for chronic-disease and pregnancy-related self-care Q&A — [Gates Foundation](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/ideas/articles/nneka-mobisson-mdoc-healthcare); [search snippet citing Borgen Project/Medium coverage]
- mDoc describes a "human-in-the-loop" safety design for Kem: guardrails, manual review, and escalation to human coaches for sensitive cases — [search snippet, Frontier Tech Hub / related coverage]
- NaviHealth.ai verifies labs listed in its directory in partnership with Nigeria's Medical Laboratory Science Council — [search snippet]
- mDoc's revenue model is explicitly diversified and for-profit: consumer tiered-pricing plans (with some free services), B2B employer contracts (coaching as an employee benefit), B2B training (MQN tele-education sold to healthcare facilities/organizations across Africa), and B2G capitation-style fees from state health-insurance plans under Nigeria's National Health Act — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/building-for-profit-health-model-that-reaches-the-poorest)
- mDoc reports 150,000+ members, disproportionately women, most earning under $2/day (market women, petty traders, drivers) — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/building-for-profit-health-model-that-reaches-the-poorest)
- Only 5% of Nigerians have health insurance, cited by mDoc/Yale as the structural gap its capitation/B2G channel targets — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/building-for-profit-health-model-that-reaches-the-poorest)
- Separate reported impact metrics (source: aiworld.eu, drawing on mDoc's own reporting): 84% of members show improved chronic-condition management; 15,000+ trained healthcare workers; 2.28 million patient interactions — [AI World](https://aiworld.eu/story/mdoc-provides-ai-driven-disease-support-across-nigeria)
- mDoc explicitly supports low-connectivity access via USSD on basic/feature phones (not solely a smartphone-app product) — [AI World](https://aiworld.eu/story/mdoc-provides-ai-driven-disease-support-across-nigeria)
- mDoc's founder states the company deliberately avoided venture capital ("didn't want to lose control or our focus on social impact") and instead bootstrapped via grants while building revenue lines, wanting to prove for-profit work at low-income populations was possible — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/building-for-profit-health-model-that-reaches-the-poorest)

### Inferences
- Unlike Tarragon Health's doctor-tier ladder (Care Coordinator → Medical Officer → Senior Medical Officer → Chief Medical Officer) with per-action DB-enforced clinical authority, mDoc's model appears to be coach-led with clinicians further back in the chain (multidisciplinary team reachable via booking) — this reads as a lighter-touch, adherence/behavior-change-first model versus Tarragon's doctor-escalation-first model, though mDoc's exact clinical-staffing/escalation architecture is not publicly documented in comparable detail.
- The Kem chatbot with declared "human-in-the-loop" escalation is conceptually similar to what Tarragon's `ai-governance` module and `runGovernedAi()` registry are built to formalize — but there's no public evidence mDoc has an equivalent registered-AI-system/audit-trail/kill-switch architecture; this is inferred absence, not confirmed absence.
- The B2G "capitation fee from state health insurance plans" revenue line is notable because Tarragon Health's CLAUDE.md explicitly states Tarragon's founder-narrowing decision was "no capitation, ever" — this is a genuine strategic point of differentiation between the two companies, not just a feature gap.
- The four-pillar structure (app + physical hubs + directory + provider tele-education) suggests mDoc's moat/differentiation leans on the *physical* NudgeHub network and its B2B training/data business (MQN) more than on the consumer app alone — the consumer app's modest download count (see Mobile Apps section) supports this reading.

### Gaps
- No confirmed details on lab/pharmacy fulfillment logistics (e.g., whether mDoc integrates order/delivery workflows, vs. NaviHealth.ai being purely a directory/referral tool).
- No confirmed details on wearables integration — not mentioned in any source found.
- No confirmed details on telemedicine/video-consult infrastructure (vendor, e.g., Zoom-equivalent) — "appointment booking with a multidisciplinary team" is mentioned but the modality (video/audio/in-person) is not specified in found sources.
- Consumer pricing tiers/amounts could not be found publicly (see Marketing section gap).
- No independent verification of the 84%/15,000/2.28M impact figures beyond mDoc's own reporting as relayed by aiworld.eu — treat as company-sourced claims, not third-party-audited.

## Mobile apps — App Store / Google Play presence, downloads, ratings, reviews

### Takeaway
mDoc CompleteHealth™ is Android-only via Google Play (10K+ downloads, package `com.mDoc.completehealth`), with no visible public star rating or reviews section despite that install count — a real reputation-visibility gap; there is no live iOS app (an iOS waitlist is referenced but not a shipped product), and mDoc also publishes a separate companion app, "mDoc LMS."

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed directly via the Google Play listing (browsed live): app name "mDoc CompleteHealth™," developer "mDoc," content rating "Everyone," **10K+ downloads**, last updated Sep 16, 2026, category "Health & Fitness" — [mDoc CompleteHealth™ on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mDoc.completehealth)
- The live Play Store page, as rendered, shows **no star-rating figure and no visible "Ratings and reviews" section at all** — no aggregate score, no review count, no individual review text surfaced anywhere on the page — confirmed by direct browser inspection of the listing.
- The Play Store "Data safety" section states: no data shared with third parties; app may collect personal info, health & fitness data, and 4 other data categories; data is encrypted in transit; users can request data deletion — [Google Play listing, browsed live]
- "What's new" for the current version reads only "Bug fixes and performance improvements" — no changelog detail — [Google Play listing, browsed live]
- Play Store shows a second app under the same developer: **"mDoc LMS"** (a learning-management-system app, presumably supporting the MQN tele-education pillar for healthcare providers) — [Google Play listing, "More by mDoc" section, browsed live]
- Per a search-engine-summarized citation of the mymdoc.com site (not independently verified), an iOS version is "in development with a waitlist" rather than live — [search snippet citing mymdoc.com]
- Two other, unrelated apps surfaced in App Store searches for similar names and must **not** be attributed to this company: "MDOC Wellness" (App Store id1589490464) is a wellness app whose only visible review explicitly references "Michigan department of corrections" — i.e., a US prison-system wellness app, a false-positive naming collision, not Nigeria's mDoc. "mDoc™" (App Store id1467167427) and the Google Play "myMDOC" real-estate-CRM app (package `com.mdocbox`) are likewise unrelated products that share the name.

### Inferences
- The complete absence of a visible ratings/review section on a listing with 10K+ downloads most likely means the app has too few *written* ratings to clear Google Play's disclosure threshold (Google suppresses the public rating summary below a minimum review count) — this is itself a competitive-intelligence signal: engaged, review-leaving usage appears low relative to raw installs, or the app is young enough on this exact package ID that reviews haven't accumulated.
- No shipped iOS app despite a "waitlist" signal is a real distribution gap versus a competitor targeting the full Nigerian smartphone market (roughly evenly split iOS/Android in urban/higher-income segments that are also the more monetizable subscription base) — plausible strategic reason: Android-first matches mDoc's stated low-income/market-women target demographic, where Android device share is dominant.

### Gaps
- No representative sample of positive or negative user reviews could be obtained for the real mDoc CompleteHealth app — none are surfaced publicly on its Play Store page, and no iOS listing exists to check. This directly could not satisfy the "read positive and negative reviews, extract recurring complaints/praise" objective; flagging as a hard gap rather than inventing sentiment.
- Could not confirm exact current download count precision beyond Google's bucketed "10K+" label, nor country-level install breakdown.
- Could not confirm the "mDoc LMS" app's own install count, ratings, or purpose in detail (inferred to support MQN provider tele-education based on naming/context only).

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

### Inferences

## Business/network signals — funding, partners, press, team

### Takeaway
mDoc is a bootstrapped-by-choice, grant-funded (not venture-funded) Lagos company founded in 2013 by Nneka Mobisson (CEO) and Imo Etuk (CTO/COO), with roughly 50–95 employees depending on source, a small disclosed grant-funding total (~$148K–$248K across trackers, likely understating true lifetime grant funding given ongoing named partnerships with Gates Foundation, Roche, MSD for Mothers, USAID, GIZ and others), and a self-reported footprint of 150,000+ members plus tele-education reach into 25 African countries.

### Cited Findings
- Founded 2013 in Lagos, Nigeria, by CEO Dr. Nneka Mobisson and CTO/COO Imo Etuk — [StartupList Africa](https://startuplist.africa/startup/mdoc)
- Nneka Mobisson's founding motivation: her father, a prominent Nigerian technologist (worked on early DECnet infrastructure, founded Nigeria's first science and technology university), suffered a preventable stroke from uncontrolled hypertension in Nigeria and died in 2010 after being flown to the US for care — [Gates Foundation](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/ideas/articles/nneka-mobisson-mdoc-healthcare)
- Mobisson holds degrees from MIT (public health), plus medical school and business school training — [Gates Foundation](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/ideas/articles/nneka-mobisson-mdoc-healthcare)
- Headquarters address on file: 1A Hakeem Dickson Road, Lekki Phase 1, Lagos, Nigeria — [StartupList Africa](https://startuplist.africa/startup/mdoc)
- Employee count reported as 11–50 (StartupList/Crunchbase-derived) versus 89–94 (LinkedIn-derived, per search-engine summary) — sources disagree; noted as a conflict rather than resolved.
- Disclosed/tracked funding: Crunchbase-derived figure of **$248K total**, from a mix of grants/non-equity assistance — Google for Startups ($100K, October 2021), MassChallenge, and ~$148K in additional grants between 2015–2019 — [Crunchbase, via search snippet]; a separate StartupList Africa pull shows **$148K total tracked**, backed by Google for Startups, Google, and MassChallenge — [StartupList Africa](https://startuplist.africa/startup/mdoc) — **these two totals conflict**, likely reflecting different snapshot dates/data completeness, not two different facts.

- Named partners/collaborators across sources: Roche, Gates Foundation, Google for Startups, WHO, USAID, GIZ, Ashoka, Merck for Mothers, plus Nigerian State Ministries of Health and local government facilities — [AI World](https://aiworld.eu/story/mdoc-provides-ai-driven-disease-support-across-nigeria); [search snippet, PSI/related coverage]
- mDoc partners with Nigeria's Medical Laboratory Science Council for lab verification on NaviHealth.ai — [search snippet]
