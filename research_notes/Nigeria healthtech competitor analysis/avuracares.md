# Avura Cares (avuracares.com) — Competitor Research Note

## Marketing website: homepage, product/feature, pricing, about — copy, tone, UX patterns

### Takeaway
Avura Cares markets itself as a Nigeria-focused **preventive healthcare-tech company** built around telemedicine, home-based (domiciliary) care, and a low-cost entry plan (₦5,000/month); the site is a small, single-page (anchor-link) marketing site with a warm, community/empathy-forward tone, including Nigerian Pidgin phrasing in its hero copy — a notably different voice from Tarragon Health's more clinical/institutional positioning.

### Cited Findings
- Homepage hero copy (verbatim, under 15 words): "No wait make body knock ground — Prevention still better pass cure." — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Value-proposition line: "Avura Cares connects you to preventive healthcare wherever you are." — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Secondary tagline: "Personalized healthcare plans that work for you — anytime, anywhere." — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Company description: "Avura Cares is a preventive healthcare-tech company providing preventive, affordable and accessible care across Nigeria." — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Mission statement: "Delivering quality care at every stage of life — early, easy and everywhere." — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Vision statement: "Make healthcare accessible, preventive and emphatic for every Nigerian." (site's own spelling — likely intends "empathic") — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Stated core values: Empathy, Innovation, Accessibility, Community, Prevention — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Origin-story framing: the company positions itself as "born from frustration with hard-to-access hospitals, expensive treatments, and a lack of trust," aiming to meet patients "where you are" — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Pricing copy: "Our plans start at ₦5,000/month for basic telemedicine access, tailored for Nigerians seeking affordable home-based preventive care." — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Site navigation is a simple 4-item menu: Home | Services | About | Contact | FAQs — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Listed service lines on the homepage: Outpatient Clinics; Telemedicine (described as toll-free consultations, remote monitoring, mobile access); Domiciliary Care (home-based medication support, health monitoring); Health Insurance (tailored plans covering consultation and pharmacy); Nutritional Profiling; Ambulance Support (emergency and non-emergency transport); mental-health/"emphatic listening" sessions; Occupational Therapy and physiotherapy — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Contact channels listed: an on-site contact form, WhatsApp number +234-703-825-0202, and Instagram/Facebook/Twitter/WhatsApp social links — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Requesting `/about`, `/services`, and `/pricing` as separate URLs each returned HTTP 404 — confirms the marketing site is built as a single scrolling page with in-page anchor sections rather than discrete subpages (unlike Tarragon Health's multi-route marketing structure) — [avuracares.com/about](https://www.avuracares.com/about) (404), [avuracares.com/services](https://www.avuracares.com/services) (404), [avuracares.com/pricing](https://www.avuracares.com/pricing) (404)

### Inferences
- The Pidgin-English hero line ("No wait make body knock ground") signals a deliberately local, informal, relatable brand voice aimed at mass-market Nigerian consumers rather than an upmarket/clinical tone — a different positioning lane from Tarragon Health's "Care that stays with you" branding.
- The single flat entry price (₦5,000/month) suggests a simple, low-friction consumer offer rather than a multi-tier plan ladder (no evidence of multiple named plans/tiers was found — see Gaps).
- Because the site is a single page with no dedicated app/pricing/product subpages found, Avura Cares' web presence reads as an early-stage/lean marketing footprint rather than a mature, content-rich SaaS-style site.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether there are multiple pricing tiers beyond the ₦5,000/month "basic telemedicine" entry point (e.g., a higher tier bundling insurance/domiciliary care) — the fetched content did not enumerate a tier table.
- Could not directly inspect page HTML/source for design/UX pattern details (hero image style, testimonial carousel, trust badges, pricing-table layout) — the fetch tool returned extracted text only, not visual/DOM structure.
- No blog or resources section was discovered or confirmed to exist.

## Product functionality — patient/clinician features, chronic disease support, screening, labs/pharmacy, telemedicine, wearables, HMO integration, payment model

### Takeaway
Publicly described functionality is limited to a short marketing list of service categories (telemedicine, domiciliary/home care, an insurance-like plan product, nutrition profiling, ambulance dispatch, mental health support, and physiotherapy/occupational therapy); no evidence was found of chronic-disease-specific programs, lab/pharmacy network integration, wearables, HMO partnerships, or a described doctor/clinician-facing product — these appear to be either unbuilt, undocumented publicly, or simply not surfaced on the marketing site.

### Cited Findings
- Telemedicine is described only generically as "toll-free consultations, remote monitoring, mobile access" — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Domiciliary care is described as bringing "qualified healthcare professionals directly to your home" for "home-based medication support, health monitoring" — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- "Health Insurance" is listed as a service line with "tailored plans with consultation and pharmacy coverage" — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)
- Additional listed offerings: Nutritional Profiling; Ambulance Support (emergency and non-emergency transport); mental health/"emphatic listening" sessions; Occupational Therapy and physiotherapy — [avuracares.com](https://www.avuracares.com/)

### Inferences
- The product appears positioned as a general wellness/primary-care and home-care bundle rather than a chronic-disease-management platform (Tarragon Health's core wedge) — this is a meaningful positioning difference worth flagging in the comparative report.
- The phrase "Health Insurance...tailored plans" combined with the flat ₦5,000/month price suggests a subscription-style bundled-benefits product (similar in shape to an HMO-lite/direct-primary-care model) rather than a true underwritten insurance product, but this is inferred from marketing copy only and not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No information found on whether Avura Cares has a mobile app at all (see App Store section below), a patient portal, or any clinician/doctor-facing dashboard.
- No information found on lab or pharmacy network partners, wearable integrations, HMO partnerships, or specific chronic-disease (diabetes/hypertension) programs.
- No information found on how the "Health Insurance" plan actually works (underwriter, claims process, network) — likely requires a signup/login to observe, which is out of scope per research constraints.
