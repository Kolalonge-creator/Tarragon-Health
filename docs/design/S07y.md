# S07y design note: blood pressure "taken at a pharmacy or clinic machine", and the paper diary

Invariants in play: INV-01 (no model in any triage or red-flag path), INV-07 (events and notifications carry no reading), INV-10/12 (no new staff read path), INV-13 (`is_test`), INV-14 (go-live guards), INV-16 (versioned configuration), plus the standing rules: English only, strings through `packages/i18n`, "your care team", no new clinical threshold, nothing signed by an agent. Research: `docs/research/S07y.md`. Nothing here is switched on: three guards are born OFF and one AI system is registered DISABLED.

**Delivery.** Built in two pull requests. Part 1 carries the database layer (one migration and its proof, which cover both behaviours, so they are proved together) and the pharmacy label on web and phone. Part 2 carries the paper diary screens, routes and the AI-021 call site. Sections 5, 7 (diary events) and the diary files in section 10 describe part 2; until it merges the two diary guards and the AI-021 registration are inert rows.

## 1. The decision that shapes everything: a label on the ordinary reading, not a new source value

A new `vital_source` value would silently skip the glucose triage wiring, allow backdating, and fall outside the source lock and the dedupe class (research note section 1). So both new behaviours are stored as **`source = 'manual'`** readings with a frozen label:

- `vitals_readings.entry_origin` (null for an ordinary reading): `pharmacy_machine` (blood pressure only) or `paper_diary` (blood pressure, glucose or pulse). A CHECK holds the combinations.
- `vitals_readings.place_label` (optional, 1 to 80 characters, pharmacy label only). Never used for any decision.
- Both columns are in the frozen-column list of `a_clinical_value_supersession`, so a person cannot relabel a reading afterwards (relabelling a pharmacy reading as home would change every home average). A correction is a supersede, as for any recorded value.

Because the label rides on `manual`, **every one of the 28 triggers fires for a labelled reading exactly as for a typed one**: plausibility hold, the BP, pulse, glucose, SpO2 and temperature red-flag engines, the emergency safety net, `observation.recorded`, the monitoring bookkeeping. The proof (section G) shows a labelled 205/128, a labelled severe-low glucose and a labelled pulse of 190 fire exactly what an identical ordinary reading fires, with a non-vacuous control.

## 2. Time rules (the no-backdating rule is bounded, not removed)

`private.stamp_manual_vitals_timestamp()` now does three things for a labelled reading:

1. Refuses it unless its guard is open for the person (`private.go_live_open_patient`: test accounts pass, as everywhere), with `55000`.
2. A pharmacy reading keeps the ordinary rule: the device time inside 72 hours, otherwise server time.
3. A diary reading keeps the person's stated time inside `offline_sync_config.diary_window_days` (PROPOSED 30, mirrored as `vitals.diary_import` with a drift test) and is **refused with `22023`** outside it, with no date, or in the future. It is never silently stamped as "now": an old value dated today would look like a live reading. `time_basis = 'diary_stated'` records which rule applied. `received_at` stays server time.

**Corrections.** `public.correct_clinical_value` supersedes a reading by copying it (label included) and re-inserting it with `client_recorded_at` set to the old `taken_at`, which re-runs this trigger. Found by the code review: without a rule for that, a diary reading older than the window could never be corrected (22023), a correction after its guard was switched off failed (55000), and a pharmacy reading corrected after 72 hours was silently re-stamped as now. A correction whose time is **unchanged** now keeps the time it had and needs no guard (it creates no new labelled reading); a correction that **changes** the time is judged by the ordinary rules again (proof section I). `supersedes_id` can only be set by the correction function, so this cannot be used to skip the rules from outside.

The screens validate the same window first and say why in words ("That date is more than 30 days ago, so it cannot be added here. You can show the paper diary to your care team."). The check also exists in the database, so a hand-made request gets no further than the screen does.

## 3. What a labelled reading is left out of

| Calculation | Pharmacy | Diary | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| The signed 7-day home protocol, `home_bp_assess` (so also the white-coat / masked comparison, which reads `home_bp_checks`) | out | out | `entry_origin is null` |
| The 7-day home average, `hbpm_average`, and the window statistics, `bp_window_stats` | out | in, labelled | `entry_origin is distinct from 'pharmacy_machine'` |
| The phone's 7-day average and its home check | out | average in, home check out | `homeBpReadings()` / `toHomeReadings()` in `@tarragon/shared` and the phone |
| History, Trends, Health Passport, doctor summary | shown with the label | shown with the label | `SourceBadge`, trend caption, passport and PDF suffix |

Both views (`private.vitals_readings_current`, `_diagnostic`) gain the two columns at the end, so every reader that selects `v.*` sees them. Left unchanged and recorded in OQ-S07Y-2: the ML control-rate assessment, `risk_compute`, outcome snapshots, the S75 cohort view, health report and FHIR export still treat a pharmacy reading as any cuff reading.

## 4. Go-live guards (all OFF, CMO's switch, no conditions)

`pharmacy_bp_readings` (the label), `paper_diary_entry` (typed rows), `paper_diary_ai_reader` (the photo; the read also needs `paper_diary_entry`, since a photo of a health diary is not sent to a paid model for a person who cannot then save the result). They are separate on purpose: the typed sheet is useful with no model and must never depend on the model's switch. **No conditions are defined**, so none can be switched on from the dashboard until a later change adds them (the S43 reasoning: `private.go_live_conditions` is patched by many branches and is not restated here). Enforcement is in the database (the stamp trigger) and again in the handlers and screens, so a stale page or a crafted request is refused with words. **The one exception is the pharmacy label on a dangerous reading's path:** if the label's guard turns out to be closed at the moment a labelled blood pressure is saved (a stale page, or a reading the phone queued before the switch went off), the reading is NOT refused: it is saved as an ordinary manual reading with the setting kept in its note, triaged as always, and the person is told (`pharm.label_dropped`; `label_dropped: true` in the phone's answer). A dangerous value must never be lost to a go-live switch; with the guard off the system behaves exactly as it did before the label existed. (Found by the second code review: the first build refused it with 403 and the phone's outbox then kept it as "not accepted", so the server's red-flag triage never ran for it.) `go_live_guard_is_open` answers for the signed-in caller, not for a person they support, so the handler pre-checks only for the caller's own account and, for someone they support, lets the trigger judge (a refusal comes back per row as "closed"); the pharmacy box and diary link on screen follow the caller's guard, which differs from the subject's only for a test account.

## 5. The paper diary

### 5.1 Typed sheet (works with no model)
Web `/patient/diary`, phone modal from the Vitals screen. Rows of blood pressure (top, bottom, optional pulse) or glucose (value, unit, when taken), each with a date and a time. One shared pure module (`@tarragon/shared` `diary-import` and `diary-sheet`) validates and summarises for web, phone and server, so they cannot drift. A row with a problem says why beside the row; a row that is saved leaves the sheet; a failed save keeps everything; at most 20 rows per save; online only (a diary row is kept only if it is inside its window when it arrives, so it is never queued).

### 5.2 Photo (suggestions only; AI-021, off)
`POST /api/patient/diary/read` (cookie) and `/api/mobile/diary/read` (bearer) share `handleDiaryRead`: guard check, file check (the declared size is checked before a byte is loaded; image only; at most 8 MB), a per-person ceiling of 20 reads an hour (`vitals.diary_import.max_reads_per_hour`, the platform limiter; 429 `rate_limited`), then **only** `runGovernedAi` with `AI_SYSTEMS.paperDiaryReader` (`AI-021`, fail closed). The model transcribes text as written; our code turns it into editable rows that **start unchecked**; a date or time or number that cannot be read with confidence is left empty; a unit is never guessed; "morning" is never turned into a clock time; a time written with no am or pm that could be morning or evening ("8:30", "08:30", "0830") is left empty too, because a guess would shift the reading by twelve hours (only "8.30pm", "20:15" and the like are filled). **Nothing is stored**: the route has no table access, the photo stays in the page (web) or on the phone, and the audit trail receives counts only. Every row needs the person to tick "This row matches my diary"; editing a suggested row clears the tick.
Fallback (guard off, registry off, no key, a failure, an unreadable page): the typed sheet, with a plain sentence.

### 5.3 Confirm and save
`saveDiaryAction` (web) and `POST /api/mobile/diary/save` (phone) share `handleDiarySave`. The server re-validates every row with the same pure rules, judged by the window the **database** will apply (`offline_sync_config`, read at save time, so the check and the trigger cannot disagree; the registry value is the fallback), requires `confirmed: true` in the request schema, and inserts each reading through the **ordinary** `vitals_readings` insert (`source manual`, `entry_origin paper_diary`, `client_recorded_at` = the stated time, never a `taken_at`). A pulse is its own reading, as the phone's quick-log saves it. Row ids are UUIDs made when the row is created, so a retry or a second press of Save stores nothing twice (`23505` counts as already saved) **and still runs the post-insert assessments and returns the signed wording**: when the first answer was lost this is where a dangerous value is explained, and the post-insert checks may not have run the first time (found by the code review). A row that matched a reading already in the record from a better source leaves the sheet and is counted apart ("Matched a reading already in your record from your device, so not added again"). After the inserts the same post-insert assessments run as for a typed reading (BP control, heart rate, glucose), once per kind, through `runBestEffort`; a failure is **reported in the answer** and the screen says so in words; it is never a plain "saved". The held (impossible-value) and merged outcomes are read back (only after an empty answer, so a normal save is one round trip per reading) and are not reported as saved; every part of a row is tried, so a blood pressure linked to a reading already there never stops the pulse beside it from reaching the pulse red-flag engine.

### 5.4 Dangerous values
Exactly as a live entry: the database triggers fire; the web shows the same signed wording a typed reading gets (`vitalSavedMessage` with the worst band) and the dashboard's emergency alert follows from the emergency event; the phone runs `classifyVitalOffline` on the rows **before** the save is sent and shows `EmergencyGuidanceModal` at once, with no network. No model is involved at any step. Whether a stale value (up to 30 days old) should page is a CMO question (OQ-S07Y-4); as built it behaves as asked.

## 6. States covered on each screen
Empty (one blank row), loading (reading your page, saving), offline (save blocked with words, rows kept), error (each failure kind has its own sentence), guard off (an honest "not switched on yet" page; the photo area says so and the typed sheet stays), partial success (saved rows leave, the rest stay with reasons), accessibility (labels on every field, `role="alert"` on problems, ticks shown by icon and state, never by colour alone, 44 pt targets on the phone), shared phone (nothing is stored on the device: no draft, no photo, no suggestion; the web keeps the photo only in the open page).

## 7. Events
No new event type. A labelled blood pressure emits the existing `observation.recorded` (ids only); glucose emits its existing observation event. The brief's "outbox events ids only" is met by those; adding a `diary.imported` event would add a type with no consumer.

## 9. Phase B (a pharmacist records a reading at the counter): designed, not built

**Idea.** A patient at a partner pharmacy opens a short-lived code in their app; the pharmacist enters it with the machine's reading; the patient confirms in their app; only then does a reading exist.

**What would be reused.** S28's pharmacy partner and location tables and licence fields, the hashed single-use code with a five-try lock (`prescription_collection_codes` pattern), the audited `SECURITY DEFINER` pharmacist functions, and the seam PR #1185 adds (`private.pharmacy_assisted_entry_allowed`, which returns false until its question is answered).

**Why it is not built here.** It needs decisions beyond the invariants (OQ-S07Y-8): (1) whether a pharmacist's reading is authored by the pharmacist or by the patient who confirms it, and what the record shows; (2) which pharmacy roles may write, and who carries the duty of care when the pharmacist sees a dangerous value at the counter; (3) code shape and lifetime, and the threat model for a code read over a shoulder; (4) INV-10/12: the pharmacist must get a write-only door and read nothing of the record; (5) whether the machine's make and calibration must be recorded; (6) rate limits and abuse; (7) that no pharmacy is active today (OQ-274), so nothing could be exercised for real; (8) it should be one decision with the assisted-entry seam, not a second seam beside it. A clean build is small once those are answered: one request table, three functions, one event, a reuse of `pharmacy_machine` as the label with a "recorded by" fact.
