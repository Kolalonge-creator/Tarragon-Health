# S39 Design: security, privacy and compliance hardening

Spec section 13, Part D.3 to D.6. Depends on S38. Invariants touched: INV-07, INV-10, INV-12, INV-13, INV-16. Research: `docs/research/S39.md`.

## 1. What exists (extended, not rebuilt)
Row-level security on 702 tables, audited clinical reads for about 10 core tables (S05, S22e), a break-glass function and CMO review, auth and SMS rate limits, account lockouts, breach runbook, DPIAs and DPA drafts, a data-rights request workflow, `data_retention_policies`, GitHub secret scanning with push protection, CodeQL and Dependabot.

## 2. Method
A read-only review of the live catalog and simulated sessions, split into six areas: RLS for every table and role, audit coverage, secrets and rate limits, retention and deletion and export, backups, processors. Every finding is either fixed here with a proof, or recorded in `docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` (OQ-260 to OQ-269) when the fix conflicts with how the platform works today or needs counsel.

## 3. Built
1. `s39_security_catalog.sql`: one test over every table and role. RLS on for all tables in public, private and analytics; anon grants and anon EXECUTE equal to reviewed lists; every SECURITY DEFINER function pins its search path; owner-rights views equal a reviewed list; public buckets equal a reviewed list; a stranger in a brand-new organisation (patient, clinician, care coordinator, finance) and anon read zero rows about anybody else from all 289 `patient_id` tables. Not covered yet: the 177 tables with an organisation but no patient id, and the admin role (OQ-260). A new table, grant, function or view that breaks a rule fails the test until someone reviews it and adds it to the list. Four sabotages must flip.
2. Migration `20261007104237_s39_security_hardening_round1.sql`: row-level security switched on for `analytics.rpc_snapshots` (it was off); failed guesses on the emergency card and record share doors counted per hour (no token stored) with one security incident at a configured threshold (`security_config`, versioned, mirrored as `security.rules` in the PROPOSED registry with a drift test); the two live bugs in `record_share_by_token` fixed. The doors are never blocked, because the emergency card must open for a stranger in an emergency.
3. `s39_public_lookup_failures.sql`: counting, alert once, valid lookups add nothing, anon cannot read or call, all seven share sections open (regression), two sabotages.
4. `secret-scan.yml` and `.gitleaks.toml`: full-history gitleaks in CI (not a required check; a false positive must not block a hotfix).
5. Documents: `docs/PROCESSORS.md`, `docs/legal/questions-for-nigerian-counsel-S39.md`, OQ-260 to OQ-269.

## 4. Reviewed lists (change only with a reason)
anon tables: consent versions, public impact metrics, testimonials (patient and doctor), marketing resources, passport signing keys (public keys only). anon functions: eleven public or token-verified RPCs, listed in the test. Owner-rights views: eight, each with its own caller predicate, a public directory, or no grant. Public buckets: clinical staff photos, patient avatars (OQ-265).

## 5. Not built, and why
Tied-only reads with audit on 226 tables (OQ-260), in-org break-glass and CMO alert (OQ-261), erasure (OQ-262), retention periods (OQ-263), complete export (OQ-264), private avatars (OQ-265), PITR (OQ-266), more rate limits (OQ-267), DPAs (OQ-268). Each needs a founder or counsel decision first, and OQ-260 would change behaviour clinicians rely on.

## 6. Safety rules
1. No change to `private.is_org_staff` and no weakening of any policy.
2. The emergency card door is never rate-blocked.
3. Counters hold no token and no caller detail.
4. A failure to record a failed guess is loud, never swallowed.
