# S08z design note: dose times anchored to daily events

Extends S08 (`docs/design/S08.md`), S08b to S08f (windows, midnight, the database refusing what the phone refuses) and S08x. Reconciled against `origin/main-dev` and the live project on 2026-10-20. Nothing here signs anything, turns a guard on, or changes a medicine, a dose or a plan (INV-02): an anchor changes only when a patient's own reminders and Today list fall.

## 1. What the feature is

A patient can tie a dose to a part of their day instead of a clock time: waking, breakfast, lunch, dinner, bedtime, or one of the five daily prayers ("with breakfast", "30 minutes after the morning prayer"). Nothing is computed from location or a calendar. The patient says, once, what time each part of their day they use is. A "my day has shifted" control moves chosen parts by chosen minutes for a chosen run of dates (a month of fasting, a trip) and then stops applying by itself. The schedule keeps its spec; only the resolved Lagos clock times change.

An anchor is a scheduling convenience the patient picks, never advice. No string suggests taking a dose at a different time than prescribed, and anchors are only offered on patient-added medicines (a prescribed schedule is still fixed by the care team: the existing column allow-list already refuses a patient edit of `schedule_spec` on those).

## 3. Resolution model (the part that can silently go wrong)

- A spec carries `anchored: [{anchor, offsetMinutes}]` beside `times` (which may be empty). A resolved dose is `anchor time + the day's shift + offset`, in minutes.
- **Midnight crossing.** An anchored dose belongs to the SCHEDULE day it is resolved from, so one that lands after 24:00 lands on the NEXT Lagos date (23:30 bedtime plus 60 minutes is 00:30 tomorrow, never 23:59 today), and one before 00:00 lands on the PREVIOUS date. `slotsOn(date)` looks at the day before, the day itself and the day after and keeps what resolves onto `date`; start and end dates and the every-N-days and weekday rules apply to the schedule day. The SQL does the same. One consequence, stated plainly: a dose that resolves past midnight is listed on the new date (as any dose at 00:30 is), not on the evening before.
- **No drift on days already lived.** Resolution of a past date must never change, or a dose logged at 08:15 would reappear as "no record" and the server missed job would write a false "missed". So:
  - anchor times are **versioned by date** (`patient_dose_anchors(patient, anchor, effective_from, local_time)`): a change applies from its own date and earlier dates keep the earlier time. The proposed default is the implicit first version.
  - ending a shift records `cancelled_from`; days before it keep their shifted times.
  - a change or shift that would start today while today already has a logged answer on a medicine tied to those anchors **starts tomorrow** (`private.anchor_day_has_answers`, yesterday to tomorrow). The database applies this rule and wins; the phone applies the same rule so what it shows is what the server will hold.
  - an effective date is never in the past EXCEPT for a change made on a phone that was offline and sent up to 3 days late: it keeps its own date only when no dose on an affected anchor has been logged since (`private.anchor_logs_since`), so the doses the patient logged meanwhile, at the times the phone showed, stay consistent; otherwise it is clamped to today (and to tomorrow when today has answers). A shift ended by the phone that created it can name it by the client id it sent.
- **Shifts.** Newest shift naming an anchor wins where two cover a date. At most 45 days (`max_shift_days`, PROPOSED, a table constraint mirrors it), at most 360 minutes either way per anchor.
- **Windows.** A flexible window may not overlap the next dose. The check constraint cannot see a patient's anchor times, so two triggers close the gap: one on `medications` (an anchored spec with a window whose doses at the person's normal times are closer than the window is refused) and one on `patient_dose_anchors` (a deferrable constraint trigger: an anchor time that would put any active medicine's doses closer than its window is refused, judged on the FINAL state of one save, so moving breakfast and lunch together is judged as a pair). The phone checks the same way first (`anchorChangeConflicts`, all changes together) and says why. **A day shift is not checked against windows**: during a shift two anchors may end up closer than a window for those days; the due state then reads "due" a little longer, never wrongly "taken" or "missed" (accepted, recorded; OQ-S08z-6 if it should be enforced).
- **Legacy list.** `medications.schedule_times` keeps listing the normal look of all doses (clock times plus anchored doses at the person's current anchor times at the moment of saving), for older readers. Resolution never reads it for an anchored dose.

## 4. Data model (one migration, `20261021011847_s08z_dose_time_anchors.sql`)

`dose_anchor_defaults` (10 PROPOSED starting times, mirrored by `medicines.dose_anchors` and a Jest mirror test), `patient_dose_anchors`, `patient_day_shifts` (RLS select for the person and an acting guardian ONLY: which prayers a person ties a medicine to discloses religious belief, so tied clinicians and caregivers do not read these tables, and the migration's closing check fails if a read policy ever names a clinical reader; **no insert/update/delete policy or privilege**: the three RPCs `save_dose_anchor_times`, `create_day_shift` (idempotent by client id), `cancel_day_shift` are the only write path; each takes the patient, so an acting guardian can manage a dependant; anon cannot execute). The two patient tables are listed in the S39d data registry (unreviewed, as the standing rule requires).

## 5. Phone (Expo)

- `anchor-profile.ts` holds the person's profile in memory (server rows mirrored locally plus anything still in the outbox) and `scheduleOf` attaches it to every spec, so the Today list, catch-up, adherence, supply and the reminder planner all resolve the same way. With nothing read yet, the proposed defaults apply: an anchored dose still shows at a sensible time.
- **Staying in step**: once an anchor change or shift has been sent, the person's anchors are read back from the server (the row has left the outbox and the server may have chosen a different effective date); a change the server refused stays visible as a rejected row but is not applied; a person whose anchors the phone has never read gets one read from the server first (at most once a minute), so times set on another device are not ignored.
- **Offline-first**: three new outbox kinds (`anchor_times`, `day_shift`, `day_shift_end`). A change is applied on the phone at once and sent when there is a connection; a shift not yet sent is simply removed if ended. A refused change is kept and reported like any rejected row.
- **Reminders** keep generic keyed wording (INV-07). When every dose in one notification is tied to the same part of the day the body says so in plain words ("Time for your care plan check, with breakfast."); otherwise the unchanged generic text. The notification id carries the anchor so a changed wording replaces the old notification.
- **Screens**: "Tie this to my day" in the add-medicine form (anchor chips, offset chips in plain words, the time to confirm, plain explanation that it is not advice), "My day" section (time per anchor, "my day has shifted" with per-part movement, active and upcoming shifts, "Return to my usual day"). 44 point targets, accessibility labels and roles, every state shown, live-region messages.
- The Today list and the catch-up sheet show the part of the day in plain words beside the dose.

## 6. Web

A dose tied to the patient's day is shown on the web Today list only once the patient's own times have been read (a note says so while they have not): at the proposed times it could show a time that is not theirs and an answer logged against it would sit on a slot the server does not hold. `MyDayCard` on the patient Medications page (same two controls, native time and date inputs, axe-tested), `AnchorChooser` in the add form (patient self-add only), the Today list, the dashboard summary and the printed adherence report resolve against the same profile. The report refuses to print (rather than printing times worked out from the wrong anchors) if the patient's own times cannot be read.

## 7. Not built, recorded

- Clinician-prescribed schedules tied to the day (a prescriber choosing "with dinner"): not built; patients cannot tie a prescribed medicine to their day. Prescribed reminders keep their exact times.
- No care-team view of a patient's shifts. The tables are not readable by clinicians (religious belief), so a care team sees only the resolved dose times through the ordinary records. A patient who shifts the day for a fast is making a decision their care team may want to be part of: what to surface, without naming a prayer, is open (OQ-S08z-2, OQ-S08z-5).
- `medications.schedule_times` (the legacy list for older readers) is written once, at the person's normal anchor times when the medicine is saved, and is not rewritten when an anchor time later changes. Resolution never reads it for an anchored dose; it matters only to a reader that ignores `schedule_spec`.
- The server-side dose reminder queue (`queue_medication_dose_reminders`) reads resolved times through the patched call; it was checked structurally and not exercised end to end against real notification delivery.
- No device or simulator run (see the unverified list in `docs/BUILD-PROGRESS.md`).
