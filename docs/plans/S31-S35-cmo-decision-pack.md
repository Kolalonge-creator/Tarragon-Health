## C1. Breathing exercise BRE-01: pace, length and safety card (OQ-305, OQ-300)

**Why it is yours.** `breathing.bre01` is a PROPOSED value owned by the CMO. The card is visible to patients today while the lessons are not.

**As built.** Four seconds in, six out (about 6 breaths a minute), three minutes, a gentler variant (3 in, 5 out), a one minute variant. Silent pacer, optional haptics, safety card before first use, Stop always visible. It never claims to lower blood pressure and always says to keep taking medicines and measuring. Stop list: dizziness, tingling, chest pain, new breathlessness, racing heart. Ask-the-care-team-first list: lung disease, heart rhythm problems, problems in pregnancy.

**Guideline basis.** Slow breathing at 6 to 10 breaths a minute gave a modest average fall in the meta-analysis; effect varies and device-guided breathing alone showed no benefit in one analysis. Major hypertension guidelines (NG-HTN, AHA/ACC 2025, ESC 2024, NICE NG136, WHO HEARTS) do not present app-guided breathing as treatment `[verify per guideline]`. Hence "a calm moment", never a treatment claim. The stop and ask-first lists are not taken from a guideline; they are reasonable safety wording and need your clinical judgement.

**Options**
- A. Confirm as built (4/6, 3 min, current stop and ask-first lists) and keep it named BRE-01 "Three minute calm". Recommended.
- B. Confirm as built, and also ship BRE-03 "Slow breathing for blood pressure" now with a longer length.
- C. Confirm the pace but edit the safety lists (you supply the wording).
- D. Hide the card from patients until you have signed it.

**Recommendation: A.** The evidence supports a modest effect only, so keep the claim-free framing and do not lengthen the session before real use data exists. If you want the card hidden in the meantime, choose D.

**Why it is yours.** All 14 lessons were written by a build session from the Production List briefs. No clinician has read them. Founder decisions already recorded: only the CMO approves a lesson and the CMO's name is recorded; the understandability test runs before any lesson is published.

**What you are confirming.** The claim checklist at `docs/research/s33-understandability/cmo-review-checklist.md`. Key claims and their basis:

The S11 research found 2 readings 1 minute apart, morning and evening, over 3 to 7 days as the common protocol.

- Warning signs: chest pain, severe headache, weakness or numbness, speech difficulty, sudden vision change, breathlessness: matches AHA public advice, NICE, ESC 2024 and WHO HEARTS (see C3 for the emergency number).
- No lesson gives a numeric target, a dose or a drug name. The target is the patient's own care-plan target.

**Options**

## C3. One emergency instruction: the number in BPC-13 versus EMG-001 (OQ-87, OQ-203)

**The inconsistency.** The lesson BPC-13 says "call one one two or go to the nearest hospital emergency department". The signed emergency wording EMG-001 deliberately prints no phone number (OQ-87). Two safety messages in one app should not differ.

**Guideline basis.** AHA public advice, WHO HEARTS, NICE and ESC all say: call emergency services or go to hospital now for chest pain, severe headache with the other warning signs, weakness or numbness, speech or vision change, and do not drive yourself if faint, confused or weak. Nigeria has a national emergency number (112) but coverage and response reliability are uneven `[verify; field-check in pilot areas]`. The reason for omitting a number was reliability, not a clinical objection to calling.

**Options**
- A. Neither the lesson nor EMG-001 prints a number; both say "go to the nearest hospital emergency department now; ask someone to call emergency services" and "do not drive yourself if faint, confused or weak". Recommended until 112 is field-tested in the pilot areas.
- B. Both print 112 (edit EMG-001 and its audio script; re-sign).
- C. Keep as is: lesson prints 112, EMG-001 does not (not recommended).
- D. Make the number a per-region config value, filled only for regions where it has been verified.

**Recommendation: A now, D when a region is verified.** It keeps the one rule, avoids a number that may not connect, and does not delay the first recording.

## C7. AI scribe (AI-017): retention and the pass bar before it is switched on (OQ-213, OQ-214)

**Why it is yours.** AI-017 is off. Governance blocks it until two evaluation suites pass, and nothing has been run. Each evaluation represents a human clinical judgement; none is seeded.

**Guideline basis.** WHO guidance on ethics and governance of AI for health calls for human oversight, transparency and accountability `[verify]`. NDPA 2023 purpose limitation and MDCN confidentiality apply to consultation audio and transcripts `[verify]`. Published studies (secondary snippets) report omissions (about 18 percent in one pilot) and hallucinations (about 11 to 31 percent depending on study) in ambient scribe notes, clustered in allergy, medicine and negation data, and found that LLM judges are poor at catching omissions. This is why the build uses a closed facts-to-confirm list and per-section confirmation, not a model that checks itself.

**Two decisions**
1. **Retention of audio and transcript.** (a) Discard after the visit; keep only the signed note and hash. Recommended: it minimises personal data, fits NDPA purpose limitation, and removes the need for a transcript store; cost: no evidence link from note text to transcript span. (b) Keep for a short fixed period for evidence links and quality sampling (period set by you and counsel; consent text must say so). (c) Keep indefinitely (not recommended).
2. **Pass bar for switching on.** Run on local, consented Nigerian-accented audio; classes allergy, medicine and dose, negation, follow-up are critical. Options: (a) zero critical-class misses and zero invented facts surviving to the signed note across a sample you set (suggest at least 100 consultations, a proposed figure for you to change), plus reported word error rate with the threshold set after you see the local baseline; (b) a numeric error-rate ceiling per class set now; (c) pilot with two named clinicians under audit sampling first, then decide.

**Recommendation:** retention (a); pass bar (a) with (c) as the first stage. Do not set a word-error threshold before seeing a local baseline; the published Nigerian-language figures are far too weak to borrow.

## F1 research addendum (2026-10-07, public sources, not legal advice)

Read directly: Aluko & Oyebode overview of the Deduction of Tax at Source (Withholding) Regulations 2024 (effective 1 July 2024); a 2026 employee-versus-contractor guide. Search summaries: EY, KPMG, Mondaq, Grant Thornton on the Regulations and the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (in force 1 January 2026).
- **Rate.** 5 percent (was 10) on commission, consultancy, technical, management and professional fees to a resident; 10 percent to a non-resident. Withholding is an advance credit against the recipient's own tax, not their final tax.
- **No TIN, double rate.** A recipient with no tax identification number is withheld at twice the scheduled rate (10 percent for resident professional fees). So **a clinician's TIN must be on file before payment**; the S31 tax profile already stores it, and S31c should refuse approval without one.
- **Remittance and receipt.** Remit by the 21st of the following month (federal; the article names FIRS, which the 2025 reforms replaced with the Nigeria Revenue Service, so confirm the authority) or the 30th (state). The payer must issue the recipient a receipt or credit note so they can claim the credit.
- **Small-company exemption.** Applies to a small-company supplier with a valid TIN and a monthly transaction value of N2,000,000 or less. The sources disagree on the definition of a small company (turnover N25m in one, N100m turnover and N250m assets in another) and one article does not exclude any services. It is about companies, not individuals. Counsel to confirm, and I withdraw my earlier statement that professional services are excluded.
- **Employee or contractor.** Seven substance-over-form tests (control, integration, tools, exclusivity, financial risk, substitution, continuity). If a clinician is in substance an employee, PAYE and pension apply instead and Tarragon owes employer pension. The guide says nothing on healthcare, on-call or retainer pay. Per-task fees look like contractor pay; on-call shift fees and a fixed per-patient lead fee, paid to someone working only for Tarragon under its protocols, lean toward employment. This is the question that decides the whole design and is why counsel is the right first step.
- Net effect on the plan: the S31c work (rate as versioned config, deduction line, credit note, TIN required, monthly remittance report) is confirmed as the right shape; the rate, the authority and the contractor status per clinician type wait for counsel.
