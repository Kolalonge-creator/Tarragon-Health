# S08z research note: why dose times anchor to the day, and what must not go wrong

No web research was run for this note (no network tool was used). It records the product reasoning and the safety reasoning behind the design, and what still needs a human to check.

## Why anchors

Many patients organise a day around events (waking, a meal, a prayer, bedtime) more than around a clock. A reminder at a clock time that no longer matches the day is ignored. During a month of fasting the day moves wholesale; a patient who sets a clock time once and then has to rewrite every medicine twice a year will not do it. Letting the patient name the event, and shift the day for a run of dates, matches how they already think.

## What the patient sets, and what we never do

- The patient sets the time of each part of their day. We compute nothing from location or a calendar and make no claim about prayer times or the dates of a fast. The starting times are placeholders chosen to be plausible for Lagos (OQ-S08z-1) and are confirmed by the patient the first time they use an anchor.
- An anchor is a scheduling convenience. No copy suggests taking a dose at a different time than prescribed. Anchors are offered only on patient-added medicines; a clinician-prescribed schedule stays as written.

## What must not go wrong

1. **History must not move.** If changing "breakfast" rewrote last week, every dose logged at the old time would turn into "no record" and the server missed job would write a false "missed" for a patient who took everything. Hence versioned anchor times, shifts that end without rewriting the past, and a rule that a change starting today moves to tomorrow when today already has an answer on it.
2. **A dose across midnight lands on the right date.** 23:30 plus an hour is tomorrow at 00:30; slots are keyed by date and time, so the wrong date orphans the answer.
3. **The database must refuse what the phone refuses**, including overlapping windows that depend on the patient's own anchor times, which a check constraint cannot see (triggers close it).
4. **A shifted day for a fast can matter clinically** (for example for diabetes or blood pressure medicines). The app does not advise and does not hide it: tied clinicians can read a patient's shifts under the existing access rules, but no screen shows them yet. The CMO should decide how a fasting plan is surfaced and counselled (OQ-S08z-2).

## Open for decision
