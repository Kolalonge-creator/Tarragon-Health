# S17 Research: "Next task" claim queues

Public web sources only (2026-10-06). **Health vendors publish almost nothing about claim, lease, decline or scoring mechanics**, so "not published" below is an unknown, not a confirmed gap. The mechanics worth copying come from non-health systems (Salesforce, Zendesk, SQS, SKIP LOCKED queues). Health platforms inform ordering, target times and eligibility filters only. Doctolib, Glooko, Cadence and Abridge: nothing relevant found; nothing is inferred about them. Adapt, do not copy.

## Eligibility and routing
- Wheel and OpenLoop filter by patient location, specialty or symptom match and active licence before anything else. Amwell adds language and per-service-line licensure, and offers tiered pools (a customer's own clinicians first, then overflow). Ordering, timers and scoring are not published.
- Salesforce Omni-Channel and Zendesk use skills plus capacity: a per-channel concurrent-item cap, with capacity released when work is declined or finished.
- Adopt: hard filters first (active, licence and indemnity in date via S15's `clinician_is_eligible`, tier, competency, conflict, declared availability), ranking after. Owned-clinician-first maps to our lead window and push path.
- Avoid: using marketing claims (for example "16,000 clinicians") as evidence of mechanism.
- Nigeria: MDCN registration is the equivalent of a state licence. A task offered outside a freelancer's declared shift is a harm, so declared availability is a hard filter.

## Priority ordering and target times
- Manchester Triage targets to first assessment: red 0, orange 10, yellow 30, green 90, blue 120 minutes (sources disagree on yellow and blue). It is time to first contact, not to resolution, and a nurse assigns the class.
- Zendesk priorities are per queue, not per item, and strict queue priority starves low classes.
- Adopt: nine classes then `due_at` then age (spec 7.6), targets held as data. Starvation backstop: S16's overdue sweep escalates; add a queue-health "oldest open per class" measure.
- Avoid: reading NHS-style numbers as validated for Nigeria. The CMO signs every value.

## Claim, lease and timeout
- SQS: a visibility timeout (default 30 seconds, max 12 hours) that a worker can extend, a receive counter, and a dead-letter queue after N failed receives.
- Salesforce: push with a timeout; unaccepted work returns to the queue; a lone eligible rep who declines is not re-offered the same item.
- Graphile Worker and the SKIP LOCKED pattern: claim in one statement, record who holds it and until when, and run a reaper for expired leases. The row lock lasts only for the claim transaction; the lease is a timestamp column, not the lock. Retries use backoff.
- Epic In Basket pools: explicit Take (others see "taken"), Put Back, Done. Timeouts for a stale taken item are hospital policy, not software, so a taken item can sit unseen.
- Adopt: a lease timestamp (`claim_expires_at`, already in S16), a reaper, the claimant recorded, a dead-letter equivalent that is a human escalation (`escalated`), never a parked task.
- Avoid: a long fixed lease with no way to extend (a crashed clinician blocks work), and any design where a timeout silently closes or drops a task.
- Nigeria: power cuts and poor data. Every call is idempotent (a retry returns the same claim, never a second one), a dropped connection is never treated as a decline, and the lease is generous with an explicit release.

## Hand-back
- Salesforce releases capacity on decline but its decline carries no reason. OpenLoop, Wheel, Amwell: decline handling not published.
- Adopt: a required reason code (spec 7.6), a handed-back task is never re-offered to the same clinician, and repeated hand-backs go to the clinical lead for review (more than 3 in 7 days, PROPOSED).
- Avoid: a silent skip, which would let a clinician cherry-pick by repeated "next".
- Gap: a `conflict_of_interest` hand-back should also record a conflict so the patient is never offered again.

## Cherry-picking and fairness
- General queueing practice (inference): no browsing, server picks, the clinician sees counts by class until a claim; patient identity opens only after the claim through the audited read; skips are logged and rate-limited; aging or a breach clock prevents low classes waiting forever.
- Fatigue: concurrency cap (1 for urgent work), shift limits, a visible pause. Resident doctors in Nigeria report 24 to 72 hour on-call stretches, so do not assume constant availability.

## Reliability scoring
- Not published by any health vendor found. Standard practice (inference): count only delivered, acknowledged offers; separate a reasoned decline (neutral) from a silent timeout while online (penalised); exclude windows when the platform was down; rolling window with decay and a prior for new clinicians so small samples do not swing the score; use it to break ties only, never to gate an emergency, and never to change pay without a person reviewing.
- Our inputs (spec 7.8): on-time completion, claim timeouts, hand-backs, audit results.

## Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria
- Auto-closing or silently dropping a task on timeout.
- SMS or USSD paging as a queue fallback (INV-08; OQ-113 chose email).
- Anything that treats lost connectivity as unreliability without server-observed evidence.
- Sending patient detail to a foreign vendor to route work (NDPA): a task row carries ids and neutral facts only.
