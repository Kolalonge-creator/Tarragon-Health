# S42 research: consent, privacy and dependants (functions 1.13 to 1.19)

Evidence caveat (same as the S41 to S45 plan): written from public product pages, help-centre knowledge and published guidance as remembered, plus the competitor notes in `docs/research/competitor-review-*.md`. No app was installed and no store reviews were read. Re-verify any specific claim before it is used in a decision. Nothing here is legal advice.

## 1.13 Granular consent by data type and purpose (Clue, Eka Care)
- Clue is the benchmark for sensitive-data consent: reproductive data is its own explicit choice, separate from the account, and is stated to be stored under European rules. Eka Care shows consent per record and per sharing, with a visible list of who holds access.
- Complaints in this category: one giant "I agree" that bundles marketing and research with care; consent that cannot be changed later without emailing someone; "turn off sharing" that also quietly turns off a feature (the Samsung Health lesson: withdraw a data permission and core screens stop working).
- Unsafe or unsuitable here: a withdrawal that silently reduces access to care; a consent screen that needs reading skill and bandwidth; wording invented by engineers (counsel has not approved any, OQ-49).
- Tarragon version: a 5 x 4 matrix (vitals, reproductive, mental health, documents, device data against care, Care Circle, research, sponsor reporting) in the database, with the rule that a needed-for-care cell cannot be withdrawn enforced by a trigger, not only by a screen. Defaults are bundles; sensitive types are never in a bundle for anything but care, so each is a deliberate tap in the "every choice" view. Care access never reads the matrix, and a test proves it.

## 1.14 Plain-language privacy summary with audio (Clue)
- Clue's privacy pages are short, specific and written for the person, with no legal vocabulary in the first screen.
- Complaints: policies that are long and cannot be read on a small screen; audio that exists only in one language or autoplays.
- Unsuitable: autoplaying audio on a shared phone, claims of "we never share" that the product does not hold to.
- Tarragon version: English only (Pidgin and other languages were removed by PR 984). The summary lives in `packages/i18n`, includes a line on the matrix, and carries the S32 audio seam (the words of script HLP-036 on a tap, and an honest "spoken help is not ready" until a recording is signed off). Nothing plays by itself.

## 1.15 Consent history, two-tap withdrawal, export, deletion (CareClinic)
- CareClinic offers an export of the record and an account-deletion option in settings; reviews mention export arriving as a spreadsheet nobody can read, and deletion that "removes" the login but leaves data.
- Unsafe: a one-tap deletion with no recovery window for a person on a shared phone; deleting clinical records that must be kept.
- Tarragon version: history is append-only and always visible; withdrawal is two taps (arm, confirm). Export stays admin-reviewed (founder 2026-09-07, OQ-50) and now has a PDF as well as the data file, rendered at download so a withdrawal or correction made since is already reflected. Deletion stays reviewed, and completing it runs an anonymiser that consults the retention policy in code: identity and login go, personal rows outside any retention category go, clinical, audit, financial and consent records stay (pseudonymised) because no statutory period is confirmed.

## 1.16 Notification settings, quiet hours, discreet wording (Flo Health)
- Flo's discreet options (hidden names and previews, a PIN) are praised by users with a partner or family looking at the phone; complaints are about reminders that still arrive at night.
- Unsuitable: SMS as a reminder channel (INV-08), a toggle that promises a channel the platform will not use.
- Tarragon version: quiet hours and discreet mode already existed (S13, push only). S42 removes the SMS toggle and column (code first, schema last) and applies discreet mode to email too: fixed words, no template text, no attachment.

## 1.17 and 1.18 Dependants and hand-over at 18 (Ovia Health; Tarragon decision, Nigeria-specific)
- Ovia lets a person manage family members from one account; the common complaint is that a grown child's data stays visible to the parent. Pregnancy apps with partner sharing show the same failure: sharing that was never meant to be permanent.
- Unsafe: a guardian who keeps reading an adult's record by default; a hand-over that needs a form nobody can find; reproductive data reachable through a general "manage" grant.
- Tarragon version (founder confirmed the spec rule): a row per dependant from 30 days before the 18th birthday, the event `dependant.handover_due`, and a card the young person sees at first sign-in. Guardian access ends on completion unless the young person ticks that guardian, and a kept guardian is view only. Written fresh for sensitive data: an adult's reproductive health is never implied by a proxy's manage grant.

## 1.19 Set up for my parent (Nigeria-specific)
- Competitors mostly assume the user is the patient; family-account features are invitation-first, and the complaint is invitations that give the inviter access before the invitee says yes.
- Unsafe: coercion (an adult child, or a spouse, insisting on access); a proxy who sees data before the parent confirms (safety case 23); a parent with no way out.
- Tarragon version: access exists only after the parent confirms with a code on their own phone (S04). S42 adds the parent's side: their own account always shows who set it up and when, with a one-tap end that cannot be undone by the other person, and a cooling-off before the same person can start again. The mobile app now has the full flow (set up, confirm, see, end).
