## 17.1 to 17.4 Invites, permissions, pay for a loved one, supporter view (built in S29; audit only)
- Reference products (Medisafe Medfriend, Dexcom Follow, Apple Health sharing) agree on one thing S29 already does: the owner invites, the invitee accepts, the owner can remove at any time. The recorded complaint is the supporter alert that arrives late (about an hour for Medfriend, **V** in the plan); S29 pages at once and keeps the text neutral.
- Audit result on this branch: the S29 invite pattern (hashed single-use token, expiry, identical failure) is correct and is the pattern reused for cohort invites. Two small things were fixable and are fixed here: the sign-in page dropped the join link when a new person chose "Create an account" (OQ-220, join link half), and the solo wellness challenge ending nudge named the challenge and carried a progress figure (INV-07). Not fixed, listed in OQ-388: native deep links, the cross-organisation rule, and whether Paystack's foreign-card setting is switched on (not checkable from here).

## 17.6 Group challenges inside private cohorts (Strava, Personify Health)
- Best products: Strava keeps clubs and group challenges with privacy zones; Personify and Wellable run workplace team challenges; Fitbit closed open challenges in 2023 and kept private groups (**V** in the plan). Omada pairs small peer groups with a coach.
- Complaints and harms (**V** in the plan): numbers fixation, guilt from streaks, and more pre-existing eating-disorder symptoms among heavy fitness-app users; Peloton-style leaderboards expose name, avatar and metrics.
- Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria: a church, mosque, union or estate group says something about a person's belief or work; a member list plus health numbers is sensitive data (B1); WhatsApp is the usual way groups form here but is removed from Tarragon (F-02); low data and shared phones mean short screens and no live feeds.
- Tarragon decision: closed cohorts joined by a single-use hashed link; two separate withdrawable consents (join, then contribute to totals); generic cohort names, checked against a term list; effort counts only (A16); the moderator sees first names and nothing else; no free-text posts, no profiles, no feeds.

## 17.7 Totals and rankings only (Strava; Nigeria-specific)
- Competitor leaderboards rank people. The founder decided 2026-10-07 that Tarragon ranks **cohorts only**; a member sees their cohort's total and "goal reached", never a member rank.
- Small-group arithmetic is the real risk: with a floor of 5 (the institutional rule in `apps/web/src/lib/institutions/suppression.ts`), a five-person cell group makes one person's number readable from the total. This module therefore uses a separate floor of 10 contributors (PROPOSED, enforced never lower by the database), a leave-one-out share cap, rounding, a publish delay, a band instead of a head count, and anonymous labels on the cohort board. The residual differencing risk (a person withdrawing between two published snapshots) is stated in OQ-381, not hidden.

## 17.8 Clinician-led live audio group sessions (Omada)

Only the `group_sessions` schema and a dormant `platform_modules` row exist; the table cannot be created while the module is off, cannot record or use the scribe (INV-11), is audio only and capped at 20. No room, no Zoom code. When it is built it needs S21's real-device testing, a clinician host tied to every attendee (INV-12), and a confirmed Zoom plan limit (**U**).

## 17.9 Moderation, opt-out, notifications (Strava)
- Strava lets members mute, block and report; the lesson is that a block alone is not moderation. Here: per-cohort mute, a global "community off", leave anytime, report-and-remove with enumerated reasons (no free text, so nothing needs reading for abuse), moderator invite/remove/close, admin freeze, and the platform switch. Every notice is one fixed line ("Your group has an update") that names no cohort, metric, figure or condition (INV-07); a milestone notice is sent only after its figure is published.

## What a premium Tarragon version does, and why
It treats a group as a support structure, not a scoreboard: effort not outcomes, cohorts not people, nothing a person did ever shown back to the group, and the two safest defaults everywhere (not available for you, totals hidden) where a number could point at someone.
