# S83 research: reference products for privacy, security and regulatory functions

Written from general knowledge of each product's public documentation and published behaviour as understood by this session (no live browsing was done for this note). Details may be out of date; treat them as prompts to check, not as facts about those products. Adapt, do not copy.

## Granular consent and withdrawal (Clue, Eka Care, OneTrust)
- Clue separates consent by purpose (for example research use of cycle data versus the core service) and lets a person change it later in settings; the core tracking works without the research choice.
- Eka Care frames consent around the ABDM consent artefact in India: a time-bound, purpose-bound, revocable grant per data requester, with a visible list of active grants.
- OneTrust and similar consent platforms keep a receipt per decision (what text, which version, when, which purpose) and make withdrawal as easy as the grant.
- Adapt: one row per decision in the existing append-only `patient_consents`, version recorded, a list of purposes with a two-tap toggle, same screen on web and phone.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: a generic cookie-banner style "accept all" bundle; a consent artefact designed around India's ABDM rails, which do not exist here.

## Audit trail of record access (NHS Summary Care Record, Epic Break-the-Glass)
- NHS style models let staff with a legitimate relationship open a record and log every access, with audit and alerting on unusual patterns.
- Epic style break-the-glass asks for a reason and flags it for a privacy officer.
- Adapt: S39c already logs each opening with no typed reason; add periodic review reports and decide whether sensitive categories need a reason.
- Unsuitable: relying on a reason typed under time pressure as the only control; patient-facing access-log portals as seen in some EHRs until counsel answers question 10.

## Discreet mode and neutral icon (Flo Health, Clue)
- Flo offers a PIN lock and notifications that avoid naming cycle events; some period trackers also ship a neutral alternate icon and app name.
- Clue offers an app lock and lets people hide sensitive text in notifications.
- Adapt: INV-07 already makes every notification condition-free; add an alternate neutral icon and an app-specific PIN as options; hide content in the app switcher.
- Unsuitable: a "decoy calculator" style hiding that could encourage a patient to hide a medical emergency; keep emergency guidance reachable. Also be careful about public claims: Flo's published data-sharing history with advertising SDKs is the cautionary example behind the D.3 no-ad-SDK rule.

## De-identified analytics and no ad SDKs (Flo, Clue, Eka Care)
- After regulatory action in other countries, health apps moved to first-party analytics or privacy-first tools and removed third-party advertising SDKs from health screens.
- Adapt: no analytics SDK today; if product analytics is wanted, send first-party events with a hashed id, a deny-list of fields, and a CI scan of dependencies.
- Unsuitable: free-text fields in analytics events; session replay on any clinical screen.

## Encryption and key handling (general: Eka Care, major EHRs)
- Most products rely on platform disk encryption plus TLS, and add field-level encryption only for the most sensitive notes.
- Adapt: decide with the DPO whether HIV, reproductive and mental health fields need field-level keys; start by getting the platform's written encryption statement.
- Unsuitable: rolling our own cryptography; keys stored beside the data.

## Regulatory map and compliance tracking (OneTrust, Vanta-style registers)
- Compliance platforms keep a register of obligations, owner, evidence, review date and status, and a processor register with transfer basis and DPA status.
- Adapt: the repository already has `regulatory_obligations` and `vendor_assessments`; make unsigned or unconfirmed status visible in the console and tie each row to evidence.
- Unsuitable: generic GDPR templates as proof of NDPA compliance; automated "compliance scores" that read as a regulator's approval.

## Symptom checker as a regulated product (Ada, Babylon, Healthily, Eka Care)
- Several symptom checkers have registered as medical devices in the EU and UK, and publish a clear "not a diagnosis" statement plus safety-net advice.
- Adapt: keep the deterministic engine, keep the label on every result screen, and put NAFDAC position into question 14.
- Unsuitable: machine-learning triage with no deterministic floor (barred by INV-01).

## Payments and stored value (Paystack, Flutterwave, wallets in fintech apps)
- Licensed payment providers handle card data and settlement; apps that hold balances typically need their own licence.
- Adapt: checkout per item through Paystack only; no balance table.
- Unsuitable: wallet top-ups or loyalty credit that persists, until counsel answers question 13.

## Advertising pre-vetting and insurance language
- Nigerian health and fintech brands commonly submit campaigns to ARCON for pre-vetting; insurance-like words (premium, cover) are avoided by non-insurers.
- Adapt: a register of public claims and a copy lint.
- Unsuitable: price comparisons with insurance products on public pages without counsel review.
