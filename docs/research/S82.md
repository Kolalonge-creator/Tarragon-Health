# S82 research: connectivity, devices, language and accessibility

Short notes from public product behaviour and published guidance (from general knowledge, no live lookup was run in this session, so treat as not re-verified). Adapt, do not copy. Product is English only (D-14).

## Offline queue and power-cut resilience
- WhatsApp and similar messengers write the message to local storage first, show a clock then a tick, and retry on their own. The patient never has to press send twice. Adopt the visible per-item state (waiting, sent), which `sync-banner.tsx` already has.
- Offline-first note apps keep a draft on every keystroke and restore it after a crash. Our `drafts.ts` is the same idea but covers four forms.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: silent background retry with no visible state. A patient with a dangerous reading must be told it has not reached the care team yet. Keep the danger flag and the one hour stuck notice.
- Unsuitable: queueing anything that changes treatment (INV-02), or booking and payment, which need live server state.

## Low-data mode
- Facebook Lite and YouTube Go ship a data saver: text first, images on tap, a visible data counter, downloads queued for Wi-Fi. Adopt text first and tap to load.
- Unsuitable: aggressive image recompression that blurs clinical photos (a lab report photo must stay readable), and auto-enabling on a slow connection without telling the patient. Keep it a choice.
- Data bundles are expensive and shared: never autoplay audio or video, and prefer Wi-Fi for downloads, with a plain "use mobile data" switch.

## Audio coverage, "What is this?" and tab tours
- Duolingo plays short spoken prompts on tap and teaches the interface in the first session with a few skippable cards. Adopt tap-to-listen and short tours.
- Wysa and similar care apps keep spoken content short and always show the words next to it. Our `ListenButton` follows this.
- Unsuitable: autoplay in a public place (discreet mode, D.3), text-to-speech of clinical results with a synthetic voice (INV-04 bars spoken explanation of sensitive positives), and reading assistant replies aloud without review.
- A "what is this" help control should always sit in the same place and never hide the screen it explains.

## Plain language
- Government plain-language guidance (for example the US plain language guidelines and the NHS content style guide) asks for short sentences, common words, one idea per line and a reading grade around 6 to 8.
- Adopt a reading-grade report as a review aid, not a hard blocker that rewrites clinical wording a doctor has signed.
- Unsuitable: simplifying clinical thresholds or drug names in a way that changes the meaning. Signed clinical wording stays as signed.

## Touch targets, adjustable text, screen readers
- Apple's guidance is 44 by 44 points, Google's material guidance is 48 dp. Our floor of 44 is fine. Small text links need padded hit areas.
- Both platforms scale text with the system setting up to about 200 percent. Cap below that only where a fixed bar truly breaks.
- TalkBack and VoiceOver need a role, a name and a state on every control, headings for navigation, and text summaries for charts. A static scan cannot replace testing on a phone with each reader.
- Unsuitable: relying on gestures only, or naming a control with an icon alone.

## Colour-blind-safe status
- Health apps that use red, amber and green pair every colour with a word and an icon or shape, and keep amber and red apart in lightness, not only hue.
- Adopt word plus icon (the new kit `Badge` already does). Avoid red and green side by side with the same weight.

## Voice input
- Many apps offer a microphone on text fields. Founder decision S47-1 dropped voice logging for now, so nothing is proposed. The phone keyboard's own dictation remains available to patients.
