# S32 research: audio for health apps (short)

Studied public help pages and published guidance only. Nothing copied; the clip list is Tarragon's own (Audio Production List v1.0).

**Offline emergency guidance.** Public first-aid and symptom apps (Red Cross first aid, NHS-style symptom pages) keep emergency steps inside the install so they work with no signal, and keep audio short and plain. Unsafe for Nigeria: any design that streams emergency audio, or shows a "loading" state before guidance. Here the emergency, triage and number clips ship inside the app (INV-06), and the text always shows first.

**Spoken readings (number stitching).** Talking glucometers and BP monitors speak a reading by joining stored words ("your reading is" + number), and accessibility guidance warns against partial or reordered speech. Unsafe: a missing word turns a reading into a different reading ("... over 94"). Here a phrase plays whole or not at all, and a number the kit cannot say exactly (601 to 999 steps, a fraction to the hundredth) gets text only, never a rounded value.

**Audio for low-literacy and low-data users.** Language-learning and health-literacy apps (Duolingo-style lessons, WHO mHealth guidance) download audio on Wi-Fi, never auto-play in public, and offer it as a tap. Unsafe for Nigeria: autoplay of a clinical sentence in a shared room or on a bus, and downloading hundreds of megabytes on mobile data. Here post-sign-up files download on Wi-Fi only and never in low-data mode, and a first-use tour is an offer, not autoplay.

**Pidgin and synthetic voices.** Public ElevenLabs documentation lists the languages it supports officially; Nigerian Pidgin is not one of them, and the Production List already says every Pidgin clip needs two native listeners. Unsafe: playing unreviewed clinical Pidgin. Here Pidgin clinical text is held as English and a Pidgin recording cannot be approved until it is released, so the voice never says words nobody checked.

**Results explained aloud.** Result-reading products explain categories in audio but leave sensitive diagnoses to a person. Unsafe: any recorded or generated explanation of a positive HIV, hepatitis B or hepatitis C result (INV-04). The list has no such clip, and a test fails if one appears.

## Competitor pass (added after the first review; public snippets only, no page fetches)

No public evidence of voice features was found for Healthily, Babylon, mPharma, Helium Health, Wellahealth, 54gene or the WHO and Medic programmes: that means "not found", not "none".

- **Ada** (EU Class IIa device): text advice, 7 languages, none Nigerian. **Wysa**: text first; its SOS needs connectivity. **Headspace, Calm**: per-session downloads the user starts, no Wi-Fi-only default. **Medisafe**: accessibility audits found unlabelled controls for VoiceOver and TalkBack.
- **Talking BP cuffs** read systolic, diastolic and pulse in a recorded voice, in English only; a university paper found they do not voice everything shown on screen. Lesson used here: a reading is never spoken without its unit (where the kit has one) and its triage sentence.
- **Nigerian speech technology** is research-stage: NaijaVoices (recognition, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa), N-ATLaS (licence-gated above 1,000 users), a published Pidgin recognition study at 29.6 percent word error rate. Not safe for clinical input; no text-to-speech met a clinical bar. Pre-recorded, reviewed clips stay the right call; voice input in Pidgin is out of scope until it meets an accuracy bar.

Changes made in S32 from this pass: whole-phrase clinical sign-off per stitched pattern; a clinical reading is refused audio without its triage sentence; sign-offs are bound to one recording, replaced recordings are kept in history, and a rollback is possible. Left for later: screen-reader treatment of Pidgin text, silent mode, headphones, calls and lock-screen playback (all need the native module), and counsel's view on whether spoken triage is regulated software (OQ-207).
