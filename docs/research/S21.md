## Zoom Nigeria dial-in (confirmed 2026-10-06)

- Zoom's own rates page lists Nigeria for toll dial-in (about GBP 0.05 a minute per participant, paid through the caller's own plan for a toll number) and for call-out (about GBP 1.08 mobile and 1.68 landline a minute). Toll-free is not listed.
- Dial-in needs the Audio Conferencing add-on on the host account. The Video SDK says dial-in works only with Zoom-provided numbers, not our own carrier. Which Nigerian number a given session would show is UNVERIFIED until the account is configured.
- Call-out at that rate is roughly NGN 2,000 to 3,500 a minute, far too much against a NGN 10,000 consultation. The phone fallback therefore does not use Zoom call-out. It uses a Tarragon-owned number bridge (own carrier, Twilio Voice or LiveKit SIP) so neither side sees the other's number.

# Part 1. Booking and discovery

Caveat: the search tool returned summaries; direct fetches of Vezeeta/Okadoc terms failed (403/redirect). Items marked [unverified] were not confirmed on a primary page. Nigerian peers publish very little booking-policy detail.

## A. Discovery, filters, profiles, verification
- Practo (India): search by specialty/location with "cleaner doctor cards and smarter filters"; claims every doctor's degrees are verified against medical-council databases and a specialty-vs-degree rulebook ("Bluebook"). It dropped doctor badges in favour of recommendations/patient stories from verified patients. https://blog.practo.com/no-more-doctor-badges/ , https://help.practo.com/practo-profile/guidelines-for-creating-profiles/
- Doctolib (FR/DE): profiles list consultation "motives" (visit types) per practitioner/practice, with flags for video, new-patient-only and age limits; only agendas opened online are visible, some specialists open in waves. https://medium.com/doctolib/10-software-engineering-lessons-from-transforming-our-appointment-search-system-fc56bec1c226
- HeliumDoc (NG): search by specialty+location, doctor card with "Available at a certain time" button meaning instant confirmation with no confirmation call. https://heliumhealth.com/helium-doc/
- MyMedicalBank (NG): "verified professionals", video/audio/chat consults from NGN 2,000. https://www.mymedicalbank.com/meet-a-doctor
- Vezeeta/Okadoc/Clafiya/Reliance: specialty/location/price filters typical; no primary-source detail on filters retrieved [unverified].
- Nigerian regulatory context: MDCN treats telemedicine as practice of medicine; every platform physician needs current MDCN registration; MDCN publishes a sanctioned-doctors list and the "Dodgy Doctors" register exists. https://manifieldsolicitors.com/telemedicine-and-the-law-compliance-priorities-for-nigerian-health-tech/ , https://mdcn.gov.ng/page/discipline/sanctioned-doctors-list , https://www.voanews.com/a/new-tool-database-nigeria-bust-fake-doctors/3168646.html

## B. Slots, timezone, confirmation, reminders, no-show
- Doctolib: live calendar, instant booking; SMS/email reminders (SMS at 24h or 48h for patients without the app); claims no-show drop from ~10% to ~2.5% (average ~2%). https://sinch.com/customer-stories/how-doctolib-simplifies-healthcare-millions-sms/ , https://www.smsmode.com/en/send-confirmation-appointment-by-sms-doctolib/
- Doctolib waitlist/"earlier slot" notifications let patients move up when a slot frees. https://www.icthealth.org/news/how-this-platform-mastered-the-art-of-appointment-management
- Practo Ray (clinic side): confirmation, cancellation, reminder and follow-up SMS; vendor claims >70% no-show reduction with reminders hours before; doctor can mark "patient no-show" up to 3 days after, which triggers email+SMS asking the patient why. https://help.practo.com/practo-ray-v7/calendar-practo-ray-v7/how-to-mark-patient-no-show/
- Okadoc: Manage Appointment lets patient reschedule, cancel, or get SMS/email when an earlier slot opens. https://www.okadoc.com/en-eg/policy/terms
- Timezone: none of the sources documents timezone display; single-country platforms assume local time [unverified].

## C. Cancellation, rescheduling, refund, late/no-show wording and windows
- Practo Consult: cancel 2h or more before = full refund; within 2h = fixed INR 50 retained, rest refunded; doctor cancels if exam needed = refund; 3 days to flag a consult as inadequate and request refund; paid chat window stays active 24h. https://www.practo.com/consult/terms , https://help.practo.com/practo-consult/faqs-for-practo-consult-patients/
- Vezeeta: full refund if patient cancels before the appointment time or if provider cancels/no-shows; no refund for dissatisfaction; refund via same gateway "within 15 working days"; provider may decline telehealth and suggest in-person. https://www.vezeeta.com.ng/en/Generic/TermsAndConditions (via search summary)
- Okadoc: may suspend instant booking/online consult if user cancels under 2h notice or no-shows 3+ appointments; for online consults, fees and no-show terms are set by the provider/facility. https://www.okadoc.com/en-eg/policy/terms
- Doctolib DE/FR: patient self-service cancel/move within the practice's timeframe; reported move cutoffs 1h (GP, paediatrics, dermatology, ENT), 2h (ophthalmology, gynaecology, dentists, others), 4h (other specialists) [secondary source]; no platform-wide fee. Germany practices sometimes charge EUR 40-100 for unexcused absence, which consumer bodies say is only allowed exceptionally; France floated a EUR 5 deposit/rabbit tax. https://www.thelocal.de/20230804/can-doctors-in-germany-charge-a-fee-for-cancelled-or-no-show-appointments , https://www.connexionfrance.com/news/5-penalty-proposed-for-missing-a-medical-rendez-vous-in-france/652108
- Mobihealth (NG): reschedule/cancel in app subject to policy; if doctor becomes unavailable patient is notified and can rebook or request refund; annual subscription min 12 months except PAYG. https://app.mobihealthinternational.com/Patient/TermAndConditionMobile

## D. Pricing display, payment-before-confirmation, packs/credits
- Practo/Vezeeta/Okadoc: fee shown on doctor card; online pay captures at booking, refunds to original payment method; Okadoc has "How to refund an online payment" article [content not retrieved].
- Clafiya (NG): virtual-only consult about NGN 2,500, in-person nurse/CHW visit with remote doctor about NGN 5,000 (2023 reporting); USSD *347*58# for no-internet access. https://techpoint.africa/2023/05/11/9-affordable-telemedicine-startups-nigeria/ , https://www.facesofdigitalhealth.com/blog/primary-care-nigeria-clafiya
- Mobihealth: annual plans NGN 2,500-5,000 (individual/couple/family) per 2023 reporting; Reliance/Kangpe: HMO-bundled access rather than per-consult pricing. (same Techpoint article)
- Paystack facts relevant to payment-before-confirmation: failed-but-debited usually auto-reversed within 24h or next working day; Pay-with-transfer has a 30-minute window and auto-refunds late transfers. https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2127938 , https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2128642

## E. Clinician-side calendars
- Practo Ray: drag-and-drop weekly calendar, colour-coded slots, up to two sessions per day, block-calendar for unavailability, option to open a doctor outside normal timings, SMS settings. https://help.practo.com/category/practo-ray-v7/calendar-practo-ray-v7/ , https://help.practo.com/practo-ray/calendar/adding-a-doctors-unavailability-to-the-calendar/
- Doctolib: opening hours per agenda, visit types (motives) bound to agendas, ability to open agenda in waves or reserve motives for existing patients; waitlist fills cancellations.
- HeliumDoc: doctor toggles "available at a certain time" slots; instant-confirm vs request-confirm [partial].
- No public detail for Okadoc/Clafiya/Reliance clinician tooling.

## F. What failed / unsafe in Nigeria
- Fake doctors: MDCN calls the problem massive; verification must be against MDCN register, annual licence currency, and sanctioned list (links in A).
- Payments: bank debit without credit, delays, transfer windows; refunds need clear expected timelines; bank-side reversal can take 24h+ (Paystack links in D).
- Connectivity/cost: broadband ~48% (2023), power outages; Clafiya's USSD route and low-bandwidth modes exist for this reason. https://leadership.ng/is-nigeria-getting-left-behind-in-health-technology/
- Platform failure risk: Babyl Rwanda (2.8M users) shut down abruptly when its parent failed, showing dependence risk; Hello Doctor (ZA) and ConnectMed (KE) also closed. https://www.ictworks.org/digital-success-cannot-beat-corporate-failure/
- Clinician no-shows and double-booking in Nigerian apps: no primary-source data found; treat as assumed risk and design for it (provider no-show = automatic full refund, per Vezeeta pattern).

## Patterns to adopt / avoid for Tarragon (max 15)
1. Adopt: show the full cancel/reschedule/refund rule on the slot and payment screens BEFORE the pay button, in plain language with exact hours.
2. Adopt: patient cancel 2h+ before = full refund (Practo/Okadoc/Vezeeta converge); consider a longer window (e.g. 6-12h) for chronic-care continuity, set as config.
3. Adopt: clinician cancel or no-show = automatic full refund or free rebook, no request needed.
4. Avoid: percentage/fixed late-cancel penalties at launch; if any, use a small fixed retention (Practo's INR 50 model) as versioned config.
5. Adopt: a "something went wrong" refund/flag window after the consult (Practo 3 days), reviewed by a human.
6. Adopt: reserve slot on payment initiation with a short hold (about 10-15 min); confirm only on Paystack webhook; show "debited but not confirmed" recovery state with auto-reversal timeline (24h).
7. Adopt: reminders at 24h and 1-2h via in-app/push/email (SMS only if allowed), with one-tap confirm/reschedule; never make a send a booking dependency.
8. Adopt: "notify me if earlier slot opens" waitlist (Doctolib/Okadoc).
9. Adopt: patient-reason prompt after clinician-marked no-show (Practo), plus repeat-no-show soft limit (Okadoc 3 strikes) with human review.
10. Adopt: per-visit-type (motive) rules like Doctolib: new vs follow-up, video-only, duration, price.
11. Adopt: MDCN badge only when register check is current: show licence number, last verified date, and year; hide the profile when lapsed. Link to MDCN register.
12. Adopt: clinician calendar with weekly rules, blocked time, two-session days, and a conflict check; slots only from explicit "bookable" blocks, database-level uniqueness to prevent double-booking.
13. Adopt: filters by specialty, language, sex, price, next-available; show times in Africa/Lagos with explicit label.
14. Avoid: relying on one provider's undisclosed terms (Okadoc defers to the provider); one clear Tarragon policy for all clinicians.
15. Avoid: annual lock-in plans like Mobihealth for per-consult booking; and keep a low-data/offline-tolerant booking path (small payloads, retry-safe payment status).

# Part 2. Video, audio fallback, phone, async

Confidence note: web search was shallow for named telehealth competitors (Kry, Doxy.me, Teladoc internals, Mobihealth, MyDoc, K Health, Ping An are mostly NOT publicly documented at the engineering level). Items marked [UNVERIFIED] are industry-practice inference, not sourced. Verify vendor prices and Nigeria support directly before contracting.

## 1. Operator behaviours (what is actually documented)
- Degradation / audio-only / reconnect: generic telehealth guidance says platforms should downshift video and drop to audio-only without clinician action, with a phone-call fallback and a chart note of the modality change (telehealth.org, mindbowser). No named vendor's exact algorithm found. Vendor SDKs (Zoom, Daily, LiveKit) expose the primitives (network quality events, simulcast) so the ladder is something Tarragon must own.
- Dial-in / phone fallback: Zoom Video SDK supports PSTN dial-in (Zoom-provided numbers, needs Audio Conferencing plan and pre-created session) and dial-out; BYOC not supported for dial-in. Daily has dial-in in 27 countries, dial-out international by request. LiveKit does SIP outbound to a country code via your own trunk.
- Waiting room / wait time: Livi (Kry UK) targets 20-30 min to a GP for drop-in, rarely over an hour; Teladoc shows an estimated wait at request; MDLIVE medical visits typically within an hour. Peak-hour wait and video reliability are the top complaint themes for MDLIVE/Teladoc/Amwell (unstar.app aggregate, low-grade source).
- No-shows: Livi/Kry charge a no-show fee if cancelled under 24h or not attended. Clinician no-show handling is not publicly documented [UNVERIFIED]; pattern to design: patient-facing auto-rebook plus credit/refund, and clinician-side alert at T+5 min.
- Recording: MDLIVE records medical (not behavioural) visits by default under its terms, consent via app terms, for treatment/QA/peer review. Wiretap-style consent rules apply in the US; for Tarragon the NDPA consent + lawful basis analysis is the equivalent (needs counsel).
- Babyl Rwanda (most relevant LMIC precedent): USSD enrolment, call-centre/nurse triage callback, phone consult with GP, e-prescription via SMS, 5,000+ consults/day. Phone-first, no video dependency.
- Clafiya (Nigeria): N2,500 virtual consult; *347*58# USSD works with no internet; pairs virtual with community health workers.
- Hello Doctor is a South African service (Momentum/medical schemes), not Nigerian; limited detail.
- Babylon lessons: failed (bankrupt Sept 2023) on unit economics (ease of use drove over-use under a flat fee), poor continuity of care, tech-startup pace vs clinical context. Lesson: cap per-patient consult volume economics and keep continuity/attribution.
- Ping An Good Doctor: AI first (text/voice/video) then human doctor supervises; "One-Minute Clinics" cabins. Pattern: AI intake before the doctor sees the case.
- Accessibility, pre-call device check, auto-end timing, call-drop recovery: no operator-specific public sources found; treat as design requirements [UNVERIFIED].

## 2. Async consults
- Store-and-forward evidence: median response 21.4 h (German university hospital teledermatology); 1h13m same-day average (US health system); ~3.5h average / 82 min median (paediatric derm service); 2.4 days in a Spanish health area (Springer, Wiley, Medscape). Takeaway: hours is achievable with staffed queues, days with no queue discipline.
- Kry/K Health async flows, photo guidance, pre-submission red-flag triage, and async pricing: not found in public sources; I could not verify. Design from first principles: structured symptom form, red-flag screen blocks submission and routes to emergency/live path, guided photo capture (lighting, scale object, close + far), SLA stated up front.

## 4. Nigeria network realities (2026)
- Subscriber share by generation, May 2026: 2G 36.1% (from 58.4% May 2023), 3G 5.1%, 4G 53.8%, 5G growing (StatiSense, allAfrica, thewillnews). A third of subscribers are on 2G: voice-capable, effectively no video.
- Speeds: Ookla median mobile download 26.2 Mbps YTD 2026 (up from 18.9 in 2022); another cite 44 Mbps Q4 2025 (rank 85th). Medians hide rural/congested cells and upload speed (the thing video needs).
- Data cost: ~N100-210/GB on bulk plans (Glo ~N160/GB, MTN ~N210/GB VTU); ~$0.39/GB average. Video at 1 Mbps = ~450 MB/hr: a 15 min video visit is ~110 MB (~N20-25) - material for many users, hence low-data mode.
- Outages: Q1 2026 NCC: 577 major network outages, 361 fibre cuts, 144 base-station power failures; >5,000 fibre cuts H1 2026; grid collapsed twice in January 2026.
- WhatsApp installed on 95% of Nigerian smartphones (Orange/KPMG study); incumbent for calls. Expect users to try "can we just WhatsApp?" - the audio fallback must be as frictionless.

## Patterns to adopt / avoid for Tarragon
1. Define VideoProvider as a thin interface (createRoom, joinToken, endRoom, webhooks->normalised events, dialOut, recording policy); keep ladder logic (video->audio->phone) in Tarragon code, not the vendor.
2. Make audio-only the default floor, not an error state: always join with audio first, offer video as an upgrade when measured uplink is sufficient.
3. Pre-call device/network check (mic, camera, 10s uplink probe, TURN reachability); result picks starting mode and is stored for support.
4. Auto-downgrade on sustained bad quality (e.g. packet loss/RTT thresholds for N seconds), auto-offer phone after audio fails, with hysteresis so it does not flap.
5. Phone fallback = server-side call bridge from a Tarragon-owned number to both parties (patient receives call, clinician receives call on their own number via the platform); never show either personal number. Do NOT build on Twilio Proxy (closed to new customers); use Voice/SIP (Twilio Voice, LiveKit SIP, or a Nigerian carrier trunk).
6. Prefer an engine you can place near users: LiveKit (self-host or Cloud, region pinning) vs Zoom for brand familiarity; run a measured Lagos-to-region latency test before choosing second vendor.
7. Low-bandwidth mode: audio + 1 low-res still-or-video-on-demand, explicit "uses about X MB" label, and data-saver toggle persisted per user.
8. Reconnect: rejoin with same session token for a grace window (e.g. 3-5 min); if not recovered, clinician-initiated or auto phone callback; keep the consult clock paused during outage.
9. No-show rules: clinician no-show at T+5 triggers auto reassignment/rebook + patient credit; patient no-show at T+10 triggers one callback attempt then close per policy. Surface consult state as data (server-driven), not client timers.
10. Waiting room shows honest queue position/ETA (Livi/Teladoc pattern) and works without video running.
11. Async consults: structured form + guided photo capture + red-flag screen before submit (hard-stop to emergency/live path), published SLA, queue-level timers, escalation of aged items; aim for hours, not days.
12. Recording off by default; if ever on, explicit consent captured and logged, retention defined, storage in NDPA-compliant region; check cross-border transfer for any non-Nigerian vendor.
13. Offer a USSD/phone-first entry for 2G users (Babyl, Clafiya) so the platform serves the 36% on 2G, consistent with CLAUDE.md rule that no feature depends on a single channel.
14. Avoid per-patient consult economics like Babylon (cost-per-visit caps, triage before doctor, Ping An-style AI intake only via governed runGovernedAi).
15. Cost-model in kobo with bandwidth egress included; flat per-minute vendors ($0.0035-0.004) are ~8x LiveKit's headline rate but LiveKit adds bandwidth and ops cost.
16. Provide a mock provider and failure-injection (drop, loss, 2G profile) for CI; power-cut test = abrupt disconnect without leave event.

# Part 3. Scribe consent, notes, Nigerian rules
Caveat: web search only; vendor pages are mostly third-party reviews. Legal items come from law-firm and blog summaries (some low quality), not primary texts. Verify with counsel.

## 1. Scribe vendors
- Consent: Abridge faces suit (Nov 2025, Apr 2026 class action) alleging recording without meaningful consent and notes auto-inserting "patient consented" text. Lesson: never auto-write a consent statement; write it only from a recorded patient answer. https://www.medicaldaily.com/ai-medical-scribe-recording-patient-consent-2026-privacy-rights-475588 ; https://www.glacis.io/guide-ambient-ai-scribe
- Best-practice pattern: consent built into the recording flow, scribe refuses to start with no consent recorded, audit trail of who/when. https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/the-best-7-ambient-ai-scribes . Most vendors rely on clinician verbal consent plus a checkbox, not patient in-app consent. CON-001 (patient answers in app) is stricter than the market.
- Decline/withdraw: patients can decline or pause. Reported gap: after withdrawal, already-captured content often cannot be deleted (Sharp HealthCare: audio on vendor servers ~30 days). https://www.palm-er.com/blog/what-does-your-ai-scribe-save
- Retention: Nabla never stores audio (processed in chunks); transcripts/notes default 14 days, configurable. Abridge publishes no number; health systems report 7-30 days. Audio deletion is not transcript deletion; deleting audio removes the way to verify a note. https://www.commure.com/blog-scribe/nabla-ai-review ; https://healthhub.cpcmg.net/docs/ambient-documentation-charting-abridge-privacy-security
- Edit-and-sign: all review-then-sign; Suki holds output in a review and EHR-handoff state until approved. Hallucination risk is highest in exam findings, med lists, assessment/plan; review should highlight those. https://developer.suki.ai/documentation/concepts/ambient-clinical-notes/ambient-documentation
- Languages: Nabla 35+; Heidi claims 110+ with mid-session detection. None found documenting Nigerian Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo support. Report honestly: unverified/likely weak. Heidi pricing: free tier, Clinician plan $150/month (2026). https://www.veroscribe.com/blog/heidi-health-review-2026
- ASR on Nigerian-accented English (AfriSpeech-MultiBench): Nigerian WER Azure ~23.0%, AWS ~22.6%, Gemini 2.5 Flash ~23.1%, Google Chirp V3 ~24.0%, GPT-4o Transcribe ~60%. Regional specialist Intron-Sahara V2 about 15% medical. Clinical speech is harder than general (33% vs 15% avg in AfriSpeech-200). Pidgin/code-switching not covered. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14255 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.00274

## Patterns to adopt / avoid
1. Adopt: consent gate is a hard server-side precondition; no audio/stt session can open without a CON-001 answer row.
2. Adopt: record consent as an event (patient, encounter, answer, time, script version); never infer it.
3. Adopt: decline path is first-class, same UI weight as accept, no friction, no penalty.
4. Adopt: mid-call withdrawal = stop capture at once, mark span, delete already-captured audio and transcript for that encounter (decide scope with counsel), keep an audit event.
5. Adopt: do not store audio at all if possible (Nabla model); short, configurable transcript retention; deletion of transcript distinct and logged.
6. Adopt: AI draft lives in a separate draft table, invisible to Passport/patient, until clinician signs; signing writes an immutable version.
7. Adopt: review UX that forces attention on exam, medications, assessment/plan; show source-transcript snippet per statement; diff of edits.
8. Adopt: label AI-drafted content and clinician-edited percentage in audit, not on patient-facing text.
9. Adopt: intake summary as structured, flagged "patient-reported/AI-summarised", with red-flag items first, linked to source answers.
10. Adopt: patient-facing summary only after sign; plain language; emergency disclaimer.
11. Adopt: e-Rx carries MDCN number, digital signature, secure transmission; referral letters name facility.
12. Avoid: auto-inserting any consent sentence into notes.
13. Avoid: relying on ASR for Nigerian Pidgin or Hausa/Yoruba/Igbo without own benchmark; test on Nigerian accented clinical audio before launch; expect ~20%+ WER, so human review is mandatory.
14. Avoid: assuming GPT-4o Transcribe is usable for Nigerian speech (60% WER in one benchmark); benchmark Azure/AWS/Google/Intron.
15. Avoid: treating UK/EU region as automatically compliant; paper cross-border basis for each processor.
