# S15 research: clinician credentialing (2026-10-06)

Method: public web sources at search-snippet level (vendor pages, regulator pages, press and journal articles); no full pages were opened for most, and the MDCN primary-source-verification page returned 403. Claims marked (secondary) or (inferred) need checking before they go into any policy text. Not legal advice. Reference platforms are studied for behaviour and design; nothing is copied.

## Founder confirmations that settle the Nigeria questions (2026-10-06)
- **MDCN evidence is the current-year practising licence plus a screenshot of the clinician own MDCN portal page**, together with the graduation licence or degree certificate and the NYSC certificate. There is no register to query, so this is the evidence set the licence check rests on.
- **The DPO is comfortable with storing government ID.** The retention period is a configured value (7 years after offboarding, PROPOSED), kept behind restricted access with every open logged.
- **Expiry grace is a short, audited grace period** (default cap 14 days, a recorded reason and the reviewer name), never a silent extension.
- **The clinician is told 3 months before, 1 month before, and on the day it expires**, and the day-of notice is also seen by reviewers.

## Research by function

### 1. Credentialing and verification
- **Wheel / OpenLoop (US):** staged pipeline (documents, primary-source verification, protocol training, a communication/"manner" review), signed attestations, liability-coverage check, 2-4 week stated turnaround, continuous licence monitoring. Adapt: the staging, attestations, stated turnaround. Not suitable: NPI, NPDB/OIG sanctions, Verifiable API (US only).
- **Doctolib (France):** identity verified by ID plus selfie liveness and a register number match. Adapt: ID + selfie pairing. Not suitable: depends on a national registry API; Nigeria has none.
- **Practo (India):** government ID, council registration number, degree copies, certificate; specialty claim mapped to qualification. Falls back to "submit the licence copy" where a council has no online directory, the closest match to Nigeria. Adapt: qualification-to-specialty mapping (feeds competencies), go-live target in days.
- **Hims & Hers:** operations collects and verifies; a separate clinical authority decides. Maps directly to ops verify, CMO approve. Their corporate-practice structure is not applicable.
- **Teladoc / NCQA / Joint Commission:** verification freshness window (NCQA cut to 120 days in 2025), alerts at 120/90/30 days, focused review of a new clinician's early cases (FPPE) then ongoing review at least annually. Adapt: freshness window, early-case review, annual re-verification. Not suitable: the 36-month cycle (Nigerian licences are annual), board certification, malpractice-history databases.
- **UK GMC:** a public register and a named Responsible Officer. Adapt: the named responsible clinician (the CMO). Not suitable: 5-year revalidation, a public register.
- **Clafiya:** says its doctors hold valid MDCN licences; its credentialing process is not public (not found).

### 2. Nigeria baseline (most important for design)
- MDCN gives a unique folio number and a separate annual practising licence, renewed on portal.mdcn.gov.ng (secondary: needs CPD, around 20 credit units a year). Practising without a current licence is an offence and the employer can be liable (secondary). Grace periods have been announced in some years, so never hard-code a cut-off; the expiry date printed on the licence is the source of truth.
- **No public register or API found.** The documented route is a written verification request to MDCN with name, date of birth, photo and folio number. A journal source says the register is not publicly searchable (2025 claim, recheck). So verification is a human step with evidence upload (MDCN reply email or letter, or a licence-portal screenshot, which is weaker evidence).
- Forged MDCN certificates and fake doctors are documented (arrests, MDCN inspectorate says cases are numerous). Folio-number reuse and referee fraud are NOT documented; they are inferred risks. Controls: unique folio constraint, a flag when a previously suspended or rejected folio reappears, referees reached through independently sourced institutional contacts, never only contact details the applicant supplies.
- Indemnity: MDCN ethics code expects it (secondary); the NMA offers subsidised group cover; many freelancers lack it. D-10: certificate required (insurer, policy number, limit, expiry).
- NDPA 2023 / NDPC GAID 2025: ID numbers are personal data; keep no longer than necessary; a face photo matched to ID may be biometric (inferred, confirm with counsel). No ID-specific retention rule found. Open for the DPO: lawful basis, retention, a DPIA for ID storage.
- Bandwidth, shared phones: not sourced; design inference.

### 3. Expiry cadence and suspension
- Alerts at 120/90/30 days are the norm abroad. Chosen here: licence and indemnity at 90, 60, 30 and 7 days (founder asked for 3 months and 1 month; 60 and 7 are optional extras, see decisions).
- No published comparison of auto-suspend vs notify-only (not found). Practical hybrid, and what the founder chose: suspend at expiry, keep records visible to the CMO, existing cases reassignable. Suspension must block new assignment, paging, prescribing and new claims, but never hide a patient record from the person reassigning the case.

### 4. Training and test
- **Manchester Triage:** train-the-trainer, a named trainee register, an audit standard of 5 decisions per practitioner twice a year, agreement between raters is only moderate to substantial (kappa 0.55-0.78) and varies with experience; audit improved agreement. Adapt: a fixed audit sample per clinician and twice-yearly retest. Not suitable: licensed paid content, face-to-face instructors.
- **Babylon (lesson):** a clinician who raised unsafe chest-pain triage was attacked rather than heard, and marketing claimed more than was validated. Adapt: a protected "raise a safety concern" route with no retaliation, a missed red flag is a reportable incident, claims never exceed what is validated. Matches the always-visible safety concern link in spec 9.1.
- **AI scribes (Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Ambience, Corti):** consent first, AI drafts, clinician edits and signs; 30-60 minute training, about 2 weeks to ramp; reviewers must compare against their own memory, not just proofread; NHS England requires a safety officer, hazard log and DPIA. Adapt: a short scribe module with a mandatory "consent then review then sign" gate. Nigeria risk (inferred): Pidgin and code-switching reduce transcription accuracy.
- **Omada / Glooko:** role-split teams, flag rules as data, "no readings" and "not syncing" flags. Adapt for S16/S17, noted here only.
- **Test design:** an overall pass mark plus a separate hard gate on safety-critical items (any red miss fails regardless of total, as in CNA critical-step and RBT no-partial-credit practice). Retakes capped (common pattern 3 attempts, one source 1); rotate scenarios so retakes are not memorised; log answer and rationale on a red miss; a second failure goes to CMO review. None of this is an official telehealth standard (not found); it is a design choice for clinical governance to approve.
- **Early-practice audit:** locum research shows more antibiotic and opioid prescribing by short-tenure clinicians (observational), so sample new clinicians more heavily on prescribing. A decreasing sample (all of the first cases, then a fraction, then steady state) is the suggestion; the percentages are ours, not a GMC figure. Add an induction checklist and a debrief step. Optional shadowing of an experienced clinician before the first live case (Wheel).

### 5. Review screens and records
- Credentialing software (Medallion, symplr, CredentialStream): per-clinician credentialing file, reusable checklist per tier, expirable-item tracking, routed alerts, a decision audit trail of every workflow change, an exception queue. Public UX docs not found; inferred from product descriptions.
- Maker-checker: no public source; standard control. Verifier cannot approve, CMO signs, both actors recorded. Existing precedent: verified_by cannot equal the clinician, CMO signs protocols.
- Referees: NHS guidance wants 3 years of history and a written-up phone reference confirmed back by the referee. Adapt the confirm-back step and independent institutional contact. Not suitable: DBS and right-to-work.
- Conflict-of-interest declaration: no source; design fresh (declare pharmacy, lab, HMO interests and relationships at onboarding and annually). Feeds `conflicts` in S17.
- Document storage (OWASP, AWS guidance): allow-listed types, magic-byte check, size limit, malware scan, short-lived scoped links, and for government ID serve through an authenticated route that checks role and writes an access-log row. Log access, no signed URLs in logs.
- ID retention suggestion: after verification keep the result, last 4 digits, verifier and date; delete the image after a defined period (counsel and DPO to set).
