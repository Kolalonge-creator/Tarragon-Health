# S35 research: clinician console behaviour

Short notes from public documentation and published guidance. Adapted, not copied. "Nigeria" lines name what does not carry over.

## Next task and queue (Wheel, OpenLoop, Hims & Hers, Clafiya)
- Clinician marketplaces (Wheel, OpenLoop) show a single pull action and a visible queue depth, and hide the patient until a case is accepted. We do the same: the queue screen shows counts by priority class only; the patient appears after the claim.
- A claim is time-boxed with a visible countdown, and a case that lapses returns to the pool. We show the countdown and one extension (S17 config).
- Hand-back with a reason is normal practice so work is not silently dropped. Reasons are a fixed list; "other" needs words.
- Nigeria: queue depth is small and clinicians work from phones with patchy data. The screen must be a plain server-rendered page that works on a slow link, with the button as a normal form post, not a client-only widget.

## Patient summary (Omada Health, Glooko, Doctolib)
- Glooko-style summaries put readings against a target band with the last two weeks as the default window, and show adherence beside medicines, not on a separate page. We follow that.
- Omada shows "what changed since last time" first. We lead with recent triage events and any pending proposal.
- Doctolib limits what each profession can see. Our equivalent is the access category gate per section, and the audit row on every open.
- Unsafe: showing a result before clinician review. We show result metadata only (panel, release state, dates), never values (INV-03, INV-04).
- Nigeria: care circle is shown as a count only; naming a relative to a clinician who does not need it is a privacy cost in close family settings.

## Consultation and scribe (Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Ambience, Corti)
- All of them record consent first, show a live indicator while capturing, and present a draft that the clinician edits and signs. Nothing enters the record before the signature (INV-11). The S23 panels already do this; S35 mounts them in the consultation view rather than rebuilding them.
- Nabla and Suki let the clinician type instead of speak. S23c already added paste-or-type input.
- Unsafe or unsuitable: Nigerian English and Pidgin lower speech-to-text accuracy, so the draft is labelled as a draft and the clinician must read it. No model is ever in the triage path (INV-01).

## Hand-back, escalation and the red page (Manchester Triage System, Babylon lesson)
- Manchester triage names a maximum wait per class and escalates when it passes. Our priority classes and due times come from versioned config (INV-16); the screen shows the class and due time, it does not decide them.
- Babylon lesson: a symptom checker that sounded more certain than it was. The task view shows the rule that fired and its version, never a model's opinion.
- A red event is paged, never left to be pulled (INV-05). The queue screen links to On call and does not list red work as pullable.

## Safety concern button (all of the above)
- Published safety-reporting guidance asks for a report path that is always one click away and never blocked by the state of the page. We add it to the clinician shell.

# Second pass: competitor evidence and design recommendations (2026-10-06)

Method and limits: web search summaries, not full product walkthroughs. Thin coverage for Livongo, Dario, Heartbeat, Carbon, Cerner and Ada (little public dashboard detail). Epic In Basket claim timers and Wheel/OpenLoop queue mechanics were not verified. Claims tagged (inf) are inference.

## What the evidence says
- **Queues.** Epic pools are browsable and one person acts per item, with explicit transfer; inbox overload is the known failure (UT Southwestern assessment: advancing.utsystem.edu, UTSW Epic in-basket report). Teladoc shows a shared queue with a live counter and one-tap connect, but is video-first, a poor fit for patchy data (apps.apple.com Teladoc provider app). Wheel and OpenLoop run protocol-driven async work (wheel.com sync vs async; openloophealth.com). Hims & Hers is the cautionary case: the FTC alleges prescriptions with no real provider conversation (medicinenet.com FTC story). A queue tuned for speed becomes rubber-stamping.
- **Priority vocabulary.** Manchester Triage uses five colours with a target time each (metricgate.com Manchester priorities). Borrow the shape (few levels, colour plus text, a countdown), not the numbers; ours come from versioned config.
- **Summaries and lead lists.** Glooko ranks patients by risk, keeps a sticky header and has explicit "not activated / no data" states (support.glooko.com Clinic Dashboard 25.2). Omada and Glooko show the clinician the same data the patient sees (omadahealth.com/hypertension). Population views conflict with our claim-before-visible rule.
- **Scribes.** Abridge links each note section to its transcript span (abridge.com). Suki gives accept/reject per item but also stages orders (suki.ai). Nabla records consent and declines, and does not keep audio, but learns from edits (nabla.com, commure.com review). Ambience reads the whole chart and adds coding (ambiencehealthcare.com). Corti extracts discrete facts first, then writes prose (docs.corti.ai). Doctolib gates on consent and always needs the doctor's validation, and drew backlash over data use for training (clubic.com). A 2024 study of 356 notes found omissions (18%) more common than hallucinations (11.5%) and harder to spot (thelimbic.com, beckershospitalreview.com).
- **Paging.** Acknowledge-gated escalation with several channels is the norm; alert fatigue is the failure mode (rootly.com; mitel.com clinical paging). Doximity Dialer hides the clinician's number and needs no patient install (press.doximity.com).
- **Lessons.** Babylon: unsafe triage advice and unproven claims (oecd.ai incident; digitalhealth.net Lancet review). Clafiya: design for no data (techpoint.africa).

## Applied in this PR (small, safe)
- Summary order is safety first: allergies and conditions, then recent triage events, then readings, medicines, plan, notes (Glooko ordering, rec 9).
- Lead list ranks by what needs a doctor soonest: tasks due, proposals waiting, lowest adherence (rec 11). Unit-tested.
- Already true in the build: pull-next only with no browsable list (rec 1), fixed priority labels with a countdown (rec 2, 3), hand-back with a reason (rec 4), explicit empty and error states (rec 10), always-visible safety concern (rec 12).

## Follow-ups, in order (each is its own piece of work)
1. **Scribe review that surfaces omissions** (rec 5, M): a "facts to confirm" list before prose, empty sections flagged as possible omissions, a per-section tick before signing. Highest value given the omission data.
2. **Consent as a hard three-state gate with a live indicator** (rec 7, S). Overlaps OQ-211 (two unrelated consents).
3. **Offline-tolerant console** (rec 8, L): cache the held task's summary, queue actions, "last synced", an unsent state; a queued signature must read "pending upload", never final.
4. **Evidence link from AI text to transcript span** (rec 6, L): needs a retention decision first (below).
5. **Paging ladder with a voice rung and a suppressed-duplicates count** (rec 13, 15, M): D-12 allows push, in-console alarm and email only, so a voice rung is a decision. Never suppress an emergency.
6. **Number masking and a voice fallback for calls** (rec 14, M): vendor data review.

## Deliberately not doing
1. No AI-staged orders, prescriptions or titration changes (Suki). INV-02.
2. No scribe that learns from clinician edits on its own (Nabla). It changes record-generating behaviour outside AI governance.
3. No training on consultations by default or by bundled consent (Doctolib backlash).
4. No throughput targets or auto-completion of work the clinician did not review (Hims & Hers).
5. No browsable all-patients view and no coding or billing prompts (Glooko population view, Ambience).

## Questions for the founder or CMO
- Does being a lead count as a claim for visibility? The build assumes yes (spec INV-12 names an active lead assignment as a tie); the lead list therefore shows first names and numbers.
- Scribe audio and transcript retention: discard after the visit (Nabla model) or keep for evidence links (Abridge model)?
- Speech-to-text for Nigerian English, Pidgin and local languages is unverified. Evaluate before the scribe is switched on.
