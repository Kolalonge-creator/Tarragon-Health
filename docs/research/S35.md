# S35 research: clinician console behaviour

Short notes from public documentation and published guidance. Adapted, not copied. "Nigeria" lines name what does not carry over.

## Next task and queue (Wheel, OpenLoop, Hims & Hers, Clafiya)
- Clinician marketplaces (Wheel, OpenLoop) show a single pull action and a visible queue depth, and hide the patient until a case is accepted. We do the same: the queue screen shows counts by priority class only; the patient appears after the claim.
- A claim is time-boxed with a visible countdown, and a case that lapses returns to the pool. We show the countdown and one extension (S17 config).
- Hand-back with a reason is normal practice so work is not silently dropped. Reasons are a fixed list; "other" needs words.
- Nigeria: queue depth is small and clinicians work from phones with patchy data. The screen must be a plain server-rendered page that works on a slow link, with the button as a normal form post, not a client-only widget.

## Patient summary (Omada Health, Glooko, Doctolib)
- Glooko-style summaries put readings against a target band with the last two weeks as the default window, and show adherence beside medicines, not on a separate page. We follow that.
- Omada shows "what changed since last time" first. We lead with recent triage events and any pending proposal.
- Doctolib limits what each profession can see. Our equivalent is the access category gate per section, and the audit row on every open.
- Unsafe: showing a result before clinician review. We show result metadata only (panel, release state, dates), never values (INV-03, INV-04).
- Nigeria: care circle is shown as a count only; naming a relative to a clinician who does not need it is a privacy cost in close family settings.

## Consultation and scribe (Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Ambience, Corti)
- All of them record consent first, show a live indicator while capturing, and present a draft that the clinician edits and signs. Nothing enters the record before the signature (INV-11). The S23 panels already do this; S35 mounts them in the consultation view rather than rebuilding them.
- Nabla and Suki let the clinician type instead of speak. S23c already added paste-or-type input.
- Unsafe or unsuitable: Nigerian English and Pidgin lower speech-to-text accuracy, so the draft is labelled as a draft and the clinician must read it. No model is ever in the triage path (INV-01).

## Hand-back, escalation and the red page (Manchester Triage System, Babylon lesson)
- Manchester triage names a maximum wait per class and escalates when it passes. Our priority classes and due times come from versioned config (INV-16); the screen shows the class and due time, it does not decide them.
- Babylon lesson: a symptom checker that sounded more certain than it was. The task view shows the rule that fired and its version, never a model's opinion.
- A red event is paged, never left to be pulled (INV-05). The queue screen links to On call and does not list red work as pullable.

## Safety concern button (all of the above)
- Published safety-reporting guidance asks for a report path that is always one click away and never blocked by the state of the page. We add it to the clinician shell.

# Second pass: competitor evidence and design recommendations (2026-10-06)

Method and limits: web search summaries, not full product walkthroughs. Thin coverage for Livongo, Dario, Heartbeat, Carbon, Cerner and Ada (little public dashboard detail). Epic In Basket claim timers and Wheel/OpenLoop queue mechanics were not verified. Claims tagged (inf) are inference.

## What the evidence says
- **Queues.** Epic pools are browsable and one person acts per item, with explicit transfer; inbox overload is the known failure (UT Southwestern assessment: advancing.utsystem.edu, UTSW Epic in-basket report). Teladoc shows a shared queue with a live counter and one-tap connect, but is video-first, a poor fit for patchy data (apps.apple.com Teladoc provider app). Wheel and OpenLoop run protocol-driven async work (wheel.com sync vs async; openloophealth.com). Hims & Hers is the cautionary case: the FTC alleges prescriptions with no real provider conversation (medicinenet.com FTC story). A queue tuned for speed becomes rubber-stamping.
- **Priority vocabulary.** Manchester Triage uses five colours with a target time each (metricgate.com Manchester priorities). Borrow the shape (few levels, colour plus text, a countdown), not the numbers; ours come from versioned config.
- **Summaries and lead lists.** Glooko ranks patients by risk, keeps a sticky header and has explicit "not activated / no data" states (support.glooko.com Clinic Dashboard 25.2). Omada and Glooko show the clinician the same data the patient sees (omadahealth.com/hypertension). Population views conflict with our claim-before-visible rule.
- **Scribes.** Abridge links each note section to its transcript span (abridge.com). Suki gives accept/reject per item but also stages orders (suki.ai). Nabla records consent and declines, and does not keep audio, but learns from edits (nabla.com, commure.com review). Ambience reads the whole chart and adds coding (ambiencehealthcare.com). Corti extracts discrete facts first, then writes prose (docs.corti.ai). Doctolib gates on consent and always needs the doctor's validation, and drew backlash over data use for training (clubic.com). A 2024 study of 356 notes found omissions (18%) more common than hallucinations (11.5%) and harder to spot (thelimbic.com, beckershospitalreview.com).
- **Paging.** Acknowledge-gated escalation with several channels is the norm; alert fatigue is the failure mode (rootly.com; mitel.com clinical paging). Doximity Dialer hides the clinician's number and needs no patient install (press.doximity.com).
- **Lessons.** Babylon: unsafe triage advice and unproven claims (oecd.ai incident; digitalhealth.net Lancet review). Clafiya: design for no data (techpoint.africa).

## Applied in this PR (small, safe)
- Summary order is safety first: allergies and conditions, then recent triage events, then readings, medicines, plan, notes (Glooko ordering, rec 9).
- Lead list ranks by what needs a doctor soonest: tasks due, proposals waiting, lowest adherence (rec 11). Unit-tested.
- Already true in the build: pull-next only with no browsable list (rec 1), fixed priority labels with a countdown (rec 2, 3), hand-back with a reason (rec 4), explicit empty and error states (rec 10), always-visible safety concern (rec 12).

## Follow-ups, in order (each is its own piece of work)
1. **Scribe review that surfaces omissions** (rec 5, M): a "facts to confirm" list before prose, empty sections flagged as possible omissions, a per-section tick before signing. Highest value given the omission data.
2. **Consent as a hard three-state gate with a live indicator** (rec 7, S). Overlaps OQ-211 (two unrelated consents).
3. **Offline-tolerant console** (rec 8, L): cache the held task's summary, queue actions, "last synced", an unsent state; a queued signature must read "pending upload", never final.
4. **Evidence link from AI text to transcript span** (rec 6, L): needs a retention decision first (below).
5. **Paging ladder with a voice rung and a suppressed-duplicates count** (rec 13, 15, M): D-12 allows push, in-console alarm and email only, so a voice rung is a decision. Never suppress an emergency.
6. **Number masking and a voice fallback for calls** (rec 14, M): vendor data review.

## Deliberately not doing
1. No AI-staged orders, prescriptions or titration changes (Suki). INV-02.
2. No scribe that learns from clinician edits on its own (Nabla). It changes record-generating behaviour outside AI governance.
3. No training on consultations by default or by bundled consent (Doctolib backlash).
4. No throughput targets or auto-completion of work the clinician did not review (Hims & Hers).
5. No browsable all-patients view and no coding or billing prompts (Glooko population view, Ambience).

## Questions for the founder or CMO
- Does being a lead count as a claim for visibility? The build assumes yes (spec INV-12 names an active lead assignment as a tie); the lead list therefore shows first names and numbers.
- Scribe audio and transcript retention: discard after the visit (Nabla model) or keep for evidence links (Abridge model)?
- Speech-to-text for Nigerian English, Pidgin and local languages is unverified. Evaluate before the scribe is switched on.

# Third pass: deeper public-source reading (2026-10-06)

No hands-on walkthroughs: that needs accounts or logins to clinician systems, which were not created. Full pages and PDFs were read instead. Most clinician screens are still not public. Everything marked (inf) is inference.

## What was confirmed
- **Epic In Basket pools** (EpicCare Link tip sheet, vitruvianhealth.com; TriHealth overview PDF, bridge.trihealth.com; Hawaii Health Partners PDF, hawaiihealthpartners.org). A pool message goes to everyone signed into the pool. **Take** means "take responsibility until it is complete"; **Put Back** releases it. A baton icon shows who holds it, and anyone can take it over (a soft, visible lock, no consent needed). **Done** removes it for everyone. Pool users filter with "Not Taken By Others". A quick action sets high priority and "due in 3 days"; the message turns red on day 3. **No timer that auto-releases a taken baton and no auto-escalation were found**, and that is unconfirmed, not disproven. A four-doctor practice that rotated one covering physician, limited to three message categories, cut burnout (annfammed.org/content/22/3/260).
- **Oracle Health / Cerner Message Center** (Northern Light and Munson Healthcare PDFs). A pool is a shared inbox where one person acting completes the item. "Assign Item" is self-assign only and advisory; the contents stay visible to every member. No timeout or escalation documented. The Task List and Clinical Leader Organizer screens could not be found publicly.
- **Wheel** (wheel.com/clinicians). Matches clinicians by licence, experience and availability; credentialing includes a "webside manner" review; supplies protocols. Developer docs are gated (401), so routing and claim mechanics are unknown.
- **OpenLoop** (closed job posting, jobs.techstars.com). Per-visit pay: sync roughly 3x async, plus a no-show fee.
- **Hims & Hers** (FY2025 10-K, sec.gov). Physician-owned affiliated groups keep sole control of clinical decisions; quality controls are guidelines, an advisory board, credentialing and QA. Provider screens and case assignment are not public. A hiring aggregator listing (mozibox.com) shows hourly pay for async work.
- **Teladoc provider app** (apps.apple.com, Teladoc help centre). Shows queue counts and waiting-room state; a no-show patient gets a short wait, then the visit is re-queued with a fresh notification. Timeout not published.
- **Livongo** (Wayne County and City of Boston PDFs). A non-doctor response tier answers out-of-range readings, with a published 3-minute target; patients send a one-tap Health Summary Report to their clinician. No clinician console is public.
- **Dario, Heartbeat, Carbon/Alertive.** Only marketing-level workflow. Alertive UK (not confirmed to be the same company) documents alert states with acknowledgement tracking and role-based routing (alertive.co.uk/features).
- **NICE heart-failure alert triage** (nice.org.uk): review alerts, phone the patient, safety-net if well, urgent review if symptomatic. A two-outcome protocol.
- **Ada.** A vignette study (PMC7745523) rated Ada 97% safe and Babylon 95.1%, but most authors were Ada staff and it used vignettes, not patients. The Lancet critique of Babylon was about doctor-entered trial data. Ada's clinician handover report layout and whether hard red-flag rules sit outside its reasoning engine could not be confirmed.

## Not found anywhere
A claim timeout with extension, a hand-back reason list, "no patient visible before claim", a safety-concern button, QA sampling, and any clinician screen screenshot. **Our design is therefore original here, not copied.** That is a finding in itself: the timed claim, extension, reason-coded hand-back, and audited one-screen summary are Tarragon's own.

## Decisions from this pass
1. **Keep the timed, audited claim; do not copy the Epic and Cerner takeover.** Their claim is advisory and any peer can take over. Our existing rule is that only the assigned doctor or the CMO may start review (INV-12), so a peer takeover stays out. Time-out and return to the queue is the Teladoc precedent. (Applied: S17 already does this.)
2. **Overdue must re-route, not just turn red** (Epic only colours the due date). Our SLA clock is data in `escalation_slas` and S17's expiry sweep returns a lapsed task to the queue. Follow-up: show an SLA-coloured age chip on the held task and the queue counts.
3. **Pull-next stays the primary path; browsing a pool stays out** (Epic and Cerner rely on filters and etiquette). Already how the queue works.
4. **Hand-back reason codes are our own addition** (no vendor documents one). Already shipped; keep, and report why items bounce.
5. **Delegation separate from pool membership** (Epic Attach with an out-of-contact window). Ours is `provider_time_off` and availability, already consulted by auto-assignment.
6. **Scope of cover matters** (the Annals practice). Define which task types the on-duty doctor must clear same day. Follow-up for the CMO.
7. **Per-task fee needs a quality gate** (OpenLoop per-visit pay vs Hims hourly). S30 fees are per task; add sampled review and a minimum-dwell flag (S20 quality work). Consider a "patient unreachable" fee for phone tasks, like OpenLoop's no-show fee.
8. **A non-doctor tier in front of the doctor with a stated time target** (Livongo, Dario). Matches our founder principle that coordinators are a scaling lever, not a gate; do not make it a required step.
9. **A patient-generated summary pushed to the clinician** (Livongo). Candidate for a later session: it helps when readings arrive late.
10. **Do not quote vendor-run safety studies** (Ada). Our deterministic triage with `clinician_override` stays; any evaluation uses local clinicians.

## Still open evidence (would need a vendor demo or sandbox login)
Epic and Cerner admin settings for pools, Cerner Task List, Wheel's portal and consult lifecycle, any RPM vendor's clinician dashboard. If a vendor offers a demo or sandbox, a recording would close these.

# Fourth pass: code-level review of open-source software, and scribe review research (2026-10-06)

Proprietary competitor code (Epic, Cerner, Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Heidi, Healthie, Canvas) is not public, so none was reviewed. The code reviewed is open source. The reading was README, directory and single-file level through a summarising fetch, not a full source audit; "not in the docs" is not proof "not in the code". Licences: Medplum Apache-2.0, OpenMRS MPL-2.0, OpenEMR/Bahmni/GNU Health copyleft (not verified); we copy ideas only, no code.

## Console patterns (Medplum, OpenMRS, OpenEMR, Bahmni)
- **Medplum Task** (medplum.com/docs/careplans/tasks): the claim is a plain `owner` field, unclaimed is `owner` missing, a queue is a search, priority is a small fixed set, due date is separate from priority, `statusReason` explains a hold. FHIR says nothing about two people claiming at once, so the guard must be added: a single conditional update. **Our `queue_next` already does this atomically with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and the 50-session race proof; none of the open projects does.**
- **Medplum PatientSummary** (packages/react/src/PatientSummary): config-driven list of independent sections, so one failed fetch does not blank the screen. Provenance per item not evidenced; ours is the audit row plus the section list.
- **OpenMRS queue entry** (openmrs-module-queue QueueEntry.java): wait time is derived from `startedAt`, an entry is closed with `endedAt` not deleted, `sortWeight` lets a supervisor reorder, `providerWaitingFor` is a soft assignment. No claim or timeout field. Its wait display rounds first, then splits into units, and keeps SLA thresholds out of the formatter (wait-time.ts). Its queue page polls fast while the user is active and slows when idle (activity-aware-refresh-interval.ts).
- **OpenEMR patient tracker**: append-only status rows give an audit trail and time-in-state for free, with no concurrency control (what not to copy).
- **FHIR AuditEvent** (hl7.org/fhir/auditevent.html): who, role, action, outcome, patient, entity, source, recorded time, with denied attempts recorded too. Our `audit_log` rows already carry actor, reason, result (success or denied), subject patient and basis.

## Scribe review (OpenScribe MIT, 1984Doc/AI-Scribe GPL, EMRgent AGPL; arXiv studies)
- None of the three open scribes documents a consent state machine, omission checks or per-section sign-off. EMRgent's "every write is gated behind an approval card showing the exact fields" is the nearest precedent for our signature rule.
- "LLM Judges Verify Presence, Not Absence" (arXiv 2608.31016): LLM judges score 0.79 to 0.94 on added or altered content but only 0.50 to 0.63 on omissions (a coin flip). A closed, per-fact presence check does better. So omission detection must not be delegated to another model.
- "One note in three" (arXiv 2608.31017): 565 notes from three deployed commercial scribes, 31.3% had a verified failure, clustered in allergy and medicine information; omission is the dominant class; **review instructions alone moved measured error rates from 9.3% to 79.0%**. Telling reviewers what to look for matters most.
- Showing a model's reasoning or highlights did not protect against biased predictions in a vignette study (PSNet summary, snippet only). No trial of forced per-section confirmation was found, so that design is reasoning, not evidence; measure it.
- Nigerian Pidgin ASR is under-resourced (arXiv 2010.11123, older, non-clinical). No published figures for Nigerian-accented clinical speech were found; measure on local consented audio before any live use.

## Applied now (branch `s35b/review-ux`)
- Scribe draft review: every section must be confirmed before "Use in note"; an empty section is flagged as a possible omission and confirmed as "Nothing was discussed about this"; editing a section clears its confirmation; a visible prompt tells the reviewer to look for what is missing (allergies, medicines and doses, negations, follow-up). This does not use a model to judge omissions. Writes nothing to the record (INV-11).
- Queue: an overdue badge and minutes-left chip computed from the task's own due time with no threshold in code (OpenMRS lesson: thresholds are data); an "Updated HH:MM" stamp so a stale page never looks fresh.

## Plan, in order (each its own piece)
1. **Facts-to-confirm step before prose** (closed list of typed facts with transcript spans, confirmed by the clinician; prose may cite only confirmed facts). Needs an `scribe-draft` output change, an AI-017 evaluation and CMO review, so it is not a UI-only change.
2. **Hard three-state consent gate** (not asked, given, declined; no audio unless given; live indicator). A separate session already made scribe consent patient-answered (`s21g`); confirm the clinician-side gate against it first (OQ-211).
3. **Hash-bound sign**: store a content hash of exactly what was signed, with the AI draft, edits and signed text kept as separate records and the model and prompt version recorded.
4. **SLA warning colour from versioned config** (a PROPOSED value, CMO to confirm) and **activity-aware refresh** for the queue page.
5. **Supervisor `sortWeight`** for the queue (CMO reorder on top of priority), only if the CMO asks.
6. **Measured evaluation**: sampled audits of scribe notes against audio, edit rates by section, and a local WER test for Nigerian English and Pidgin before the scribe is switched on.
