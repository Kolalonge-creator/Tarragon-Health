# S07y research: cuff size, validated devices, the pre-visit summary (short)

Written 2026-10-20 on `s07y/bp-cuff-and-visit-summary`. Studied for behaviour and numbers only, nothing copied. Everything below is PROPOSED for the Chief Medical Officer; nothing is signed.

## 1. Cuff size by arm circumference

A cuff that is too small reads high and one that is too large reads low, which is why every guideline asks for the bladder to fit the arm. The published tables agree on four adult bands by mid-arm circumference (measured halfway between the shoulder and the elbow):

| Mid-arm circumference | Cuff size |
|---|---|
| 22 to 26 cm | small adult |
| 27 to 34 cm | adult (standard) |
| 35 to 44 cm | large adult |
| 45 to 52 cm | extra large adult (the AHA table labels the largest band for the thigh) |

- **AHA 2019**, Muntner P, et al. "Measurement of Blood Pressure in Humans: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association", Hypertension 2019;73:e35 (the cuff-size table). ESH carries the same bands (ESH 2023 guidelines, Mancia G, et al., J Hypertens 2023;41:1874, and the ESH 2018 guidelines). The Nigerian guideline (NG-2023 section 3.1.1) carries a sizing table by mid-arm circumference; the CMO checks the numbers against it.
- **Source honesty**: these ranges were written from the published tables as remembered by the author of this note, not fetched and re-read during this build (no network reading step was part of it). They are therefore a proposal until the CMO compares them with the source documents. The migration seed and `bp.cuff_sizing` carry them as versioned PROPOSED config; a mirror test keeps the two identical.
- **Design choices**: the whole centimetre is looked up (round half up) so there is no gap between 26 and 27 cm; a measurement under 22 or over 52 cm gets no size, only "tell your care team"; anything under 15 cm or over 60 cm is refused as a typing slip. The step says "published tables suggest", never "you need".
- Not adopted: a cuff-size recommendation for children or for pregnancy (the tables are for adults; a pregnant arm is measured the same way but the device must be validated for pregnancy, which the existing C4 line already says).

## 2. Validated devices: where the list comes from

The list is not invented here. `public.device_catalog` (S70a, 18.2) already holds recommended devices with evidence columns and a CMO-only review; S07y adds only an admin editor and a CMO review page over it and reads it. A model is called "on our checked list" only when a row is active, recommend-only, blood pressure, and reviewed. Public sources a clinician (or an admin helping her) checks:

- **ValidateBP** (validatebp.org): the US validated device listing run for the AMA and AHA, with the validation basis for each model.
- **STRIDE BP** (stridebp.org): the international listing backed by ESH, ISH and WHL. The Nigerian guideline text (C4 wording) already points patients here.
- Other national lists, such as the Hypertension Canada list, and a published validation study (the editor offers "a published validation study" as the basis).
- **Nigerian supply**: a NAFDAC registration number or a named authorised distributor is required before a row can be reviewed (the existing database rule). Tarragon sells nothing (founder decision 2026-08-02).
- Wrist, finger, watch and ring devices are not used for care decisions (existing C4 wording; AHA 2025 summary).

The list is empty of reviewed rows today (the table held 3 unreviewed rows on 2026-10-20), so the honest patient answer is "we do not have a checked list yet, look up your model at stridebp.org". The words "not validated" are never used for a model that is merely not on the list.

## 3. The "bring this to your visit" summary

- Common practice, not re-read for this build (no web research step was run, so treat as background, not evidence): a visit sheet is short, shows the average rather than every number, medicines taken and symptoms, and is held by the patient. Decision taken here: no colour, grade, "controlled" label or target comparison on the patient-held sheet. A patient reading "above target" on paper outside the app, with no care-team context, is not safe, and OQ-67 means the bands are not yet settled.
- Averaging: the 7-day average appears only when the home programme's own gate is met (the shared `assessHomeCheck`, the same code the phone card runs, 12 readings over 3 days in the proposed values); otherwise "not enough readings yet" with what is missing. The in-app trend card uses the looser S07 gate (3 readings over 3 days); the sheet is deliberately the stricter of the two so it never shows a thinner average than the programme would.
- Medicines: the S08x adherence function (`private.weekly_adherence`, read through `medication_weekly_adherence` as the patient), counting taken plus late as marked taken, percentage only when the server gives one.
