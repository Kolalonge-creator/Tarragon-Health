# S39 research: security, privacy and compliance hardening

Reference platforms were studied for behaviour only; nothing was copied.

- **Row-level security review**: Supabase's own guidance (RLS on every exposed table, `security_invoker` views, pinned search paths, no anon execute by default) is the checklist; the catalog test turns it into one automatic test. Unsuitable: relying on a dashboard linter alone, because it does not know our reviewed exceptions.
- **Audit of record reads**: Break-glass access in large health record systems is normally logged with a reason and reviewed soon after (to be sourced before this note is quoted). Ours matches for about 10 tables; the gap on the rest is OQ-260. Unsuitable for a small team: alerting a human on every read (noise); a weekly summary plus an alert on a break-glass grant is enough.
- **Secrets**: GitHub secret scanning with push protection plus a history scan (gitleaks) is the common baseline; payment providers advise keeping secret keys server-side and rotating on exposure. We follow that; no real key was found in the tree.
- **Retention and erasure**: NDPA 2023 says keep personal data no longer than needed and honour erasure unless a legal duty applies; other countries set fixed periods for clinical records (to be sourced). There is no confirmed Nigerian clinical period, so we do not guess one (OQ-263). Unsafe: deleting a clinical row that must be kept, or cascading a profile delete through the ledger.
- **Backups**: managed Postgres providers advise point-in-time recovery plus a tested restore; an untested backup is not a backup. Production has neither (OQ-266). Unsuitable: restoring on a branch, which this plan does not offer.
- **Processor register**: the NDPC filing and DPIA practice expect a named list of processors, what each receives, where it sits and the contract in force. Cross-border transfer is the sensitive point for Nigeria since every processor is outside the country.
