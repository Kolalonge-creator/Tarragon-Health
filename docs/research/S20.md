# S20 research: clinical quality and safety controls

Date: 2026-10-06. Public sources only. "Unverified" means I did not find a public document confirming it; treat as industry folklore or my inference.
Scope note: Glooko, Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Ambience, Corti, Clafiya, Doctolib, Wheel, OpenLoop, Hims & Hers publish no clinician audit, graduation or speak-up mechanics that I found. Every competitor claim below not cited is unverified.

## 1. First-N-task audit counters and graduation

- Competitors: Omada says peer and supervisor feedback plus clinical chart audits are part of its quality assurance, and chart audits featured in its NCQA accreditation. No public first-N or graduation numbers. Wheel/OpenLoop (clinician marketplaces): credentialing and onboarding are described, per-task probation counters are unverified.
- Manchester Triage System: audit is defined per user, not per organisation (see section 2); this is the closest public precedent for per-clinician counters.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: a fixed N that resets whenever a freelancer is idle for months would stall a small pool; counting calendar time instead of tasks would graduate people who barely worked. A hard block when no senior reviewer is online can strand red events.
- Tarragon should adapt: per-clinician, per-task-type counter (a graduated generalist is still on audit for a first titration). Graduate only when N audited tasks are done AND score and zero-critical-miss criteria met, never on count alone. Reviewer SLA fallback: if the auditor is late, the task is still released (patient safety first) but the counter does not advance. Clinical lead can extend or reset. Log every graduation as a signed decision.

## 2. Monthly audit sampling and structured scoring

- Competitors: MTS developers define audit sample as 2 percent of episodes, minimum 10 per user, and one adopter audits three times a year (PLOS ONE paper, PMC). NHS-style and ISO 2859 acceptance sampling are the general-QA references; I did not verify exact ISO 2859 plan tables, treat as unverified. Omada structured scoring forms: not public.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: 10 percent of all tasks plus 100 percent of red and titration events can exceed reviewer capacity when the senior pool is two or three people; a per-clinician minimum (MTS style) over-samples part-timers with 3 tasks a month.
- Tarragon should adapt: keep 100 percent of red events and titrations as hard rules; sample the rest at 10 percent with a floor of min(5, all tasks) per clinician per month, and a ceiling per reviewer per week so the queue stays honest. Seeded random draw stored with the seed (reproducible, not cherry-picked). Reviewers never audit own work or an associate they supervise. Structured form: safety-critical items scored pass/fail (red-flag recognised, dose within protocol, escalation timely), quality items on a 3-point scale, free-text rationale mandatory for any fail. Offline-tolerant: review forms save drafts locally (low bandwidth).

## 3. Hand-back thresholds and reliability scores

- Competitors: no public data. Marketplace norms (Wheel, OpenLoop) on declining or returning work: unverified.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: power cuts and poor connectivity cause legitimate hand-backs; freelancers with few tasks produce noisy rates; punishing hand-backs teaches clinicians to keep work they should return, which is the unsafe direction.
- Tarragon should adapt: never penalise a hand-back with a reason code in a safe set (out of competence, connectivity loss, conflict). Flag only patterns: e.g. rolling 30-day rate above a threshold AND at least a minimum task count, then route to the clinical lead as a conversation, not an automated sanction. Reliability score is descriptive (timeliness, hand-back reasons, audit score) with confidence shown, never used alone to cut pay or access. Late hand-backs (near SLA) tracked separately because they are the dangerous kind.

## 4. Protected speak-up route

- Competitors: NHS England Freedom to Speak Up guidance requires guardians and states organisations will not tolerate retaliation or detriment; policies stress "speak up, listen up, follow up" (response tracking). GMC and CQC expectations on raising concerns: consistent with this but I did not fetch them; treat the specifics as unverified. Babylon Health: public reporting of clinician-raised safety concerns exists in UK press; I could not verify details from a primary source in this pass, so the lesson is inferred: concerns routed through commercial management or dismissed as "disruption" is the failure mode.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: a small team means anonymity is easily broken by context; freelancers have no employment protection and fear losing work silently (quiet de-allocation of shifts is retaliation that leaves no trace); Nigerian whistleblower law coverage for private health companies is unverified.
- Tarragon should adapt: concerns table with row-level access for clinical lead (and a named backup, plus superadmin break-glass logged) only, invisible to operations and finance. Separate from patient complaints. Acknowledge within 2 working days, outcome tracked to closure, reporter sees status. Retaliation guard: any de-allocation, rota cut or pay change for a reporter in 12 months after a concern triggers an automatic review to the backup lead. Allow anonymous submission with a warning that context may identify them. Out-of-band path to the board if the clinical lead is the subject.

## 5. Licence and indemnity expiry automation

- Competitors: no public detail for Doctolib, Wheel, OpenLoop; automated credential-expiry removal is standard marketplace practice but specifics are unverified.
- Nigeria facts (MDCN, via search summary): practising licence renewal fee must be paid before 31 December for the ensuing year; fees N20,000 (under 10 years) or N40,000 (10+ years) per the sources found; renewal needs CPD evidence, 20 credit units a year or 40 for two years, from three or more activity groups. Verify current fees on the MDCN portal before coding.
- Unsuitable: a single 31 December cliff means many clinicians lapse on 1 January together, and a hard nightly removal could empty the rota overnight in a small pool. MDCN records may lag payment. Clinicians may hold a receipt before the register updates.
- Tarragon should adapt: store expiry date plus evidence file; warn at 60, 30, 14, 7 days; nightly job removes from queue and rota at expiry, with a bounded grace only if the clinical lead records a dated written exception (receipt of payment). Reassign their open tasks automatically and alert the lead the day before. Same logic for indemnity cover and for CPD evidence. Reinstatement requires a verified document, not a toggle.

## Recommendations for S20

- Per-clinician, per-task-type audit counters; graduate on count plus score plus zero critical misses; lead can extend.
- Audit 100 percent of red events and titrations, 10 percent random of the rest, with a per-clinician floor and per-reviewer cap; store the random seed.
- Structured form: pass/fail safety items, scaled quality items, mandatory rationale on fails; no self-audit.
- Hand-backs with a safe reason code are never penalised; flag only patterns above a minimum task count, routed to the clinical lead.
- Reliability score is advisory with sample size shown; never an automatic pay or access sanction.
- Speak-up concerns visible only to clinical lead and backup, with 2 working-day acknowledgement and tracked closure.
- Automatic retaliation review on any rota or pay change for a reporter within 12 months.
- Nightly expiry job for licence, indemnity and CPD: warnings at 60/30/14/7 days, removal from queue and rota, open tasks reassigned, lead alerted.
- Handle the 31 December cluster: pre-renewal reminders from November and staggered capacity checks before 1 January.
- Verify before build: Nigerian whistleblower coverage, current MDCN fees and CPD rules, ISO 2859 plan choice, Babylon facts.
