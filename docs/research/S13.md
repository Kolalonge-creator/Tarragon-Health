# S13 research: notifications framework

Sources: public help centres and docs fetched via web search on 2026-10-05 (Omada, Hello Heart, Lark, Flo, Clue, Medisafe, MyTherapy, Strava, Duolingo teardowns, Apple, Android, Expo, Resend, NCC, HIPAA commentary). Search snippets only, no page-level reads, no copied text. Evidence is thin for most vendors named in the brief; "THIN" marks claims I could not source and "inferred" marks reasoning. Third-party blog teardowns (Duolingo) are secondary sources.

## 1. Neutral wording and lock-screen privacy
- References: Medisafe hides the medication name on the lock-screen reminder by default and offers a setting to show it (secondary sources; app-store/blog level). Flo and Clue publish no dedicated discreet-notification mode; they rely on app lock (PIN/Face ID) and tell users to use the OS "show previews" setting (Flo: Anonymous Mode and PIN; Clue: passcode). Omada, Hello Heart and Lark expose per-type toggles only; nothing found about content masking. Livongo, WellDoc, Glooko, BeatO, Dario, Noom, Oviva: THIN.
- HIPAA commentary (Courier, Holland & Hart style) converges on "send the alert, not the information": push renders on the lock screen like an SMS and rides APNs/FCM, third parties we do not control.
- Platform: iOS lets users set Show Previews to When Unlocked or Never, but that is per-user and often left on. Android has `VISIBILITY_PRIVATE` plus `setPublicVersion()` so the lock screen shows a generic version; this is a developer-side control we should always set.
- We choose: neutral by default and not user-optional. Every template has a fixed generic title and body ("You have an update", "A reminder from Tarragon"), no condition, reading, drug, dose, result or clinician name. The detail lives only behind app unlock via a deep link. Android channels set `VISIBILITY_PRIVATE` with a public version. The lint (templates plus runtime payload check) rejects clinical terms and digits that look like readings.
- Unsafe: a "show details on lock screen" toggle (a shared-phone or partner-coercion foot-gun); emoji or icons that imply a condition (pill, drop, heart); a sender/channel name such as "Blood Pressure Alerts" (Android channel names are visible in system settings); deep-link URLs containing a reading or condition slug.

## 2. Quiet hours and discreet mode
- References: Apple Focus and Android DND are OS-level; apps hook them through interruption levels (Passive, Active, Time Sensitive, Critical) and Android channel importance. Hello Heart and Lark settings found expose toggles and a Reminders page, not quiet hours (THIN; may exist elsewhere). Duolingo and Strava: no quiet hours found in what I read; Duolingo times pushes to the user's habit window instead (secondary teardowns).
- Nigeria: NCC rules cap promotional SMS to 08:00 to 20:00 WAT and run the 2442 DND registry (partial DND by category includes health). Our SMS is verification-only, so DND mainly bites if we ever market by SMS; do not.
- We choose: user-set quiet hours (default 21:00 to 07:00 Africa/Lagos) that defer non-urgent push to the next window, never drop; the in-app inbox still receives them. "Discreet mode" is a profile flag that goes further: app-name-only titles, no sound or vibration pattern distinct from other apps, no badge count, and email subjects reduced to "Message from Tarragon".
- Safe-by-default rule: quiet hours never suppress emergency-class patient safety messages (see 6) and never suppress anything the user needs to act on for a time-boxed step (recheck in N minutes) if the user has opted into that reminder; the user may still mute the channel at OS level, which we cannot override and must not try to.
- Unsafe: using Apple Time Sensitive or Critical level for routine reminders (Critical needs an Apple entitlement and is for genuine emergencies); using "full screen intent" on Android for reminders.

## 3. Categories, preferences and opt-outs
- References: Omada gives separate Push and Email lists, notes changes may take up to 24 hours, and offers one-click email unsubscribe plus a device-settings route for "personalised" pushes. Hello Heart toggles BP reminders and medication reminders separately under Reminders. Lark has one notification slider plus OS settings. Strava splits email and push and splits social, challenge, club and newsletter categories; users complain mainly about "motivational" messages with no off switch (community hub). Flo has a Reminders menu covering cycle, medication, contraception, lifestyle, content.
- Lesson: the complaints are about uncategorised or un-mutable nudges, not about reminders as such. A 24-hour propagation lag is a design smell.
- We choose: fixed category set with an immutable class per template: safety (cannot be turned off, but content stays neutral), care (appointments, clinician replies, lab/test steps), reminders (readings, medication, check-ins), engagement (tips, summaries), billing/account. Per-category, per-channel (push, email, in-app) matrix; in-app inbox is always on. Preference changes apply on the next send (store as data, read at send time).
- Opt-out of a reminder category must be one tap from the notification itself where the OS allows (long-press or in-app link), and must also suppress the corresponding automated escalation of that nudge, not just the display.
- Unsafe: dark patterns (hidden toggles, re-enabling after app update); defaulting engagement-class nudges on; letting an opt-out of reminders silently also opt out of safety-class messages without telling the user what remains.

## 4. Delivery tracking, tokens and channel fallback
- Expo: the push API returns a ticket immediately (accepted by Expo) and a receipt later (docs say check after about 15 minutes) saying whether APNs/FCM accepted it. A receipt error `DeviceNotRegistered` means stop sending to that token until the device re-registers. DeviceNotRegistered can take an undefined time to appear and is hard to test by uninstalling. A receipt "ok" is acceptance by the provider, not display on screen. `priority: high` wakes the device; normal batches under Doze.
- Resend webhooks: `email.delivered`, `email.bounced` (permanent rejection), `email.complained`, plus `suppression.added/removed`; hard bounces and complaints are auto-suppressed. Treat delivered as "accepted by recipient server", not "read".
- Android: Doze defers normal-priority FCM; Tecno/Infinix (HiOS Power Manager / Phone Master) and Itel (THIN, inferred similar) often kill background delivery until the user whitelists the app or allows auto-start. This is the single biggest real-world cause of "no push" in Nigeria.
- We choose: a delivery state machine per message per channel: queued, deferred (quiet hours), sent (ticket ok), accepted (receipt ok), opened or tapped (client-side ack), failed (typed reason), expired. Store ticket id and receipt id; poll receipts in a job; on DeviceNotRegistered, mark the token dead and fall back; never retry-loop a dead token.
- Fallback ladder for must-reach items: push, then in-app inbox (always written first), then email after a time threshold stored as data (not code), then, only for verification codes, SMS. Never fall back from push to SMS for clinical content; SMS carries a code or a generic "open the app" line at most. No feature may depend on a send succeeding.
- Add: an in-app "notifications not arriving?" health card that detects missing receipts/opens and guides the user through the OEM battery whitelist with per-brand steps; log "push silent for N days" as a support signal.
- Unsafe: treating the ticket as proof of delivery; deleting the token on the first transient error; counting email "delivered" as acknowledged.

## 5. Frequency caps and nudge tone
- Duolingo (secondary teardowns): a bandit chooses among pre-written variants, scored on whether the user acts within about 2 hours; reportedly two slot types, routine and "save", with a cap of roughly two per day and demotion of recently shown copy; guilt-flavoured tone is part of the brand. Strava users push back on unrequested "motivational" notices. Medication apps (Medisafe, MyTherapy): reminder plus snooze/skip/taken actions (general knowledge; details THIN).
- We choose: hard caps per user per day by class (reminders and engagement combined small, safety exempt but deduplicated), a per-template cooldown, and a "nothing to say" default: if the user already logged today, send nothing. Tone: warm, short, no guilt, no streak-loss language, no counts of missed readings in the text. Escalating silence after ignored nudges (back off, then offer a pause) instead of escalating pressure.
- Add: variant copy is allowed but pre-approved and lint-checked; any adaptive timing learns the user's habit window only, never content. Snooze (15 min, 1 hour, tomorrow) and "I did it" as notification actions where the OS supports it; actions must not echo clinical text.
- Unsafe: gamified streak shaming and fake urgency (also banned by brand guide); optimising for opens when the real goal is a recorded reading; sending more because the previous one was ignored.

## 6. Urgent clinical and on-call alerts vs patient alerts
- References: Apple Time Sensitive (breaks Focus, stays on lock screen about an hour) and Critical (bypasses mute, entitlement needed); Android high importance channel. HIPAA commentary: alert, not information. Hospital paging products and Livongo/Teladoc clinician-alert behaviour: THIN, nothing published I could verify.
- We choose: two separate audiences with different rules. Patient safety messages (go to hospital, call someone) use a neutral title with a generic body, high priority, one retry after a short window then in-app plus email, and never include the reading. Clinician/on-call alerts go to staff devices and the console and may be more specific only inside the authenticated app; push to staff still says "New priority case" with no patient name. Escalation timing comes from the existing `escalation_slas` data, not constants in code.
- The ladder (push, email, SMS for emergencies) from the platform spec applies to clinician paging; SMS to a clinician is allowed for paging only and must carry no patient detail.
- Add: acknowledgement is an authenticated in-app action; an unacknowledged alert escalates by data-driven rule; every send, receipt and ack is audited.
- Unsafe: relying on Critical alerts for patients; any patient name, reading or diagnosis in clinician push text; a single channel for an emergency; silent drops when quiet hours apply (safety-class bypasses deferral, but still obeys neutral wording).

## 7. Caregiver and family notifications
- References: caregiver-alert features exist in medication apps (Medisafe "Medfriends" style, THIN on details), Withings/Qardio family sharing (THIN), and Flo has no partner alerts by design. Findings on intimate-partner risk are from general privacy guidance, not a Nigerian study (THIN).
- We choose: caregiver notifications are consent-scoped, go only to the caregiver's own account (never to the patient's phone), use the same neutral wording, and carry only "Someone you support has an update, open the app" style text. What the caregiver sees after unlock is governed by the existing category-scoped access model, not the notification layer.
- Patients can see exactly who receives what, revoke in one tap, and the patient is told when a caregiver is added. Reproductive-health category never triggers a caregiver notification without an explicit grant for that category.
- Shared phones: a phone used by several relatives means a notification may be read by the wrong person; the neutral default is the main defence, backed by in-app unlock.
- Unsafe: SMS or WhatsApp-style relays to a family number; notifying a partner or relative by default; naming the patient in the caregiver push; "emergency contact auto-notify" text that reveals the reason.

## 8. Email role: receipts, statements, verification, bounces
- References: Omada email has one-click unsubscribe; Strava separates email from push. Resend gives delivered, bounced, complained, suppressed events and a suppression list; signature-verified webhooks.
- We choose: email is for things people keep or act on away from the phone: verification links/codes, payment receipts, statements, appointment confirmations, account security. Subjects and bodies stay neutral; documents (statements, results) are behind login links, not attached. No clinical content in subject or preview text. Engagement email is a separate category with unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe headers; transactional is exempt but minimal.
- Pipeline: verify the webhook signature, store each event against the message, mark address "suppressed" on hard bounce or complaint, show an in-app "we could not reach your email" banner, and never keep sending to a suppressed address. Soft bounces retry with a cap. A complaint disables engagement email and flags the account for review (it is a signal, not necessarily abuse).
- Unsafe: emailing results or readings; trusting `delivered` as read; sharing an inbox-based verification on shared family emails without an in-app re-confirmation.

## 9. Localisation (English and Nigerian Pidgin) and plain language
- References: no health app I found publishes Pidgin notification copy (THIN; absence not proven). Nigerian telehealth (Doctoora, Kangpe, ConnectClinic, Helium Health, Wellahealth, Mecure, HMO apps like Reliance/Hygeia): public notification behaviour THIN; most communicate by WhatsApp/SMS/calls, which we do not use. M-TIBA, Vezeeta, Practo, Eka Care: appointment/reminder messaging is standard but published detail on privacy is THIN.
- Policy note: the platform is English-only by recorded founder decision. Pidgin would be a new decision, so treat it as an optional later layer, not scope for S13.
- We choose: keyed templates with a locale column from day one (English only now), reading level around grade 6, under about 60 characters in the title, one action per message. Avoid idioms and abbreviations; no medical words even in "friendly" forms.
- Add: a lint step that also runs on any future locale; Pidgin copy, if approved, needs reviewer sign-off and its own forbidden-term list because Pidgin and local-language terms for conditions ("sugar", "pressure") are themselves identifying. Include these in the English lint too.
- Data cost: payloads stay small; no images, no rich media; emails avoid heavy HTML images.

## Ideas worth adopting
- Medisafe-style default masking, but make it non-optional and extend it to every template via a lint, not just drugs.
- Duolingo-style habit-window timing and a hard daily cap with template cooldown; drop the guilt.
- Strava-style category x channel matrix, with applied-instantly preferences (fixing Omada's lag).
- Expo ticket then receipt tracking with `DeviceNotRegistered` tombstoning, and a Resend event store with suppression mirroring.
- An in-app "not receiving notifications?" diagnostic with Tecno/Infinix/Itel whitelist steps, driven by missing receipts and opens.
- Android `VISIBILITY_PRIVATE` plus public version on every channel; generic channel names.
- Escalation timings and caps as data (config tables), consistent with the `escalation_slas` principle.
- Always-written in-app inbox as the source of truth, other channels as additive.

## Unsafe for us
- Any condition, reading, drug, dose, result or clinician name in push text, email subject, channel name, deep-link slug or emoji.
- A "show details on lock screen" option, or personalised content learned from behaviour.
- Critical alerts or Time Sensitive for routine reminders; full-screen intents.
- SMS for anything beyond verification codes and clinician paging; WhatsApp fallback (removed; banned).
- Guilt or streak-loss copy; escalating frequency when a user ignores nudges.
- Default-on notifications to partners, relatives or caregivers; any notice revealing why someone is being contacted.
- Treating a ticket, receipt or email `delivered` as proof the person saw it.
- Promotional messaging by SMS (NCC window, DND 2442, health category opt-in) and anything that implies Tarragon needs SMS to work.
