# S08 Research: Medication reminders and adherence (Nigeria focus)

Status: research note, no code. All sources accessed 2026-10-04. Public sources only. Nothing here is copied from a competitor; competitor notes are paraphrased observations.
Limits of this research: store pages (Play, some App Store pages) did not render in the fetch tool, so review-based findings come from search-result summaries and the vendor's own help text, not a first-hand sample of 1-star/3-star reviews. Treat review claims as "reported", and re-sample reviews manually before relying on any single one. Round Health, Noom, Dario and Omada medication features were only partly findable.

## 1. Per-product notes

### Medisafe
Sources: https://www.medisafe.com/privacy-policy/ ; https://www.medisafe.com/faq/why-did-my-reminders-stop-working-android/ (404 on fetch, snippet via search) ; https://www.trustpilot.com/review/medisafe.com ; https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.medisafe.android.client ; https://medisafe.com/education-resources/the-impact-of-medisafes-medfriend-caregiver-feature-on-adherence ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/medisafe-study-shows-the-positive-impact-of-patient-caregiver-connections-on-medication-adherence-rates-300181771.html
- Strengths: broad drug/interaction content, refill tracking, a "Medfriend" caregiver link that alerts a nominated person if a dose is unmarked (reported about 1 hour after scheduled time). Vendor claims higher 90-day retention with a Medfriend (vendor-reported, not independent).
- Weaknesses (reported): reminders stop on Android when cleaner/booster apps or OEM battery managers kill the app (vendor's own FAQ blames cleaner apps); updates that broke editing schedules; force-stops; support delays; basic reminders moved behind a paywall, which users with disabilities objected to.
- Privacy: collects health data and geolocation; shares with research partners/pharma; aggregated de-identified data may be sold; Medfriend designation is treated as the patient's consent to disclose medication information to that person.
- Adopt: a visible, plain "why might my reminders fail on this phone" troubleshooting page tied to the phone brand.
- Avoid: paywalling core reminders; broad commercial reuse of de-identified medication data; caregiver notification as an automatic default.

### MyTherapy (smartpatient GmbH)
Sources: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mytherapy-medication-reminder/id662170995
- Strengths: 4.8/5 (8.6K ratings on the US App Store at fetch time); praised for simplicity; combines medication, BP/weight measurements, symptom/pain diary, charts, refill reminders, family profiles; praised by an ADHD user for "reward" without heavy gamification (single review, anecdotal).
- Weaknesses (reported): manual stock entry; limited custom symptom tracking; App Store privacy labels list third-party advertising/analytics use of identifiers and health-and-fitness data (labelled not linked to you).
- Adopt: low-key, achievement-neutral feedback; BP and medication in one timeline (matches Tarragon's shared record).
- Avoid: advertising/analytics SDKs near health data. Tarragon should ship none on this module.

### Round Health
Sources: https://www.imedicalapps.com/2016/11/round-health-app-personal-assistant-pill-taking/ ; search summary of review pages (apprview.com listing)
- Strengths: dose "windows" (morning, evening) instead of a single exact minute, with up to three alerts across the window (start, middle, end); pill count and refill awareness; Apple Watch support; streak counting.
- Weaknesses: iOS only; no caregiver sharing; no injection support; the product appears unmaintained or niche (could not verify current release notes).
- Adopt: time windows with one gentle follow-up rather than repeated alarms; the window concept suits patients without fixed routines.
- Avoid: streak counters (see Rejected).

### CareClinic
Sources: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tracker-reminder-careclinic/id1455648231 ; https://careclinic.io/medicine-tracker/
- Strengths: meds + symptoms + vitals + sleep/mood in one place; report export for clinicians; biometric lock; localisable time zones and units.
- Weaknesses (reported): much of the app is paywalled (editing/deleting past entries, analytics, number of symptoms); forced account creation with demographic fields; reviews mixed (about 4.2 iOS, 4.4 Play).
- Adopt: clinician-ready exportable adherence summary; biometric/app lock on a health app.
- Avoid: paywalling the ability to correct a wrong log entry (it harms data quality and patient trust).

### Omada, Lark, Dario, Noom
Sources: https://www.lark.com/resources/prescription-medication-adherence ; https://www.omadahealth.com/product-innovation ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8317037/
- Little public detail on their adherence mechanics. Lark: coach-style reminders (when, how much) inside a chronic-disease programme. Omada: adherence sits alongside food, activity, sleep and lab behaviour change; human coaching. Dario/Noom: no specific reminder features found, so no claims made.
- Pattern worth noting: for these products adherence is a conversation inside a coached programme, not a standalone alarm. That matches Tarragon's model (clinician review).
- Adopt: route repeated misses to a human review path (care team message), not more alarms.
- Avoid: conversational "coach" copy that sounds clinical or judgmental.

### Cross-product failure modes to design against
1. Reminder silently stops on aggressive Android OEM builds (all products affected; vendors' only fix is user settings).
2. Confusing snooze semantics (snooze vs skip vs taken, unclear what is logged).
3. Accidental "taken" taps on a lock-screen action, recording a dose that was never taken.
4. Shaming or guilt copy and broken streaks.
5. Caregiver alerts that expose a patient's medication to others without a clear, revocable consent.
6. Paywalled basics and advertising/analytics in health apps.

## 2. Evidence and formula

### What the literature says
- WHO (2003), "Adherence to long-term therapies: evidence for action": adherence to long-term therapy in developed countries averages about 50%, and is worse in developing countries; it calls adherence a multi-factor problem (patient, condition, therapy, health system, socio-economic), not just forgetfulness. https://www.paho.org/sites/default/files/WHO-Adherence-Long-Term-Therapies-Eng-2003.pdf
- PDC (proportion of days covered) is a claims-based measure: days in a period with medication on hand divided by days in the period. The Pharmacy Quality Alliance uses 80% as the "adherent" threshold for chronic therapies (CMS Part D Stars use it); some specialist areas (antiretrovirals) use 90%+. PDC is preferred over MPR (medication possession ratio) because PDC caps coverage at the period length and handles overlapping supply; MPR can exceed 100%. https://www.bcbsnd.com/providers/programs/bluealliance-and-bluealliance-care-plus/hedis-tip-sheets/hedis-tip-sheet-search/pdc ; https://pharmaanalytics.substack.com/p/adherence-and-persistence-measure
- Limits of 80%: it is a population-level convention rather than a clinical cut-off for any one drug; it measures supply, not ingestion; it hides timing (a patient taking a twice-daily drug once daily can look about 50%, but a patient taking all doses 8 hours late looks 100%); it does not apply well to short courses or as-needed drugs; the right threshold is drug-specific (antihypertensives forgive less than vitamins).
- Reminder trials: mixed. Meta-analyses report modest gains (e.g. SMS reminder pooled SMD 0.36 in type 2 diabetes; app interventions improved MMAS-8 by about 0.57 points) but a large trial (REMIND, 2,710 CVD adults) found no significant 12-month difference from reminder devices. Conclusion: reminders help some people, are not a cure, and self-logging is subject to bias. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9850787/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7045248/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10916-024-02135-2
- Implication: an in-app percentage is self-reported behaviour, not pharmacy-verified coverage. Label it as "doses you marked taken", never as a clinical PDC.

### Recommended single formula: weekly dose-level adherence
Name: `weekly_adherence_pct` over the last 7 completed Lagos calendar days (rolling, inclusive of today's already-due doses).

```
due      = scheduled doses whose scheduled time is in [window_start, now - grace]
           for scheduled (not as-needed) medications, within each medication's active dates
excluded = doses on days the medication was paused by a clinician or before start / after stop,
           and doses logged as "not applicable: clinician held"
numerator   = due doses with status TAKEN (taken at any time from window opening until
              the dose's close time, see below, including "taken late")
denominator = due - excluded
weekly_adherence_pct = round(100 * numerator / denominator)   // null if denominator < 3
```
Rules:
- As-needed (PRN) medications are excluded entirely (no schedule, no denominator). Show use counts separately.
- Multiple doses per day: each dose is its own item (dose-level, not day-level). Do not collapse to "any dose today".
- Dose close time: a dose is "open" from its scheduled time until the earlier of next dose of the same drug minus 1 hour, or 6 hours (24 hours for once-daily drugs marked flexible). Marking taken inside the open interval counts as taken (late is recorded for the timeline but not penalised in the percentage).
- Missed = open interval ended with no log. Counts in denominator, not numerator.
- Skipped = patient deliberately chose Skip with a reason (side effect, ran out, felt well, other). Counts in the denominator as not taken (otherwise the metric can be gamed or hide side effects), but is reported separately from missed with the reason, and the UI never describes it as failure. Clinician-held/paused is the only excusal.
- Fewer than 3 due doses in the window: show "Not enough doses yet", not a percentage.
- The 80% line is a display colour band for internal triage (see decision table), not a patient-facing grade, and is configurable per medication class via versioned config (CLAUDE.md rule on PROPOSED values).
- Day boundaries use Africa/Lagos (UTC+1, no DST), computed from stored UTC instants.

Why this and not PDC/MPR: no dispensing data exists in-app, so supply-based coverage cannot be measured; dose-level taken/due is the standard self-report analogue, it works for multi-dose regimens, and a 7-day window is short enough to act on (chronic review decisions) yet long enough to smooth a bad day. Excluding PRN avoids a meaningless denominator. Counting patient skips in the denominator keeps the number honest; separating them from misses keeps it fair.

## 3. Platform findings (verified against current docs)

### Expo notifications (expo-notifications)
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/notifications/ ; https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/main/packages/expo-notifications/CHANGELOG.md
- Repo today: `expo ~54.0.36`, `expo-notifications ~0.32.17` (apps/mobile/package.json). The changelog shows v58.0.0 added `delivery: 'alarmClock'` for Android date and repeating (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly) triggers, using `AlarmManager.setAlarmClock()` to bypass OEM deferral. That is newer than the installed version, so it is NOT available on SDK 54 without a deliberate SDK upgrade. I could not confirm which Expo SDK ships 58.x; check at S08 build time.
- Trigger types: time interval, date, calendar (iOS), daily/weekly/monthly/yearly (Android), plus an optional `timezone` on calendar triggers.
- Android exact timing needs `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` declared in the manifest (Android 12+). Boot persistence needs `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`.
- Docs advise Apple's guidance of not over-notifying (about two or three per hour). The 64-cap is not in the Expo page.
- Local notifications do not need network, which suits unreliable connectivity in Nigeria.

### iOS pending-notification cap
- The cap is 64 pending local notification requests per app. Apple engineer statement on the developer forums: "there is a limit of 64 ... This is a system limit and there is no way around it." Source: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171 ; also https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765490. Behaviour when exceeded (soonest 64 kept, rest discarded) comes from community and search summaries and has varied by iOS version; do not rely on it, stay under the limit.
- Calendar triggers with `repeats: true` count as ONE pending request, so a repeating daily dose costs 1 slot.
- Strategy for recurring doses: (1) use repeating calendar triggers (daily/weekly) for fixed-time regimens; (2) for irregular patterns, windows, or tapering courses schedule individual dates only for the nearest N (budget about 48, leaving 16 for follow-ups, refill and other app notifications); (3) rebuild the entire schedule (cancel all, reschedule) on app launch, foreground, medication edit, timezone/clock change, and from a once-daily background task when available; (4) track the budget centrally, one module owns scheduling; (5) surface "reminders reschedule when you open the app" only if the user has more than the budget, and keep a server-side or in-app today list as source of truth.

### Android exact alarms, Doze, battery
Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule ; https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby ; https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13161072
- `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`: not pre-granted on fresh installs for Android 13+ (user-granted, revocable); on Android 14 it is denied after backup/restore to a new device. Check `canScheduleExactAlarms()` and listen for the permission-state-changed broadcast to reschedule.
- `USE_EXACT_ALARM`: auto-granted and not revocable, but Google Play policy restricts it to alarm/timer apps and calendar apps that show event notifications. A medication reminder is not named, so do NOT declare it (risk of Play rejection). Use `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` and ask the user in a clear in-app screen.
- Alarms are cancelled at reboot unless rescheduled from a `BOOT_COMPLETED` receiver (`RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`).
- Doze defers normal alarms; `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle()` can fire in Doze but not more often than once per about 9 minutes per app; `setAlarmClock()` fires at exact time and the system leaves low-power mode, but is intended for apps where precise time is core and it shows an alarm-clock indicator.
- Force stop: the system stops the app and its pending alarms/intents until the user opens it again; cannot be worked around (community and vendor sources, e.g. https://help.pushwoosh.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028478751). Same applies to some OEM "clean up/freeze" actions.
- Battery optimisation exemption (`REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`) is restricted by Play policy to specific core-function categories; medication reminders are not listed explicitly. Prefer a settings deep-link ("Battery settings") that the user opens, rather than the direct exemption intent, until policy fit is confirmed with Google.

### Vendor battery killers (Tecno, Infinix, itel, Samsung)
Sources: https://dontkillmyapp.com/tecno ; https://dontkillmyapp.com/samsung ; (itel and infinix pages returned 404, so Transsion coverage comes from the Tecno page and a developer PR note at https://github.com/jeffrine7882-spec/LabCare/pull/36)
- Tecno/Infinix/itel run Transsion OSes (HiOS, XOS, itel OS), which share a Phone Master / power manager. dontkillmyapp reports the OS can freeze apps (timers, services) until the user returns to the app and states there is no known developer-side fix. User-side mitigations: lock the app in the Recents screen; allow auto-start in Phone Master; add the app to App Booster or protected list (limited slots, reported max 4 apps); turn off power-saving management and screen-off push blocking; turn off Power Boost/sleep optimisation.
- Samsung: "Sleeping apps" and "Deep sleeping apps" can restrict apps unused for a few days (including A-series); the site says apps can be re-added to the list after firmware updates. User mitigations: set battery to "Unrestricted" for the app, remove it from sleeping/deep sleeping lists, disable "Put unused apps to sleep", lock in Recents.
- Practical mitigations Tarragon can ship: (1) detect manufacturer (Tecno/Infinix/itel/Samsung) and show a one-time, plain-language "Keep reminders working" checklist with deep links where an intent exists (best-effort; intents vary by firmware and must never crash); (2) a "Test reminder" button that fires a notification in about 60 seconds so the patient confirms it works while the screen is off; (3) detect missed-delivery signals: on every app open compare expected vs actually delivered (via `getPresentedNotificationsAsync`) and show a gentle "Your reminders may have been blocked" banner; (4) in-app inbox/Today screen always lists due doses even if a notification never fired (CLAUDE.md: no feature depends on a send succeeding); (5) opt-in fallback channel for critical medications: email or push via Expo (server-sent), SMS only per OQ-05 constraints (verification and clinician paging only, so not for dose reminders); (6) consider `delivery: 'alarmClock'` for Android only after an SDK upgrade and only on opt-in, with the alarm-clock icon explained.

### Time zone and clock handling
- Africa/Lagos is UTC+1 with no DST. Store schedules as local wall time plus IANA zone string "Africa/Lagos" and reminder instants as UTC. A fixed-offset schedule never needs DST maths.
- Travelling patients (diaspora, cross-border): default to the profile zone Africa/Lagos for "dose time"; offer an explicit "I am travelling" choice rather than silently following the device zone. A device zone or manual clock change should trigger a reschedule on next foreground and a banner stating which zone reminders follow.
- Listen for time/zone change on app foreground; recompute and reschedule all pending notifications. Never log a dose with a client clock that differs from server time by more than a tolerance without flagging it; store `logged_at` (server) and `taken_at` (user stated).
- Apple forum thread on missing local notifications after a clock change confirms this is a real iOS failure mode: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/115612

### Phone off, app force-closed
- iOS: scheduled local notifications are owned by the system and still deliver when the app is force-quit or in background (UNUserNotificationCenter). If the phone is powered off, they deliver when it is next on only if still relevant, typically as missed; do not assume catch-up. Focus modes and Notification Summary may silence or delay them (time-sensitive interruption level exists but needs justification and entitlement; treat as optional).
- Android: force-stop cancels alarms until next launch; reboot cancels alarms unless rescheduled by a boot receiver; OEM freezes may block. Therefore the Today view with "due now / overdue" computed from the schedule must be authoritative, and on next open show a catch-up sheet ("Did you take your 8:00 dose?") with taken / skipped / missed options and no guilt.
- Both platforms: a device that is off at dose time means the dose is simply late; the open-interval rule in section 2 handles it.

Recommendation: build a small in-house pure TypeScript module in `packages/shared` (no dependencies): schedule expansion (fixed times, interval hours, every N days, weekdays, windows, PRN), dose-instance generation for a UTC range given Lagos wall times, open/close interval logic, and the weekly adherence formula. No third-party dependency is being recommended, so `docs/THIRD-PARTY.md` is not created or edited.

## 5. Decisions

### DECISION TABLE (behaviours Tarragon will ship)

| # | Behaviour | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notification text never contains the medicine name or dose by default: generic "Time for your scheduled care item" with discreet title; name shown only in-app after unlock (user can opt in to names) | Privacy on shared/lock-screen phones; stigma (HIV, mental health, contraception) |
| 2 | In-app Today list is the source of truth, independent of any notification | CLAUDE.md rule: no feature requires a send to succeed |
| 3 | Dose windows (flexible) in addition to exact times, with at most one gentle follow-up per dose (default 30 min after window mid-point or close, configurable) | Reduces nag fatigue; Round Health style windows are well received |
| 4 | Three clear actions: Taken, Skip (with reason), Snooze (fixed options 10/30/60 min, max 2); snooze never logs anything | Avoids confusing snooze semantics |
| 5 | "Taken" requires opening the app or a deliberate confirm on notification action; undo available for 2 minutes and any later edit is logged with reason | Prevents accidental taken taps recording false doses |
| 6 | Weekly adherence formula in section 2, labelled "doses you marked taken", min 3 doses, PRN excluded, skipped shown separately | Honest, fair, fits multi-dose |
| 7 | Neutral wording for misses ("No record for this dose. Add one if you took it.") | Avoid shame |
| 8 | Skip-with-reason feeds a clinician-visible signal (e.g. side effect reported); repeated misses (rule configurable, proposed: below 80% for 2 consecutive weeks, or 3 consecutive missed doses of a critical medication) create a care-team review task, not more alarms | Human review path, aligns with Omada/Lark pattern and Tarragon clinician model |
| 9 | Reminder health check: manufacturer-aware "Keep reminders working" checklist, test reminder, post-hoc delivery banner | Transsion/Samsung kill behaviour |
| 10 | Exact alarm permission asked in context with plain explanation; fallback to inexact when denied, with a visible "may be a few minutes late" notice | Android 13+ not pre-granted; Play policy |
| 11 | Reschedule-all on launch, foreground, medication edit, zone/clock change, and boot; iOS budget under 64 using repeating calendar triggers first | iOS cap, Android reboot behaviour |
| 12 | Catch-up sheet on open for dose intervals that closed while the app was unavailable | Phone off / force-closed |
| 13 | Dose times anchored to Africa/Lagos wall time; explicit travelling mode | Fixed offset, avoids silent drift |
| 14 | Caregiver / family visibility only by explicit, revocable consent per person, summary-level (adherence), through the existing consent graph and access categories; never push a caregiver alert without the patient having chosen it | Consent, dignity; reproductive-health category rules |
| 15 | Refill reminder from stock count (optional, opt-in) | Real cause of lapses in supply-limited settings |
| 16 | Corrections are free: users can edit or delete past dose logs with an audit trail | CareClinic paywall lesson; data quality |
| 17 | No ads, no third-party analytics SDKs in this module; core reminders never paywalled | MyTherapy/Medisafe privacy and paywall lessons |
| 18 | Offline-first: full schedule and logging work without a connection and sync later | Connectivity |
| 19 | Clinician-exportable adherence summary per medication and week (not a clinical PDC claim) | CareClinic strength; supports review |

### REJECTED (safety, dignity, or policy grounds)

- Shaming, guilt, or fear copy ("You forgot again", "You failed your streak", red alarms for a miss). Violates brand voice and increases avoidance.
- Streak counters and streak-loss messaging. Streaks punish an honest missed dose, encourage false logging, and a lost streak causes guilt; use neutral weekly totals instead.
- Medicine name, dose, condition, or caregiver identity in notification text or lock-screen preview by default.
- Auto-changing treatment: no automatic dose, schedule, or medication changes from adherence data or AI; any change goes through a clinician (tier gates unchanged). Reminders must never suggest taking a double dose after a miss.
- Caregiver public or automatic alerts: no default "tell my family I missed a dose", no alerts to unconsented contacts, no alert wording that names the medicine.
- Using `USE_EXACT_ALARM` (Play policy: alarm/timer and calendar apps only) and forcing battery-optimisation exemption intents without a policy review.
- GPL/AGPL source reuse (RxDroid, Daily Pill) and any copying of competitor text, icons, or screens.
- SMS or WhatsApp as a dose-reminder channel (SMS is for verification and clinician paging only per OQ-05; WhatsApp removed 2026-09-30).
- Claiming a clinical "PDC" or "80% adherent" status from self-reported taps; showing a patient-facing pass/fail grade.
- Selling or sharing medication/adherence data with advertisers or pharma, including "de-identified" data, without a separate explicit decision.
- Persistent unremovable or repeated high-frequency alarms that exceed Apple's guidance of two or three alerts per hour.
- Gamification that rewards logging speed or quantity (points, leaderboards).
