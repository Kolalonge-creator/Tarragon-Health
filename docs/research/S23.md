# S23 Research: AI Scribe Platforms

Reference platforms studied for the AI scribe function (spec line 1564, module 15.4).
Research covers consent flows, draft visibility, safety guardrails, STT approach, and Nigeria suitability.

## Abridge (US, enterprise-only)

- Per-encounter verbal consent from the clinician to the patient; patients can request the microphone be turned off at any time. A 2026 class-action (Washington et al v. Sutter Health) alleges some health systems auto-inserted false consent statements into records without actually informing patients, highlighting that consent must be recorded structurally, not just assumed.
- AI draft visible only to the clinician. "Linked Evidence" hyperlinks each sentence to the exact audio timestamp and transcript segment, letting the clinician audit any statement against the source conversation. Note enters the medical record only after clinician review and sign-off.
- Proprietary/custom ASR (no named third-party STT provider). Supports 28+ languages and multi-speaker diarisation. Reports a 97% catch rate for AI-generated clinical hallucinations. AES-256 at rest, TLS in transit, HIPAA/BAA. Raw audio auto-deleted within 24-48 hours after doctor approval.
- Nigeria suitability: US-only data centres, no NDPA/GDPR compliance, enterprise-only (150+ US health systems). Not viable for direct Nigeria deployment without a bespoke data-residency arrangement. The Linked Evidence audit-trail pattern is worth adapting.

## Nabla (France, GDPR-native)

- One-tap in-app patient consent screen before recording starts. Suggested consent language: "This tool will listen to our conversation and convert it into a summary, which I will review and edit for accuracy." Per-session or blanket consent is configurable by the deploying organisation. Patient can decline; clinician proceeds without the tool.
- Draft visible only to the clinician. A separate, provider-initiated action can share a patient-friendly summary post-consultation (not automatic). Audio is processed in the browser and not stored on Nabla servers by default; only the transcript and generated note are retained for a configurable period (default 14 days).
- SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, dual HIPAA and GDPR compliance. Does not train AI on customer data. Supports 35+ languages. Hosting region selectable at signup (no Africa region available; closest is EU).
- Nigeria suitability: strongest structural fit among the three dedicated scribe platforms due to GDPR heritage, multi-language support, and configurable data residency. Still requires an NDPA adequacy assessment for the hosting region. The in-app consent screen pattern and audio-not-stored architecture are directly relevant to Tarragon's design.

## Suki (US, clinician voice assistant)

- Consent obligation is placed entirely on the deploying clinician/organisation. No built-in patient-facing consent screen; clinicians must obtain "all necessary authorizations from a patient before any personal data is made available to Suki." No specification of per-session vs blanket consent.
- Clinicians receive the structured draft after the session ends, review it, then push to the EHR. No patient draft visibility before signing. Users can start, pause, resume, end, or cancel recording at any time.
- Audio permanently deleted after 30 days; de-identified data used for ML training; audio chunked so original cannot be reconstructed. HIPAA, SOC 2 Type 2, AES-256, TLS 1.2, BAA. Google Cloud infrastructure (US).
- Nigeria suitability: US-only, no international availability documented, no GDPR/NDPA compliance. The pause/resume recording control is a useful UX reference. Not viable for Nigeria.

## Clafiya (Nigeria, telehealth)

- Nigeria-based doctor consultations (online, home visits, office-based), an AI symptom checker, and care navigation with specialist referrals. No publicly documented AI scribe or ambient clinical note-drafting feature; documentation appears to be manual, clinician-written.
- As a Nigeria-native platform, data residency is inherently local. Has privacy and terms-of-use policies; specific NDPA compliance documentation not publicly detailed. Offers a USSD feature for non-smartphone access.
- Relevant not as a scribe reference but as a confirmation that no Nigerian competitor currently offers an AI clinical note scribe, meaning Tarragon would be first to market.

## Practo (India, booking + EMR)

- Practo Ray (doctor-facing) provides structured-form EMR creation, digital prescriptions, and record storage. This is a form-based EMR, not an ambient AI scribe. AI features are patient-facing (health recommendations, specialist matching), not documentation-focused.
- India-based, governed by India's DPDP Act. No NDPA compliance, no Africa data residency.
- Relevant as a reference for how a booking/records platform structures its EMR workflow without an AI scribe layer. Confirms that a structured note template (reason, history, examination, assessment, plan) is the industry standard.

## K Health (US, AI-first primary care)

- "Perfect Note" automatically generates structured draft notes from intake data, ambient audio transcription, and treatment plans. The AI copilot completes initial patient intake, summarises relevant EMR history, and generates the draft note. Closest to a full AI scribe among the supplementary platforms.
- Specific details on clinician review workflow, patient visibility of drafts, and consent mechanisms for the ambient audio component are not publicly documented. Tightly integrated into US health-system EHRs; not available standalone.
- Nigeria suitability: US-only, no international availability, not viable. The intake-to-draft-note pipeline (structured intake + audio = pre-populated note) is a useful architectural reference.

## Cross-cutting: NDPA/NDPR implications

Nigeria's NDPA (2023) prohibits cross-border transfer of personal data by default unless the receiving country has an NDPC adequacy determination, or the controller implements an approved Cross-Border Data Transfer Instrument (standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, or explicit informed consent). Health data is classified as sensitive personal data, attracting stricter requirements. None of the three dedicated AI scribe platforms has published NDPA-specific compliance. Tarragon's design must ensure: (1) audio is never stored beyond the session or sent outside the Supabase EU region without explicit patient consent; (2) the Claude API call sends the transcript, never raw audio; (3) the consent record is structural and auditable, not just a verbal claim.

## Design takeaways for Tarragon

1. **Consent must be structural and per-encounter** (Nabla's in-app screen is the model; Abridge's lawsuit shows verbal-only consent is legally fragile). Record in `scribe_consents`, not just a checkbox.
2. **Draft never visible to patient** until clinician signs (universal across all platforms). The existing `clinical_encounter_notes.status = 'draft'` + `ai_drafted = true` + RLS already enforces this.
3. **Audio retention should be minimal** (Nabla: not stored; Abridge: 24-48h; Suki: 30 days). Tarragon should store the transcript (encrypted, linked to encounter, retention-governed), not raw audio, after the session ends.
4. **Linked Evidence / audit trail** (Abridge) is worth building eventually but is Phase 2 scope; the clinician review step is the safety net for now.
5. **Pause/resume** (Suki) is already supported by the `SpeechToText.resumeFrom` interface from S14.
6. **No Nigerian competitor offers AI clinical note scribe** (confirmed via Clafiya), making this a genuine differentiator.
7. **STT vendor decision** is deferred per OQ-96; the scoring set of Nigerian consultation audio is the prerequisite.
