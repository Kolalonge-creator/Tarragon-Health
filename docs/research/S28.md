# S28 research: pharmacy partner (prescriptions to a chosen pharmacy, dispensing)

Method: public product pages and help centres only (searched 2026-10-06). Most named apps publish little about the pharmacist side, so several lines below are inference and marked UNVERIFIED. Nothing is copied; this records behaviour we adapted or refused.

**8.9 Send a prescription to a chosen pharmacy, prices compared first (Yodawy, JD Health, mPharma)**
- Yodawy: the patient uploads a prescription and the order is routed to the best-matching nearby pharmacy, then picked up or delivered; insurance claims are submitted for them. Adapted: the patient chooses, we do not auto-route. Refused: delivery (Part C.2), insurance routing (HMO is a later module).
- mPharma: a free membership, a prescription tool shared with the pharmacy, and pickup at its network. Adapted: one code the patient shows at the counter.
- JD Health: UNVERIFIED (no usable public detail found). Treated as no evidence.
- Price comparison: show a per-pharmacy total for the signed items from the pharmacy's own price list, with stock status. Unsafe for Nigeria: free-text drug names mean a wrong match could show a cheaper but different product. So the page says "price for the closest listed match" and never substitutes; the pharmacist still dispenses against the signed item.

**8.10 Refill countdown and running-low reminder (Medisafe, Yodawy)**
- Medisafe: the user enters pills left and a lead time; the reminder repeats daily until refilled. Already built in S08 (pill count and run-out date). S28 adds only the last step: the reminder opens "choose where to collect", defaulting to the pharmacy the person used last. Reminders stay neutral (INV-07): no drug name.

**8.11 Verified-batch sourcing and partner quality rules (mPharma; Tarragon decision)**
- mPharma says it buys from manufacturers and tracks expiry in its network. Tarragon cannot verify a supply chain it does not run. Built: a pharmacy can only be sent a prescription while its licence is verified and unexpired, and the pharmacist records batch number and expiry at dispensing. NAFDAC mobile authentication (8.8) stays a later prompt. UNVERIFIED: whether a Nigerian pharmacy will accept a code instead of a printed form (OQ-233).

**8.12 Pharmacist chat for medicine questions (Yodawy)**
- Yodawy offers chat with a pharmacist. Not built: who answers, liability and retention are undecided (OQ-235). The prescription "query to prescriber" flag covers the safety need in the meantime.

**Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria**
- Home delivery and cold-chain logistics (Part C.2). Controlled medicines: a pharmacy never receives them through this path (S24 hard stop).
- Sending a patient's name and clinical details by SMS or any push text (INV-07, INV-08): the pharmacy is told "a prescription is waiting" and sees detail only after sign-in.
- Power cuts and weak data: the patient must still be able to show a code on paper, so the downloadable form and the QR stay for any pharmacy.
