# S30 research: clinician fees and the earnings ledger

Date: 2026-10-06. Public sources only. "Unverified" means I found no primary document; treat as industry folklore or my inference.
Scope note: Omada, Glooko, Abridge, Suki, Nabla, Ambience, Corti, Doctolib and Clafiya publish no clinician pay mechanics that I found. They are salaried staff, software vendors, or (Doctolib) paid by the clinician. Everything below is from the platforms that do pay per piece of work.

## 1. Fixed fee per unit of work (task fee)

- Wheel and OpenLoop (clinician marketplaces): contractors are paid per completed encounter or per hour, not a share of the patient price. OpenLoop's careers page lists flat rates that differ by service and by whether the visit is live or asynchronous (about 8 to 40 US dollars, source: openloophealth.com). A task fee keyed on task type is the same shape.
- Industry write-ups put the three common models as salary, salary plus volume bonus, and fee per encounter for contractors (wheel.com blog). Tarragon uses the third for freelancers and the first for employed doctors (F-03).
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: a pay rate quoted per hour assumes steady volume. A pilot has thin volume, so a fee-only model leaves a clinician unpaid for an hour spent waiting (see section 4).
- Adapt: one fixed base fee per task type, versioned, set by the founder; no percentage of anything for tasks (D-11).

## 2. Paying more for work that has waited

- Babylon paid GPs a flat rate per appointment (reported about 20 pounds per patient at five an hour) and its pay-as-you-go economics failed (LSE, Mansfield Advisors). Lesson: a flat rate gives no reason to take the unattractive, old task, and the company margin was thin.
- Marketplaces commonly add surge or "pick-up" bonuses for hard-to-fill work. I found no published clinician-safe version, so this is unverified for healthcare.
- Unsuitable: a bonus that rises forever on a task that nobody wants can reward ignoring a task until it pays more. A task that is overdue is already paged and escalated (S16, S19), so the step must be small and must never change who is paged.
- Adapt: stepped uplift by the share of the due window that has passed when the clinician claims it (spec 7.7: +10 percent after half the window, +25 percent when overdue). The step is read at claim time, so finishing slowly never earns more.

## 3. Consultation share, fee splitting and Nigerian medical ethics

- The MDCN Code of Medical Ethics permits fees for professional services actually rendered and forbids fee splitting and payment for referrals (Code text via Scribd copy; a 2019 West African Journal of Radiology study found most Nigerian doctors did not know this). A share of a consultation the clinician personally delivered is pay for the service; a share of work they only referred is not.
- That is why D-11 says a fixed fee per task type, with the consultation share configurable. Tarragon pays a share only on a consultation the clinician delivered, never on a referral, a lab order, a pharmacy sale or a partner sale.
- Hims and Hers and similar telehealth brands run through affiliated medical groups with the clinician employed or contracted by the group; the public pay terms are not published (unverified). Do not copy a structure whose legality depends on a US corporate-practice workaround.
- Needs counsel: whether any percentage is acceptable to the MDCN for Tarragon contractors. The percentage is configuration, default empty, so the founder can leave it at nothing.

## 4. A minimum for declared hours (pilot guarantee)

- Marketplace contractors are usually unpaid while idle. A floor is a pilot recruitment tool, not a market norm (my inference, unverified).
- Risk: a floor can be farmed by declaring hours and doing nothing. Mitigations: only confirmed blocks count (an inactivity check on expired claims is not built; a follow-up), only contracted clinicians, only the shortfall is paid.
- Adapt: a top-up line per merged run of confirmed hours, computed after the run ends, equal to the shortfall.

## 5. Immutable ledger, statements and tax

- Marketplaces (Wheel, OpenLoop) give contractors a statement of each paid item and issue annual tax forms (US 1099; unverified for Nigeria). Tarragon shows every ledger line to its clinician, with the inputs that produced it.
- Append-only is the accounting norm: a mistake is fixed by an adjustment that points at the line it corrects, never an edit.
- Nigerian withholding tax: third-party guides (taxspire, nairatax, keepam, 2026) say professional fees to a resident are withheld at 5 percent and treated as final. I did not find the Act text, so it is unverified. Decision D-09 says store what is needed and do not calculate. The ledger therefore stores gross kobo only, and S31 carries the payee tax fields.
- Money is integer kobo. Percentages are applied with integer arithmetic, rounded down to the kobo, and the rule is written on the ledger line.
