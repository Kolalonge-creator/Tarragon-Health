# S87 research: how full products were forced to conform

- **Flo Health:** the US FTC settled in 2021 over health data shared with analytics and advertising firms against its privacy promises; the remedy included independent review and user consent before sharing. Lesson: the check must cover third-party SDKs, so the scan blocks ad and marketing SDK dependencies.
- **Noom:** a $56M cash plus $6M credit class settlement over trial auto-renewal and hard cancellation; the remedy was informed affirmative consent and easy cancel. Lesson: copy may mention renewal only as a negation, which scan rule `auto-renew` enforces.
- **Ovia Health:** criticised in 2019 for employer-facing reproductive data. Lesson: aggregate only, never category data; not verified in S87, tracked in OQ-312.
- **Strava and Peloton:** public maps and leaderboards created location and body-image risk. Lesson: rule `public-social`.
- **Yazio and Lifesum:** generic fasting timers are unsafe for insulin and sulfonylurea users, pregnancy and eating disorders. Lesson: rule `fasting-timer`; the guided Ramadan module is separate and signed.
- Not suitable for Nigeria: relying on vendor-published compliance badges. The scan is internal and runs in CI.
