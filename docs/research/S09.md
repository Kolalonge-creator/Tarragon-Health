# S09 Research: My Health tab, timeline, emergency card, share link

## 2.1 Timeline (Apple Health, Eka Care)

**Apple Health** shows a chronological feed of all health data, each entry labelled by source app/device (e.g. "Apple Watch", "MyFitnessPal"). Entries are grouped by day; tapping expands to show the raw value and source. No clinical interpretation inline.

**Eka Care** displays a "Health Timeline" with lab reports, prescriptions, and vitals in one stream. Each entry carries a source badge ("Self", "Lab", "Doctor"). Prescriptions appear as a distinct card type with the prescribing doctor's name. Users can filter by category. Indian platforms also show government health records (ABHA) inline.

**Tarragon adaptation**: our timeline already covers labs, medications, referrals, screenings, vaccinations, encounters, conditions, and documents. The gap is vitals (readings logged by the patient or synced from a device) and prescriptions (a prescription being signed is a clinical milestone distinct from "medication started"). Each event should carry a human-readable source label (Manual, Device, Wearable, Lab, Care team).

## 2.7 Emergency card (Apple Health, Nigeria-specific)

**Apple Health Medical ID** is accessible from the lock screen without unlocking (iOS Settings > Health > Medical ID > "Show When Locked"). It displays blood type, allergies, conditions, medications, emergency contacts, organ donor status, and a note. No QR code; a responder reads the phone screen directly.

**Nigeria-specific considerations**: power cuts make phone batteries unreliable; a printable wallet card with a QR is essential alongside any digital version. Blood genotype (AS/SS/SC) is life-critical information unique to West Africa that no international platform carries. The existing Tarragon emergency card already covers all of this: print card, QR, blood group + genotype with provenance, offline SecureStore cache on mobile.

**Lock-screen shortcut**: iOS supports Lock Screen widgets (WidgetKit, iOS 16+) and Android supports App Widgets. Both require native code and an EAS build. This is a follow-up, not an S09 deliverable.

## 2.8 Record sharing (Eka Care, Lifen)

**Eka Care** lets patients share selected health records (lab reports, prescriptions, or the full record) via a time-limited link. The patient chooses which documents to include, sets an expiry (1 hour to 30 days), and gets a shareable URL. Each access is logged and the patient can revoke the link at any time.

**Lifen** (French health data exchange) focuses on sharing between healthcare providers. A patient or doctor generates a secure link to specific documents; the recipient views without an account. Links expire and accesses are audited. GDPR-compliant consent is recorded at share creation.

**Tarragon adaptation**: the existing emergency card is a fixed-dataset emergency tool (allergies, medicines, conditions, blood, contact). Spec 2.8 asks for something broader: the patient picks which record sections to share (e.g. "my lab results and medications for the last 6 months") and generates a time-limited, audited, revocable link. This is a new `record_shares` table and public page, distinct from the emergency card.

## 2.9 Doctor summary PDF (CareClinic)

**CareClinic** generates a "Doctor Report" PDF summarizing vitals, symptoms, medications, and adherence for a chosen date range. The patient downloads it and hands it to any doctor at any facility.

**Tarragon adaptation**: the Health Passport PDF already serves this purpose (12-month summary of vitals, screenings, lab readings, conditions, medications, allergies). No new work needed; the existing PDF route (`/api/patient/health-passport/pdf`) is the doctor summary.
