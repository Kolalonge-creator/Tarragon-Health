# S25 research: catalogue, prices, orders, Paystack checkout, webhooks, entitlements

Date: 2026-10-06. Public sources only. "Unverified" means I did not find a public document confirming it, or the primary page blocked me (the Paystack docs site returned 403 to my fetch, so Paystack facts come from search summaries and third-party guides; re-read the official pages before coding).
Scope note: Omada, Personify Health, Reliance Health and the consumer apps publish little about per-item checkout or sponsor-paid display. Competitor claims not cited are unverified.

## 19.1 Per-item checkout

- Reliance Health sells personal plans on its own site: pick a plan, set the number of people, choose a billing period, then pay by card (Nairacompare, Dignited). It is plan based, not per item.
- Omada and Personify sell mostly through employers and insurers, so a patient-facing per-item checkout is not public (unverified).
- Nigerian rule of thumb: FCCPC guidance says a consumer should not normally pay more than the displayed price (Legal500, PM News 2026).
- Tarragon should adapt: one order per item, price locked at the moment the order is created and stored on the order, so a later catalogue edit never changes what someone agreed to pay. Show the final naira total on the same screen as the Pay button.
(Sources: getreliancehealth.com, dignited.com Reliance guide, legal500.com Nigeria consumer protection, pmnewsnigeria.com 2026-07-29)

## 19.2 Card, bank transfer, USSD

- Paystack lists cards, bank transfer, USSD, QR and mobile money as channels. Fees reported in search summaries: cards and USSD 1.5 percent plus NGN 100 (capped NGN 2,000, the NGN 100 waived under NGN 2,500); transfer 0.5 percent capped NGN 500. Treat as unverified until checked on the Paystack pricing page.
- Pay-with-transfer gives a one-time account number per transaction, and confirmation can lag the customer's bank by minutes (Paystack support article on transfers; timings unverified).
- Tarragon should adapt: show all three channels, with transfer and USSD labelled as "may take a few minutes to confirm". Order stays "awaiting payment" (never "failed") until Paystack says otherwise, with a visible "I have paid, check again" button that calls verify.
(Sources: paystack.com/docs/payments/payment-channels, support.paystack.com transactions pricing and pay-with-transfer, mctaba.com Paystack guide)

## 19.3 Single naira price list

- Reliance publishes naira prices per plan and period on its site (monthly, quarterly, annual) with no hidden foreign-currency layer (Nairacompare).
- FCCPC has publicly warned businesses about price display and labelling (PM News, July 2026); exact legal wording not fetched, unverified.
- Tarragon should adapt: one price per catalogue item in kobo, one source of truth, effective-dated versions. The checkout, receipt and the "what is included" page all read the same row. An admin price change creates a new version; open orders keep their old one.
(Sources: nairacompare.ng Reliance plans, pmnewsnigeria.com)

## 19.4 Pay for a loved one (international card)

- Paystack supports international cards at a higher fee, reported as 3.9 percent plus NGN 100 with no NGN 2,000 cap (search summaries, unverified against the official page). Merchants can pass fees to the customer via a dashboard setting.
- Kudi Compass and similar Nigerian guides describe diaspora families funding relatives' HMO plans by card; no standard "gift a health item" UI was found (unverified).
- Tarragon should adapt: the payer enters the recipient's phone or email, sees the item and naira price, and pays by whichever card works. The order records payer and beneficiary separately; the beneficiary gets the entitlement, the payer gets the receipt. The payer never sees the beneficiary's records. Do not show a USD number as the price; if an international card is used, the bank converts.
(Sources: support.paystack.com transactions pricing, afrotools.com Paystack fees, kudicompass.com)

## 19.5 Sponsor-paid items shown as already paid

- No competitor public pattern found for "someone else paid" display (unverified). Employer-sponsored programmes (Omada, Personify) simply show no price to the member, which is the nearest precedent.
- Tarragon should adapt: show the item with a "Paid for you by [sponsor first name or organisation]" line, a zero amount due, and a receipt that names who paid, never what the sponsor spent in total. Let the sponsor choose to be named or "a family member". If the order is later refunded, the refund goes back to the sponsor, not the beneficiary.
(Sources: none public; design inference)

## 19.7 Itemised checkout (clinician or partner fee vs Tarragon fee)

- FCCPC guidance: no price higher than displayed; price display rules apply (PM News, Legal500). No Nigerian rule found that mandates splitting clinician and platform fees (unverified).
- Hospital and clinic price transparency in Nigeria is weak and mostly voluntary (unverified); that is the opportunity, not a requirement.
- Tarragon should adapt: two or three plain lines, for example "Specialist's fee", "Lab or partner fee", "Tarragon service fee", then the total. Each line is a stored order line, so the receipt, refund and payout split use the same numbers. Show what each line pays for in one sentence.
(Sources: pmnewsnigeria.com, legal500.com)

## 19.8 Receipts, history, refunds, cancellations

- Omada's terms say fees paid are generally non-refundable unless stated in writing, and cancellation is by email request (Omada terms and support centre). That is a high-friction model to avoid.
- Calm, Insight Timer and MyFitnessPal let users turn off auto-renew in-app or via the app store; cancel must happen on the platform where you paid (their help centres).
- Paystack: refunds are reported as 5 to 10 business days for cards and 2 to 3 for transfers; gateway fees are not returned to the merchant (third-party guides, unverified). FCCPC guidance gives a refund or replacement right for defective goods within three months (Legal500); its fit for services is unverified.
- Tarragon should adapt: receipt page per order with line items, payment channel, Paystack reference and a downloadable PDF. History in one list. Refund and cancel rules written per item ("cancel before your care team starts: full refund; after: partial or none, here is how it is worked out"). Show refund status and the expected days. Refund goes to the original channel only.
(Sources: omadahealth.com/terms-of-use, support.omadahealth.com, support.calm.com and myfitnesspal help pages via search, zerrar.com Paystack refunds, legal500.com)

## 20.1 Catalogue of services and care packs

- Reliance structures a catalogue as named tiers with one-line benefit lists and per-period prices. Omada sells a single named programme, so no catalogue to learn from (unverified beyond its site).
- Tarragon should adapt: two kinds of row, a single service and a care pack that bundles services with quantities and an expiry. Each row has a plain name, a "who it is for" line, a price, what is included, what is not, and a version. Medicines are not part of a pack (founder decision of 2026-10-05).
(Sources: getreliancehealth.com benefits page, omadahealth.com)

## 20.2 Subscriptions built but off

- The 2026-10-05 membership pivot (Free plus a 100k naira yearly Membership) is in project memory; no public precedent needed.
- Industry lesson: Noom's roughly 62 million dollar class settlement stemmed from trials that rolled into paid renewals that were hard to cancel (Fox Business, Mondaq, Winston). The FTC's click-to-cancel rule has been revived in 2026 (Jones Day), US only.
- Tarragon should adapt: build the recurring plumbing (plan, billing period, entitlement dates, renewal event) behind a feature flag with no live plan rows, so turning it on is a data decision. A flag-off path must return a clear "not available", never an empty page. Add a test that no subscription row can be created while the flag is off.
(Sources: foxbusiness.com Noom, mondaq.com Noom, jonesday.com 2026-05 click-to-cancel)

## 20.3 Care packs never auto-renew, plus renewal reminder

- Noom agreed to send reminder emails before trials end and to ease cancellation (Fox Business). Calm and MyFitnessPal default to auto-renew with an off switch (help centres), which is the model not to copy.
- Tarragon should adapt: a pack ends on its date and stays ended. Reminder at 14 and 3 days before expiry through in-app, then push or email (never depends on one send succeeding). The reminder offers "buy again" as a fresh order the person confirms, with the current price shown, so a price change is never a surprise. Unused items stay usable until expiry; say so.
(Sources: foxbusiness.com Noom, calm.com and support.myfitnesspal.com cancel pages)

## 20.6 Plain-language "what is included" page

- Reliance lists benefits per plan in short bullets (benefits page). Omada's FAQ is long form; both are readable but neither pairs "included" with "not included" (observation, unverified as a policy).
- Tarragon should adapt: generated from the same catalogue row as checkout, so the page cannot disagree with what is sold. Four blocks: what you get, what you do not get, what you pay extra for (medicines, lab fees paid elsewhere), how to cancel or get a refund. Reading level aimed at someone on a phone with patchy data; no medical claims beyond what the care team does.
(Sources: getreliancehealth.com/nigeria/benefits, omadahealth.com/frequently-asked-questions)

## Webhook safety

- Paystack signs each event with an x-paystack-signature header: HMAC SHA512 of the raw body with your secret key. Without a 200, live-mode events retry every 3 minutes for four tries, then hourly for up to 72 hours (Paystack webhooks docs via search summary; confirm on the official page).
- Replay and duplicates: retries mean the same event arrives more than once. Store the event id or reference with a unique constraint and make fulfilment idempotent.
- Out of order: a "charge.success" can precede or follow other events. Never rely on arrival order; derive state from a verify call and only ever move an order forward.
- Amount mismatch: after verify, compare amount in kobo, currency and reference with the stored order. On mismatch, do not fulfil; flag for staff.
- Verify-before-fulfil: signature check, then call the verify endpoint, then fulfil in one database transaction, then return 200 quickly.
- Abandoned checkouts: orders stay "awaiting payment" and expire after a set time; a sweeper re-verifies before expiring, because transfer and USSD payments can land late.
- Double-tap: unique order reference per attempt plus a DB lock on the order, so two taps or two tabs yield one order; a second successful charge on an already paid order is queued for refund and flagged.
(Sources: paystack.com/docs/payments/webhooks, hookdeck.com Paystack guide, mctaba.com webhooks engineering guide)

## Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria

- Dark patterns: pre-ticked renewals, trial-to-paid rollovers, hidden or email-only cancellation. Noom and Chegg-type actions show the cost (Mondaq, FTC business blog on Chegg). Do not use any of them.
- Stored value or wallets: holding prepaid balances can raise CBN licensing questions (unverified, needs counsel) and was already removed on 2026-09-30. Use pay-per-order and vouchers tied to named items only.
- FX, GBP or USD pricing: a derived USD price at a moving rate invites disputes and naira swings; show naira only. Fees on international cards are higher and uncapped (afrotools), so say so before payment rather than surprise at the bank.
- Unlicensed health claims: no "cure", "guaranteed result" or outcome promises in catalogue copy; NAFDAC and advertising-code limits on health claims are unverified here and need a legal check.
- Fee surprise: never add a gateway or service fee after the total is shown; if fees are passed on, show them as a line before payment.
- "Free" claims: only call something free if no payment is ever needed for it; otherwise say what is free and what is paid. FCCPC has said it will sanction price and refund breaches (PM News).
(Sources: mondaq.com, ftc.gov Chegg blog, afrotools.com, pmnewsnigeria.com)
