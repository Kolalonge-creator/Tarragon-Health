# S79 research: Module 24 Institution console

Basis: public product pages and help centre knowledge of the named platforms, written from memory of their public material. No live browsing was done in this build session; nothing is copied. Claims about what the platforms do should be re-checked before they are quoted outside Tarragon.

## 24.1 Cohorts, enrolment codes, approved bulk upload (Omada, Personify, Wellhub)
- Best products take an eligibility file from HR (Wellhub-style: one row per person, a stable employee reference, add, remove or full-replace modes), validate it, show a preview, and only then apply it. A "full replace" file means anyone absent is a leaver. Leavers lose the sponsored benefit but keep their own account.
- Complaints: a bad file silently ends coverage for hundreds of people; error messages name no row; nobody can tell what a re-upload will change before it lands.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: payroll exports arrive as pasted CSV or photographed sheets, phone numbers in local format, shared inboxes; many people have no work email. Codes and QR sheets on a noticeboard work better than email invites.
- Tarragon version: stage, validate row by row, show the plan (insert, update, end, no change), a downloadable row-level error report, approval before commit (a second person, or Tarragon, when a file would end a large share of the roster), and leavers end sponsorship only, never the person's own record. One enrolment-code entry that checks employer and programme codes together.

## 24.2 Programme configuration (Omada, Personify)
- Best products let the buyer choose pathways, challenges, rewards and sponsored services from a catalogue and show what each costs. Complaint: the buyer can promise services the operator has not priced or cannot deliver.
- Unsuitable: rewards pools that hold a balance create a stored-value and licensing problem (INV-09); employer visibility of who earned what breaks aggregate-only access (I9).
- Tarragon version: one screen reads what already exists (programmes, campaigns, sponsored items) and records a versioned request that Tarragon approves. A rewards budget is a requested figure only; the pool itself waits (OQ-S58-06 design rules: never a balance, never an individual, per-person cap).

## 24.3 Aggregate reporting and the privacy ledger (Omada; Nigeria-specific)
- Best products publish engagement and outcome figures with a minimum group size. Complaint: tiles silently vanish, so the buyer thinks the product is broken, or small groups are shown and a colleague is identifiable.
- Tarragon version: a sponsor-visible privacy ledger that lists each withheld tile, in which month, and the plain reason (group too small, small category, month held back to stop subtraction). The ledger holds reasons and never a count below the minimum.

## 24.4 Invoices, contracts, usage billing (Nigeria-specific)
- Designed from the requirement. Nigerian buyers pay by bank transfer against a purchase order and want an itemised monthly statement tied to the invoice number. Per-person lines would identify people, so usage is aggregate per service per month and small lines fold into "other services".
- Tarragon version: append-only usage records (integer kobo, no person column), a frozen monthly statement, linked to the existing employer invoice by finance.

## 24.5 Workplace campaigns and challenges (Headspace, Calm)
- Already built (prevention and employer campaigns, S69 cohort challenges). Gap was only that the buyer could not see all of it in one place; the configuration screen closes that. No mood or mental health data reaches a sponsor (S75-9).

## 24.6 Procurement and renewal exports (Omada)
- Best products give procurement a pack: engagement, outcomes, usage, contract terms and a methods note, as PDF and spreadsheet. Complaint: figures with no definitions get rejected by procurement committees.
- Tarragon version: one pack as CSV or PDF built only from frozen, suppressed figures plus usage and contract terms, with a methodology note, every export audited before the file is given.
