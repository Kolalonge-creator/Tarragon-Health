# What the CMO is asked to confirm in the BP care course (S33, OQ-242)

The lessons are drafts written from the production-list briefs and the WHO HEARTS themes. No clinician has read them. A lesson reaches a patient only after the CMO moves it to published and sets a review date. For each lesson, confirm or change the claims below, in English first; Pidgin follows only for the lessons marked written.

| Lesson | Claims to confirm | Pidgin |
|---|---|---|
| BPC-01 What blood pressure is | The plain description of the two numbers; the list of what high pressure can harm over time; the line that readings, medicines, salt and activity all help | written, needs native review |
| BPC-02 Measuring at home | Five minutes of rest; no talking, phone, walk, meal, cigarette, tea or coffee beforehand; back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level, bare arm; cuff size effect (small reads high, large can read low); two readings about a minute apart, same arm; log both, do not pick one; logging a pharmacy reading with where it was taken; stop and use emergency guidance if very unwell | held |
| BPC-03 Numbers and target | Grouped as normal, raised and high with no figures; the target is the care team's own and may differ between people; one reading is not the whole story; follow the care plan when it says to act | held |
| BPC-04 Feeling fine | Symptoms are an unreliable guide; do not stop or change tablets because you feel well; talk to the care team first; keep reading when well | held |
| BPC-05 Your medicines | The three plain descriptions of what tablets do; many people take two or three kinds; take at the same time daily; follow the care team's missed-dose instruction and never double up unless told; ask for refills early; never share tablets; tell the care team about every product including traditional ones | held |
| BPC-06 Side effects | Ankle swelling, a dry cough, tiredness, dizziness on standing, passing urine more often as common; get up slowly; the emergency list (face, lips or tongue swelling, trouble breathing, a spreading rash, fainting); never stop on your own | held |
| BPC-07 Salt | The local sources named (stock cubes, seasoning powders, instant noodles, smoked or processed meat, salted fish, suya spice, bread); cut down step by step; the swaps; the kidney and heart failure caution | written |
| BPC-08 Eating | Half a plate of vegetables; fruit; beans, moi moi, akara with less salt; fresh over smoked or salted; fist-sized portions; less oil and sugar; the kidney and special-diet caution (the lesson names no potassium figure and no salt substitute) | written |
| BPC-09 Moving more | About thirty minutes of moderate activity on most days, in pieces if needed; start with five or ten minutes; stop if dizzy or very breathless; emergency guidance for chest pain, severe breathlessness or fainting; ask the care team first with a heart condition | written |
| BPC-10 Alcohol and tobacco | Alcohol raises pressure and builds with regular drinking; drinking less is better; smoking and smokeless tobacco both raise it and are not a safe switch; first step is a small cut; tell the care team | written |
| BPC-11 Stress and sleep | Stress and poor sleep raise pressure; loud snoring with pauses should be told to the care team; the breathing exercise is a calm moment and does not replace tablets or readings; sleep habits | written |
| BPC-12 Herbal remedies | Herbal mixtures are not a replacement for tablets; some interact with medicines or harm the kidneys or liver; strength varies; tell the care team about everything; cooking with garlic or ginger is fine; restart safely if tablets were stopped | held |
| BPC-13 Warning signs | The list (very bad unusual headache, chest pain or heaviness, trouble breathing, weakness or numbness, trouble speaking or confusion, sudden sight problems); "call one one two or go to the nearest hospital emergency department"; do not wait, do not re-measure, do not try a remedy first; do not drive yourself if faint, confused or weak | held |
| BPC-14 Staying in control | What the first months and the following years look like; yearly checks for kidneys, heart and eyes; restart the next day after a slip | written |
| BRE-01 Three minute calm | The pace (four in, six out, three minutes), the gentler and one minute variants, the stop list, the "ask your care team first" list, the line to keep taking medicines | held |

Also decide: the review date to set when publishing a lesson (the build suggests twelve months, six for BPC-05, 06 and 12), and whether the reviewer credit shows the CMO's name (it is shown only from a complete review record).
