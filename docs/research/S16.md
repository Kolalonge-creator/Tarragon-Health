# S16 Research: clinician work queues

Web research on public documents only (2026-10-06). Most vendors do not publish queue internals, so "not published" is an unknown, not a confirmed gap. Doctolib, Validic, Withings, Health Recovery Solutions, Biofourmis and Cerner specifics were not covered. The lease and hand-back design is our own synthesis, not a documented industry standard.

## Pooled inbox and ownership (Epic In Basket, Cerner)
- One message to a pool; anyone eligible acts and completion clears everyone's inbox. A named owner is meant to escalate if nobody acts. Escalation depends on a person, not a timer. Message volume is linked to clinician burnout (AMA STEPS Forward).
- Adopt: one task per pool, completion removes it everywhere. Avoid: a human as the only safety net. We back ownership with timers.

## Priority classes (Manchester Triage System)
- Five categories with target times to FIRST assessment (0, 10, 30, 90, 120 minutes). It is time to first assessment, not to resolution.
- Adopt: classes with target times held as data. Avoid: reading a target as a resolution deadline.

## Monitoring alerts to tasks (Cadence, Omada, Glooko)
- Cadence publishes a funnel (per 10,000 patients about 5,000 alert encounters a month, under 200 physician escalations, about 40 ED referrals). Omada and Cadence are salaried nurse or coach models. Glooko ranks patients by risk, not tasks; ownership and hand-back are not published.
- Evidence: 74 to 99 percent of monitoring alarms are non-actionable; alert fatigue lowers escalation. Mitigations: merge duplicates, suppression windows, timed escalation. Adopt dedup with a recorded merge count and timed escalation. Avoid silent snooze and silent drops.

## Marketplaces and async queues (Wheel, OpenLoop, Hims and Hers, Ro, Teladoc, Amwell)
- Routing by language, specialty and licence, under 24 hour review. Claim timeouts and decline rules are not published. Not chronic monitoring.
- Gap: a lease-based claim with automatic expiry, freelance pull beside employed push, hand-back with a reason, escalation when nobody claims.

## Lessons (Babylon, Clafiya)
- Babylon overclaimed validated triage and graded the same case differently on different runs. Adopt deterministic classes stored with the version that produced them, plus a clinician override that is audited.
- Clafiya's USSD request path suggests a low-data fallback. Our paging fallback is email, not SMS (INV-08, OQ-S16-3).

## Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria
- Power cuts and poor data: writes must be idempotent so a retry never double-claims.
- Resident doctors report 24 to 72 hour on-call stretches and a new 48 hour weekly cap with post-call rest: do not assume a clinician is always reachable; the working-hours window belongs to S17 and S18.
- NDPA: sending patient data through a foreign paging vendor needs a transfer basis. Tasks carry ids only.
