# S53 research: Module 8, medicines (schedule and adherence, rows 8.1 to 8.8)

Date 2026-10-07. Sources: the S08 research (`docs/research/S08.md`, already covers reminder behaviour and the adherence formula), the two competitor reviews of 2026-10 (`docs/research/competitor-review-*.md`), the S51-S55 plan (`docs/research/S51-S55-review-and-build-plan.md`), public product pages as known to the author, and one live web search for the NAFDAC service (the only source re-checked in this session, see 8.8). Product behaviour below is vendor or press description, not hands-on testing. Reference platforms were studied for behaviour only; nothing is copied.

**8.1 Add by search, pack or prescription photo, or from a Tarragon prescription.** Best products (Medisafe, MyTherapy): search-as-you-type over a drug database, a barcode or pack scan, and import from a pharmacy. Complaints: scans that fill the wrong strength silently, stale databases, free tiers capped at two medicines (Medisafe moved to a paid tier in 2026). Unsafe or unsuitable here: a scan that saves on its own, any claim that a pack is genuine, a UPC barcode (Nigerian packs rarely carry a scannable national code), photo upload on a slow connection. Tarragon version: search from the catalogue, a pack-photo reader that only fills the form and a tick box "I have checked these details against my pack" before anything saves, a Tarragon prescription added directly (already built, signed, INV-02), manual typing always possible. A prescription photo is not read (no OCR); only the existing document link is kept as a reference for specialist-started medicines.

**8.2 Nigerian medicine database.** Medisafe uses licensed Western data; Nigerian brand coverage is thin. Nothing licensed exists for Nigeria that the founder has supplied. Risks: inventing NAFDAC numbers (a false assurance about a counterfeit is worse than none), brands that look official in the list. Tarragon version: `medicine_catalogue` with a short, curated seed of generic (INN) names and strengths plus only brands whose generic is beyond doubt, every row `is_verified = false`, `nafdac_number` null and flagged for a pharmacist, the screen saying so in plain words. Growing it needs a licensed or pharmacist-copied source (open question OQ-292).

**8.3 Confirm name, strength, dose and schedule; complex schedules.** Medisafe supports several daily times, intervals and tapers; confirmation screens are the norm. Complaint: clinician-set schedules edited by patients. Tarragon already enforces that a prescribed dose and structured schedule cannot be changed by the patient (S08). S53 adds nothing here beyond the catalogue and photo prefill feeding the same confirm step.

**8.4 Reminders, USSD confirmation, taken/skipped/delayed.** Best products persist until confirmed; complaints are duplicate reminders and a phone maker killing background apps (S08 research). USSD is parked (founder decision D3). S53 closes the wording questions: S13 already rewrote every server template that named a medicine (OQ-71, checked live 2026-10-07: no active template body or subject carries a drug placeholder), the dose and refill reminders are generic, and the two-reminder question (OQ-69) stays as recommended (server copy is the backup for a killed local reminder). New in S53: a Medfriend-style in-app notice to a supporter the patient already consented to (profile access with `view_medication` and `receive_alerts` named), one a day, generic wording, never required for anything to work. Medfriend (Medisafe) notifies a family member of a missed dose; the risk here is shaming or leaking a condition, so the text names no medicine and no reading.

**8.5 Weekly adherence to the user, the clinician and the Care Circle.** S08 built one formula and the audited clinician read; S29 (Care Circle) already shows the supporter their weekly "doses marked taken" line when the patient granted `adherence_summary`. S53 verified rather than rebuilt this. Not a clinical proportion of days covered; no dispensing data exists. The 80 percent line still waits for the Chief Medical Officer (OQ-75).

**8.6 Missed doses and silence as signals, never automatic treatment changes.** Tarragon decision. Signals (`dose.recorded`, `dose.missed`, `refill.due`, `adherence.low`) are emitted into `clinical_rule_events` since S08 and nothing acts on them to change a medicine. Wiring into the triage engine's adherence and silence triggers needs a care-pack patient state that does not exist yet (OQ-89, S26); S53 does not duplicate that work and does not add an LLM or any automatic action.

**8.7 Interaction and duplication warnings; side-effect notes for the next consultation.** Medisafe shows interaction warnings from a licensed US database and warns that a stale database is a safety risk. MyTherapy is lighter. Complaints: alarmist warnings, alert fatigue, patients stopping a medicine on seeing a red banner. Unsafe here: unsigned clinical content shown to patients, clinician wording ("stop one of them") shown to patients, any hint that no warning means safe. Tarragon version: the curated rule list (already in the clinician panel) moved into `@tarragon/medicines` so web and phone run the same offline check on every add; patient wording is four fixed, reviewed sentences that always say "your care team" and never tell anyone to stop a prescribed medicine; the whole thing is behind the go-live guard `interaction_check_enabled`, off until a human signs the dataset (D6); a side-effect note per medicine, in the person's words, read by the tied care team through an audited call.

**8.8 NAFDAC Mobile Authentication Service prompt.** NAFDAC's MAS (launched 2010, extended 2012) puts a scratch panel on a pack hiding a code; the buyer texts it free to a short code and gets a reply in about a minute. Public pages (a 2010 safemedicines.org article, an outsourcing-pharma article of the same year, a Daily Trust piece and Dubawa's "four ways NAFDAC wants you to verify") agree on the short code 38353 and the scratch panel. Coverage was reported for antimalarials and antibiotics at launch and later for antidiabetic and antihypertensive products; not every pack has a panel. The 2026 state of the service was not verified from a NAFDAC page in this session, so the copy is hedged ("if your pack has a scratch panel", "a reply is NAFDAC's, not ours") and the short code is logged for a human check before store publication (OQ-294). The prompt appears when a medicine is added and when a collection is logged; the pack reader never judges authenticity.
