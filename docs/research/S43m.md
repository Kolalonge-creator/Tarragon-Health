# S43m research note: personal health records on a phone

Written from public product descriptions and the existing `docs/research/S43.md`; nothing here was tested on a device or against a live competitor account. Treat each row as "what the product is known to offer", not a benchmark.

| | Eka Care (India) | Apple Health Records (US, UK) | Samsung Health (and Health Connect on Android) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Photo or PDF upload of reports and prescriptions, read into structured values; sharing by link or QR | Connect a hospital's patient portal; data arrives already structured | Values come from sensors and apps; lab and records support is thin and region-dependent |
| Trends | Per-test charts across years | Charts by category, with the lab's own flags | Trends for sensor data (sleep, steps, heart rate); not lab history |
| Sharing | Time-limited link, doctor view | Export or share a summary; no expiring recipient link | Share through the OS; no revocable clinician link |
| Emergency | Emergency profile | Medical ID on the lock screen | Emergency info on the lock screen |
| Offline | Cached documents | Fully on-device, synced | On-device |
| Fit for Nigeria | Closest: photo-first, Indian lab formats, shared phones, patchy data. Reading quality on Nigerian lab layouts is unknown | Needs a portal connection; almost no Nigerian facility has one. The on-device privacy model is the right idea | Hardware and OS dependent; health records are not the product |

## What we take
1. **Photo first, with a person in the loop.** Eka's pattern (photograph, read, confirm) is the realistic way to get Nigerian paper into a record. We add what a clinical product must: every value is a suggestion until ticked, a rejected reading leaves nothing, and the original photo is always kept.
2. **Charts that say how, not what.** Apple shows the lab's own flag; we show only the lab's range and the care team's target and never say what a result means.
3. **Everything readable offline.** Apple keeps the record on the device. We keep a last good copy in the encrypted store and say plainly when a screen is showing it.
4. **A lock-screen emergency card, opt in.** Apple's Medical ID is on by default for emergency access; on a shared phone that is the wrong default, so ours is opt in with the shared-phone warning (S09x).

## What is unsuitable for Nigeria, and what we do instead
- **Portal connections as the main source** (Apple): no facility network to connect to. Photos, partner-lab push and a consented FHIR route (S44) instead.
- **Cloud-only reading** (Eka-style): data cost and privacy. Reading is a registered AI system behind a guard that is off; until it is on, the photo is kept and details are typed. No reading is sent without the person pressing the button.
- **Large image uploads**: the phone resizes to 1600 px and JPEG 0.7 before upload.
- **Default sharing and default lock-screen exposure**: shared phones and family handsets are common. Links expire, can carry a PIN, can be opened once, and the person sees who opened them (S09x).
- **Per-device assumptions** (a wearable or a recent iPhone): none of these screens needs one.
- **English-only**, by founder decision: short sentences, no jargon, "your care team".
