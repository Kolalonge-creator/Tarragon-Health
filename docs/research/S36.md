# S36 research: console operations, clinical lead and admin areas

Reference platforms (spec line 75): Healthily, Infermedica, Practo, Vezeeta, Commure. Read 2026-10-06 from public sources only (help centres, developer docs, terms, blogs, GitHub). Each claim is marked with its source; THIN means a search snippet or marketing page is the only support. Lenses: functionality, design (staff and ops tooling as far as publicly documented) and code or architecture (what is public). Nothing is copied: the last section of each part says what Tarragon builds in its own way.

## What the review found, in five lines

1. **Code and design are mostly not public.** None of the five publishes an ops console, an admin screen set or marketplace code. The public material is functional (Infermedica's content process and release notes, Practo's profile and verification rules, Epic's break-the-glass pattern). Where a lens has nothing public, the parts below say so rather than guess.
2. **Infermedica's change control is the best published model** (a staged content process, regression testing, dated releases with counts of what was added, removed and updated). Its vendor-released, probabilistic content cannot be copied: Tarragon's triage is deterministic and the CMO signs every rule set (INV-01, INV-16).
3. **Nobody publishes per-item review dates, per-language translation status, or a re-verification schedule.** Tarragon can lead there.
4. **Support access to a patient record is where the invariants bite.** Epic's break-the-glass (reason plus audit) is the right shape; Zendesk-style "act as the user" blurs who acted and is rejected. Tarragon already has a read-only, consent-bound view-as; S36a adds the reason-gated lookup in front of it.
5. **Payout approval, role grants and access reviews are documented only as controls Tarragon should have** (maker-checker, expiry on grants, periodic review). They are built here only where the data exists; payout approval waits for S31.

# S36 Part A: Healthily and Infermedica (research only)

Method and limits: standard web search plus page fetches, 2026-10-06. THIN = only a search snippet or a marketing page supports it. Neither vendor publishes its staff-facing editing tool screens beyond a short description, so the DESIGN lens is mostly inference (marked inf). Healthily (now healthily.ai) has very little public engineering or governance detail; Infermedica has a lot. Format follows S35.md.

## Infermedica

### FUNCTIONALITY
- Change control: a published 10-stage medical content process: scope by data scientists, evidence entry with sources, peer review by a second medical editor, clinical case assembly, expert panel review (issues loop back to peer review), technical review, automated regression against all clinical cases, manual doctor testing, translation, deployment. Source: https://developer.infermedica.com/docs/content
- Versioning: every change carries history, timestamp and author; models are named by release date (for example model-2026-09-16). Source: same page, and https://help.infermedica.com/changelog-intake (the intake changelog; the model changelog summary came from https://developer.infermedica.com/docs/changelog).
- Review dates: no per-item "next review" date found. Cadence is release based, roughly monthly to quarterly, with themed review rounds (trauma, paediatrics, gynaecology, mental health). Source: changelog page.
- Translation status: 19 languages, native speakers with medical education, quoted at 120 to 160 hours per release. No per-item translation status shown publicly. Source: content page.
- Sign-off: expert panel specialist signs content; implementers of the Platform API must pass a post-implementation audit and display UDI, manufacturer and instructions from the `/info` endpoint. Source: https://developer.infermedica.com/documentation/platform-api/mdr/
- Safety reporting: the MDR page does not describe incident or vigilance reporting (it says to contact the team). Treated as not public.

### DESIGN (staff tooling)
- "Metabase" is described as a web platform built by doctors and UX staff for collaborative modelling, with change tracking, comments, issue resolution and peer review. THIN (search snippet; dblp record and secondary pages). Source: https://dblp.l3s.de/d2r/resource/publications/conf/mie/AhlbrandtBMNMR15
- Inference: a status per item (draft, in review, returned) is implied by "status tracking" on the content page. No screenshots found.

### CODE / ARCHITECTURE
- Public: developer portal with Engine API and Platform API, versioned docs (v3), an official Python client (https://github.com/infermedica/python-api), a public changelog with added/removed/updated counts and links to browse each change, public test cases and accuracy results (content page).
- Regulatory: MDR Class IIb, ISO 13485, ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type 2 stated on the vendor site. THIN for each (search snippet): https://infermedica.com/blog/articles/infermedica-achieves-iso-13485-certification
- Stated accuracy figure (96% conditions, Oct 2025) is the vendor's own number.

### Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria
- The knowledge base is built from Western literature and prevalence data; priors for malaria, typhoid, sickle cell and the like are not the same. Using it unmodified in Nigeria would under-weight local disease (inf).
- Probabilistic condition ranking invites false certainty; Tarragon forbids a model in the triage path (INV-01).
- A vendor-owned release cycle means a content change can land without our clinician sign-off. Unacceptable under our sign-off rules.
- Languages: 19 languages, but nothing public on Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo or Pidgin (THIN, inferred from the lack of mention).

### What Tarragon should build (original)
1. A change record per rule set: author, reviewer, second reviewer, evidence links, date, status. Nothing goes live without the CMO sign-off already in the hub.
2. Date-stamped release names with a plain added/removed/changed list shown to the signing clinician before they sign.
3. A regression set of canned cases that must pass before a release can be signed; a failing case blocks the Sign button.
4. A "what changed since the last signed version" diff view, built on versioned config, not a free-text note.
5. A named review owner and a next-review date per rule set, with an overdue list (Infermedica does not publish this; a gap we can fill).

## Healthily

### FUNCTIONALITY
- Change control: no public description of the content workflow was found. THIN. The only governance claims are the Patient Information Forum TICK mark (production process) and ORCHA review (Level 4, 76%). Source: https://www.healthily.ai/post/understanding-class-i
- Versioning: nothing public found.
- Review dates: nothing public found for Healthily articles. A search for "medically reviewed / next review" returned no Healthily page. Do not assume these exist.
- Translation status: not found.
- Sign-off: states an audited quality management system and adherence to ISO 14971, ISO 13485 and DCB0129 (healthily.ai page above). THIN, vendor marketing.
- Safety reporting: the page does not detail post-market surveillance or incident handling.

### DESIGN (staff tooling)
- Nothing public. Not inferable. Marked: no evidence.

### CODE / ARCHITECTURE
- Regulatory: described as a Class I medical device (MHRA), with an independent evaluation by Imperial College London's Self Care Research Unit with the RCGP reporting 97% safe and 72% condition accuracy. The page predates the UK and EU rule changes, so check the current status before citing. Source: https://www.healthily.ai/post/understanding-class-i
- Whitepaper: https://assets.ctfassets.net/iqo3fk8od6t9/08pX2xBSPIjHltvLkv6YG/c3f139de4d6777d4af934c53b8dae204/Healthily-AI-whitepaper_The-chatbot-will-see-you-now.pdf (found in a search, not read in full; THIN).
- No public GitHub organisation, OpenAPI document or engineering blog was found in this pass.
- My two direct fetches of healthily.com clinical-safety and livehealthily.com returned 404.

### Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria
- A Class I (self-declared, lower risk) status is a weaker assurance than a notified-body route; an evaluation showing 72% condition accuracy is not a basis for naming a likely diagnosis to a patient here.
- Consumer self-care framing assumes NHS-style onward care; in Nigeria the next step may be a pharmacy or a private clinic.
- Do not adopt its marketing claims of safety as evidence; they are vendor claims.

### What Tarragon should build (original)
1. A per-article record with reviewer name, review date, next review date and a visible "reviewed" stamp, null-gated like `ReviewedByDoctor` (nothing shown if there is no record).
2. A per-language translation status (draft, translated, clinician-checked) with the reviewer and date, and a patient-facing fallback to English labelled as such.
3. An overdue-review queue for the Medical Content owner, sorted by risk class of the content.

## Governance, incident and review (both vendors)
- Neither publishes a safety incident form, reporting timelines or a public post-market report. Treat "how they handle incidents" as unknown.
- Tarragon should keep: one-click safety concern in the shell (S35), an incident record linked to the rule set version that produced the output, and a quarterly governance review recorded as data.

## Five-line summary
1. Infermedica publishes a 10-stage content workflow, date-named model releases with added/removed/updated counts, and an audit duty for implementers.
2. Healthily publishes almost nothing on content change control, review dates, translations or staff tooling; its claims are marketing-level (THIN).
3. Neither shows a public "next review" date or an incident-reporting path; Tarragon can lead there.
4. Infermedica's probabilistic, vendor-released knowledge base conflicts with our clinician sign-off and no-model-in-triage rules.
5. Build: per-item review owner and dates, translation status per language, regression set blocking sign, a diff before sign-off.

# S36 part B: Practo and Vezeeta, operations review (2026-10-06)

Method: web search plus a few page fetches. Many primary pages returned 403/404 (Vezeeta terms, Practo provider terms), so most claims are THIN (snippet only). Nothing here is copied; nothing was invented. Extends S15 (credentialing), S21 (booking), S35 (console); those findings are not repeated. Not legal advice.

## Practo (India)

### Verification and onboarding
- FUNCTIONALITY: medical registration number is mandatory for doctors (allied health excepted); the registration is checked against state medical councils. Degrees, institute and year are required; ID proof is optional. Specialty claims are checked against qualifications by a medical team using an internal rulebook. Awards and new associations are reviewed before going live. Source: https://help.practo.com/practo-profile/guidelines-for-creating-profiles/ (fetched, not THIN).
- DESIGN: one "Practo Profile" is the single source of truth for doctor and practice, with most edits live in about 24 hours and payment-mode and email edits immediate (same page). A practice named after a doctor needs proof of tie (certificate, prescription or invoice photo).
- CODE/ARCHITECTURE: no public engineering post on verification or search was found (THIN, absence only). Public GitHub holds infrastructure (Kubernetes queue-worker autoscaler, a change-data-capture pipeline with data masking, a design system), not marketplace logic: https://github.com/practo. Useful signal only: masking before analytics, queue-based workers.

### Profile freshness and re-verification
- FUNCTIONALITY: the help page says nothing about periodic re-verification or "last verified" dates; it covers only update turnaround. Suspension rules are not stated there. I found no public evidence of a freshness alert. Treat as an absent feature, not a hidden one (THIN).

### Reviews
- FUNCTIONALITY: Practo dropped "most booked" badges, citing weak usefulness and that direct phone connections broke booking counts, and moved to recommendations and patient stories from verified patients: https://blog.practo.com/no-more-doctor-badges/ (fetched). The page does not describe verification or ranking mechanics.
- THIN: a search summary says only verified patients can review and removal requests need substantial documentation; Trustpilot complaints allege genuine reviews removed and "paid" five-star reviews. Treat the complaints as unproven allegations, but they show the trust risk.

### Support, refunds, payouts, commission, roles
- FUNCTIONALITY: Practo Ray (practice software) bundles scheduling, records, billing, reminders; reported at roughly INR 999 to 1,999 a month (THIN, aggregator snippet: https://www.techjockey.com/detail/practo-ray). Listing is described as free to the doctor (THIN). Refund complaints exist on Trustpilot (THIN). No public information on settlement cycles, commission tables or admin roles was found.
- Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria: paid subscription tiers that raise discoverability turn the directory into pay-for-placement; opaque review takedown; a registration check that rests on council directories (Nigeria has no queryable MDCN register, per S15); badges based on booking counts reward volume, not safety.
- What Tarragon should build: (1) a per-credential "last verified" date and next-due date on every provider record, with a queue of due-in-30/14/0 days owned by a named reviewer; (2) an internal specialty-to-evidence rule (qualification to allowed service types) that gates what a clinician can be assigned, not what they advertise; (3) a public reason-coded log of review removals visible to the reviewer and the author; (4) no ranking inputs from money, ever.

## Vezeeta (Egypt, MENA)

### Verification and licence terms
- FUNCTIONALITY: the doctor terms make the provider warrant they are licensed and let Vezeeta terminate use if required information is not provided or updated, or the agreement is breached, at its discretion: https://www.vezeeta.com/en/Generic/TermsAndConditions (search snippet only, THIN; page fetch returned 403). That is a contractual right, not a published schedule.
- DESIGN: marketing claims "20,000+ verified doctors" (THIN, app-store aggregator: https://appfollow.io/ios/vezeeta-doctors-pharmacy/1010281314?country=us). No visible verification-date or expiry display was found.
- CODE/ARCHITECTURE: no public engineering blog, GitHub or API documentation found (searched; THIN absence).

### Reviews
- FUNCTIONALITY: ratings are described as coming only from patients who booked and visited through the platform, and the platform says it does not filter positive or negative reviews (THIN, same aggregator). Sound principle: review right tied to a completed booking.

### Booking fees, subscription, payouts, support
- FUNCTIONALITY: revenue is reported as a fee per confirmed appointment plus a SaaS subscription for practice management (THIN, third-party: https://www.apptunix.com/blog/build-an-app-like-vezeeta/ and https://dealroom.co/companies/vezeeta/). Payout cadence, commission percentages, refund and cancellation handling, support ticketing and admin roles were not publicly documented in anything I could open.
- DESIGN: Doctor-side product is a booking calendar with working hours that auto-confirms bookings and sends reminders (THIN, aggregator). Clinic-type selection is the first step of the site, which routes the right workflow per provider type: a pattern worth keeping for labs, pharmacies and clinics.
- Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria: per-booking fees collected from providers create a pull to steer patients to paid slots; auto-confirmation without clinician acceptance is unsafe for triage-sensitive care (INV-01 stays: no automation in the clinical decision); insurer-style bank discounts and promotions can distort choice; Egyptian licensing sits with a different regulator, so the "licensed warranty" model cannot substitute for evidence checks against MDCN, PCN (pharmacies, premises) and NHIA accreditation where relevant.
- What Tarragon should build: (1) separate onboarding paths and evidence lists per partner type: clinician (MDCN), pharmacy (PCN premises and superintendent pharmacist), lab (accreditation, contract), clinic (facility registration); (2) a suspension state machine with reason, owner, reviewer, effective date and a patient-facing "temporarily unavailable" fallback, never a silent delete; (3) a payout ledger per provider with explicit fee schedule versions and a paid-by-bank-transfer-only rule (no cash payouts); (4) a refund and cancellation inbox where the reason code, amount and approver are mandatory and the patient gets an in-app status (never relying on a send succeeding); (5) role-based admin permissions split into verifier, finance approver, support agent and CMO sign-off, with the person who edits fees unable to approve payouts.

## Cross-cutting findings
- Neither platform publicly documents re-verification cadence, freshness alerts, suspension procedure, payout schedules or admin RBAC. Tarragon cannot copy these; it must design them. S15 already fixes the credential grace and notice schedule (3 months, 1 month, day-of); extend that same engine to labs and pharmacies rather than building a second one.
- Trust failures to design against: review removal that looks one-sided, paid prominence, and booking-count badges.
- Suggested data model (original): provider_credential(kind, evidence_ref, verified_at, valid_to, reviewer, next_check_due), provider_status(active, flagged, suspended, with reason code and audit row), fee_schedule_version(effective_from, rate, approver), payout(provider, period, amount_kobo, approver_1, approver_2).

Fetched in full: Practo profile guidelines, Practo badges post, Practo GitHub. Everything else is a search snippet and marked THIN. Not reached: Practo terms (404), Vezeeta terms (403), any Practo or Vezeeta engineering post.

# S36 part C: Commure and support-operations practice

Method and limits: standard web searches plus search summaries only; no page fetches, no hands-on walkthroughs, no vendor logins. Claims marked THIN rest on a search snippet. Extends S35 (which covered clinician queues, claims and hand-back) and does not repeat it. No GitHub repository for Commure was found.

## Commure, three lenses
- FUNCTIONALITY. Commure sells revenue cycle automation, ambient AI documentation and "agents" from a call-centre front office to denials work, claiming over 85% of revenue-cycle work completed without a person and 60+ EHR integrations (commure.com; hospitalogy.com/articles/2025-10-22/inside-commure-the-ai-platform-powering-healthcare-transformation-today/). THIN: vendor claims, unaudited.
- DESIGN. No public ops-console screens were found. Inference only: the pitch is "work completed automatically, people handle exceptions".
- CODE/ARCHITECTURE. Its EHR FHIR API is an R4B server on US Core 6.1.0 with token registration done by contacting the vendor; the old open developer portal is reported gone and access is partner-only (docs.athelas.com/air_developer/fhir_api/commure_ehr_fhir_api; businesswire.com/news/home/20200206005289/en/Commure-Launches-FHIR-Standard-Based-Platform-for-Healthcare-Innovation). Takeaway: capability statement published, client registration gated.

## Routine task automation (reminders, partner chasing, reports)
Support suites automate by rule: a time-based condition ("hours until next SLA breach") fires an alert or escalation before a breach, because the default only shows deadlines to agents (eesel.ai/blog/zendesk-sla-breach-automations-and-alerts; THIN). Commure applies the same idea at much larger scale to claims work.
- Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria: a chaser that texts a partner or patient would break INV-08 (SMS only for verification); an AI agent deciding a clinical or payment outcome would break INV-01.
- What Tarragon should build: a deterministic partner-chasing list. Each open partner item (lab result late, pharmacy confirmation) carries a due time from versioned config; a sweep marks it overdue, raises it on the ops work list, and sends only in-app, push or email. A human closes it. Scheduled report generation writes an aggregate-only file with a run record (who or what ran it, inputs, row counts).

## Support inbox across app, email and phone
Zendesk SLAs depend on business-hours schedules, and breaches are computed against the schedule on the ticket (getmacha.com/blog/zendesk-sla-explained; THIN). Intercom (renamed Fin in May 2026) offers HIPAA support and SLAs only on its top plan (search result, THIN). Takeaway: healthcare-grade controls sit on the most expensive tier, so a self-built inbox is justified.
- Unsafe or unsuitable: third-party helpdesks hold message bodies and attachments outside Nigeria; under NDPA 2023 that is a cross-border transfer needing a lawful basis. Pasting clinical detail into ticket text is the risk.
- What Tarragon should build: one ticket model across in-app, email and phone notes, with a category, a priority class, a due time from config, and a "contains health information" flag that restricts who may open it. Phone calls become typed notes, not recordings by default.

## Admin patient search, reason-to-open, break-glass
Epic Break the Glass flags a record so anyone outside the care team must enter a reason; the event is audited and a message goes to compliance (utsouthwestern.edu/sites/campus-news/archive/year-2020/break-the-glass.html; texaschildrensnews.org/compliance-and-privacy-spotlight-break-the-glass-epic/). Proactive monitoring tools then scan access patterns for misuse (UConn FairWarning FAQ, THIN). Zendesk lets an admin or agent with profile-edit permission "assume" an end user, with actions attributed to the user, and a security audit event records enabling it (support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408894200474; docs.datadoghq.com/security/default_rules/zendesk-account-assumption-settings; THIN). The gap: attribution to the impersonated person blurs who acted.
- Unsafe or unsuitable: Zendesk-style assumption with write rights; an admin search that returns names or values before a reason is given; reason as free text only, which becomes "ok".
- What Tarragon should build: search returns a match count and masked initials until a reason code (fixed list, "other" needs words) is chosen and the open is logged. "View as" is read-only, shows a banner, needs patient consent or a superadmin break-glass reason, is time-limited, and every action is attributed to the staff member, never the patient. A weekly break-glass review list sorts by volume per person, off-hours opens and repeat patients.

## Payout approval and four-eyes
Maker-checker means one person prepares and another approves; payouts sit in a queue until a second authorised user approves (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maker-checker; support.coingate.com 4-Eye approval article; THIN).
- Unsafe or unsuitable: any queue that holds a patient balance or credit breaks INV-09 (no stored balances). Partner and doctor payouts are Tarragon's own payables, not patient funds.
- What Tarragon should build: a payout batch with a maker, a different checker enforced by the database, a locked amount after approval, and a rejection reason. Approval of a batch above a configured threshold needs two checkers. Nobody approves their own batch, tested with a sabotage case.

## Role grants and access reviews
Epic and Zendesk both separate who may see from who may act; periodic reviews are a compliance habit, not a product feature (inference).
- What Tarragon should build: role grants as requests with a reason and an expiry; a quarterly access review list showing each staff member, role, last use and who granted it, signed off by a second person. Revoking is one click and logged. Never let an admin grant themselves a role.

## Configuration change control
No vendor documents this publicly in the sources found. Commure's FHIR registration being gated by the vendor is the nearest sign (THIN).
- What Tarragon should build: SLA, priority and routing config as versioned rows with draft, proposer, approver, effective date and a diff view. Clinical config needs the CMO signature. Rollback creates a new version, never an edit.

## Incident lists, post-incident review, reliability dashboards
Google SRE practice: blameless postmortems focused on contributing causes and process, not people (sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/; THIN for summary).
- Unsafe or unsuitable: a status page or incident note that names a patient; AI-written root causes accepted unreviewed.
- What Tarragon should build: an incident list with severity, owner, timeline and a review form (what happened, what detected it, what we change) that must close with at least one tracked action. A reliability dashboard shows notification delivery, queue ages against SLA, and failed jobs, all aggregate, with no feature that depends on one send succeeding.

## Summary of originality
Reason-coded search, consent-bound read-only view-as, database-enforced maker-checker for payables, and versioned SLA config with signatures were not found documented by any vendor reviewed. Treat as Tarragon's own designs.
