# S84 research: AI and clinical governance references

Written from general knowledge of each product's public documentation and published standards; nothing copied, and no live fetch was made in this session, so every point should be re-checked against the current public page before it is quoted. Adapt, do not copy.

## Wysa
- Positions the AI as a wellbeing companion built on rules and scripted, clinician-written content, with a generative layer added carefully and kept away from crisis handling.
- Crisis language (self-harm, suicide) triggers a fixed safety flow and helpline signposting that does not depend on the model. Same shape as our deterministic `keyword-guardrail` running first (INV-01).
- Clinical safety is documented in a public safety case and the product has pursued medical-device style assurance (UK DCB0129 style) for its clinical pathways.
- Escalation to a human coach or clinician is a paid or partner path, not the default.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria as is: helplines and crisis numbers are UK and US; there is no Nigerian numbering, no Pidgin or local idiom in the red-flag list, and chat-based therapy is not licensed here. We keep only the pattern (fixed flow, human handoff).

## Ada Health
- Symptom assessment driven by a medical knowledge base and a probabilistic reasoning engine, not a free-text language model. The knowledge base is authored and reviewed by in-house doctors and versioned.
- Publishes accuracy studies and is regulated as a medical device in the EU and UK; keeps a clinical safety and post-market surveillance process.
- Output is framed as possible conditions with an urgency level, never a diagnosis; red flags override the probabilistic ranking.
- Accuracy reporting against clinician conclusions is the model for our monthly audit (D.5), including breakdown by age and sex.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: the knowledge base is calibrated on Western disease prevalence (low malaria weighting, no sickle cell or typhoid emphasis) and assumes a nearby GP and easy lab access. We would need local prevalence and referral reality in our own rule sets.

## Infermedica
- Sells a symptom checker and triage engine to other companies; the knowledge base is expert-built, versioned and has a public evidence and change log, which is the closest analogue to our versioned `triage_rule_sets`.
- Emphasises explainability: each outcome can show the evidence that drove it. Maps to "answers shown with sources" in D.5.
- Supports a configured triage level mapping per customer, so the deploying organisation can tighten urgency. We already have this through signed configuration.
- Clinical validation studies and CE marking are marketing and compliance anchors; a technical file and post-market monitoring accompany each release.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: priced and packaged for insurers and health systems with local language support limited to major European and some Asian languages; no Nigerian languages, and routing assumes an existing referral network.

## Healthily
- Consumer symptom checker and self-care information backed by a clinician-written content library with explicit clinical review and review-by dates on each item.
- Publishes a clinical governance framework, a clinical safety officer role, and an approach to the UK DCB0129 and DCB0160 standards. A named clinical safety officer is the strongest idea to adopt (our named clinical lead gap, G-06).
- Output labelling separates general information from personalised assessment, close to our three-kind labelling rule.
- Had public scrutiny over accuracy claims, a reminder not to over-claim accuracy in marketing and to publish measured performance with its limits.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: content is tuned to NHS pathways and UK medicine names, with no NAFDAC brand names, no local food or herbal context.

## Hippocratic AI
- Positions healthcare-specific agents, with clinician-in-the-loop safety testing and a stated policy of non-diagnostic, non-prescriptive tasks (outreach, follow-up, education) rather than clinical decisions.
- Uses layered checks and a supervisor model plus human clinician reviewers to grade agent conversations before deployment; evaluation is done with licensed clinicians in volume.
- The pattern worth keeping: restrict autonomy to low-risk tasks and require measured clinician-graded evaluation before a task is switched on, which mirrors our registered `autonomy_level` and evaluation gate.
- Unsuitable for Nigeria: voice calling agents assume reliable telephony and US-style consent rules; Nigerian NDPA consent, local languages and accents, and call costs differ. Also a vendor dependency that would put patient data in a third country. We only study the evaluation method.

## UK DCB0129 (and DCB0160)
- NHS clinical risk management standard for health IT manufacturers (DCB0129) and deploying organisations (DCB0160). It requires a named Clinical Safety Officer, a hazard log, a clinical safety case report and a clinical risk management plan, updated on each change.
- Useful for us as a structure, not as a legal requirement here: a hazard log per AI system and per protocol, a named clinical owner, a safety case before go-live, and change control that re-opens the hazard log. This maps to our go-live guards (`prescribing_enabled` already lists "clinical safety case") and `clinical_incident_reports`.
- Not law in Nigeria. NMCN, MDCN and NDPC are the relevant bodies; the standard gives vocabulary and artefacts only. Do not describe the platform as "DCB compliant".

## WHO guidance on ethics and governance of AI for health
- Six principles: protect autonomy, promote wellbeing and safety, ensure transparency and explainability, foster responsibility and accountability, ensure inclusiveness and equity, promote responsive and sustainable AI. Large multimodal model guidance (2024) adds governance across developers, providers and deployers.
- Direct implications for us: human oversight (clinician signs; INV-11), disclose that content is automated (three-kind label), report performance by group (age, sex, region), keep a way to contest and to switch off (kill switch), and assess bias before and after deployment (`ai_bias_assessments`, currently empty).
- Relevant to Nigeria specifically: data and models trained elsewhere may under-serve local populations, so equity reporting by region and language is a safety measure, not decoration.

## NIST AI Risk Management Framework
- Four functions: Govern (policies, roles, accountability), Map (context and intended use), Measure (testing, metrics, monitoring) and Manage (prioritise, respond, decommission).
- Our registry already covers much of Govern and Manage (owner role, risk class, kill switch, incident table). The weak areas it highlights are Map and Measure: no named owner per system, no bias or drift records, no monthly accuracy measurement.
- The Generative AI Profile adds risks to watch: confabulation, data privacy leakage, harmful bias, and over-reliance. These support keeping retrieval limited to reviewed content, minimising data sent to the vendor, and labelling output.
- A voluntary US framework; use as a checklist for our evaluation and monitoring plan, with no legal claim.

## Cross-cutting: unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria
- Do not copy any competitor's prompts, copy, icons or content; study behaviour only.
- Any product that assumes a nearby GP, same-day labs or free emergency transport will under-triage here; our rule sets must name the Nigerian referral reality.
- Do not quote a competitor's accuracy percentage as ours; publish only our own audited numbers with sample size and suppression of small cells.
- Third-country data transfer for any vendor model needs NDPA review and a signed DPA (release-blocker checklist).
