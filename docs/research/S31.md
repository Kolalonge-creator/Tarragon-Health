# S31 Research: weekly payouts, statements, bank verification

Sources: Paystack Transfers docs and help centre (single transfers, recipients, webhooks, pending status), public descriptions of contractor payout flows at Wheel and OpenLoop, Clafiya's published model for community clinicians, Omada and Doctolib help pages. Adapted, nothing copied. NDPA means the Nigeria Data Protection Act.

**Drafts and cadence.** Contractor marketplaces (Wheel, OpenLoop) pay on a fixed weekly cycle from an immutable earnings record and show the clinician a statement that reconciles to the line. We do the same: a weekly draft built from unpaid ledger lines, never from a running balance. Unsuitable for Nigeria: instant cash-out on demand, because transfer fees and failed-transfer handling make many small payments costly; below a minimum, lines carry over.

**Approval.** Payout platforms put a human approval between "calculated" and "money moves". We make the approval re-check the ledger at that instant and refuse if it changed since the draft. The approver may never be the payee. Guard `payouts_enabled` (INV-14) sits in front of approve and send.

**Paystack transfers.** A transfer is asynchronous: the API replies `pending` (or `otp`), and only `transfer.success`, `transfer.failed` or `transfer.reversed` is the outcome. The caller's reference is the idempotency key (16 to 50 lower-case characters), so a retry after a timeout cannot pay twice. A transfer can succeed and later be reversed by the bank, so `reversed` is reachable from `succeeded`. The webhook must therefore be keyed by event and reference, not by reference alone, or the reversal is dropped as a replay. Unsuitable: Paystack OTP confirmation of transfers must be switched off for API transfers, or every payout waits for a human on a phone; treated here as `needs_attention` and shown to admins.

**Bank verification.** Paystack resolve returns the account holder name. Fintech payout flows compare it with the verified legal name and refuse a mismatch, which blocks paying a relative's or stranger's account. We compare it with the credentialed name (`clinical_staff.full_name`, set from the verified application) using a token match that tolerates order, initials and an extra middle name, and we never set `bank_verified_at` on a near miss. We keep the bank, the last four digits and the Paystack recipient code only, never the full account number (NDPA data minimisation; it is not needed after the recipient exists).

**Statements.** Doctolib and Omada show a period statement listing every item. The clinician sees each ledger line (task type and date only, INV-07), the payout it went into, its state and the transfer reference. Gross only: no tax shown or calculated (D-09).

**Withholding tax (data only, D-09).** Findings are in OQ-193: the 2024 Deduction of Tax at Source Regulations (effective 1 July 2024, reaffirmed under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 in force in 2026) set 5 percent on professional and consultancy fees paid to a Nigerian company, with a different treatment for individuals, and the payer must remit and issue a credit note. Whether Tarragon's freelance clinicians are contractors or employees for tax is a legal question. We store TIN, contractor status and a registered business name; nothing is calculated or withheld.

**Unsafe or unsuitable, summed up.** Stored balances (INV-09: the ledger is what we owe, not a wallet the clinician draws on); payouts to unverified accounts; test accounts reaching real money (INV-13); automatic retry loops on a failed transfer (a person decides).
