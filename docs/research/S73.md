# S73 research: Module 20, pricing engine

Evidence tags as in `docs/research/S70-S75-review-and-build-plan.md`: **recall** means my own knowledge of the product, not re-checked this session (no live browsing was done for this session; the machine was heavily loaded). Nothing below is copied from any product. Reference platforms for Module 20 are MyFitnessPal and Calm (self-serve subscriptions) and Omada Health (per-member contracts). Tarragon decisions override where they conflict (D-14 English only, S73-1, I8 no capitation, INV-09 no stored balance).

## 20.1 Catalogue of pay-per-use services, packs, devices, sponsor programmes (Tarragon decision)
- Best products keep one catalogue and show one price per item with what it covers; plans are a view over the same items (recall: Calm, MyFitnessPal show a short plan comparison, not a long matrix).
- Complaint pattern: prices that change between the plan screen and the checkout screen (recall: app-store review themes for both).
- Nigeria: shared phones and low data, so a price must be a small, fast, cacheable fact; amounts in whole naira shown from kobo, never rounded differently on two screens.
- Tarragon version: keep `catalog_items` and `prices` (S25). Add an `audience` (patient or institution) so the two institutional products sit in the same catalogue without ever appearing in a patient's shop. Devices stay out (S74-1).

## 20.2 Subscription plans built but off (MyFitnessPal, Calm; Tarragon decision)
- Best products make the switch-on a deliberate, reviewed act and show the next charge date and a cancel path before any card is touched (recall).
- Complaint pattern: trial-to-paid surprises, renewal charges without a reminder, cancellation hidden behind support (recall: the most common consumer complaint about both).
- Unsafe for Nigeria: card auto-charge on a shared or prepaid phone; a failed recurring charge that silently downgrades care.
- Tarragon version (S73-1, OQ-324): no `subscription_plans` table. The Membership item stays one-off with an end date (no `auto_renew` anywhere, proved structurally in S26). Enabling it needs an admin, a reason, a go-live guard that is on, and writes an audit row. The six leftover Paystack Plan codes are a founder dashboard action, recorded as a guard condition the founder attests.

## 20.3 Care packs never auto-renew (Nigeria-specific)
- Designed from the requirement. Already true by construction (an entitlement has `ends_at`, nothing charges again). S73 adds a regression proof that the new engine introduces no recurring field on orders, codes or price books.

## 20.4 Discount codes, cohort pricing, hardship pricing (Nigeria-specific)
- Best products: single-purpose codes with an expiry, a use cap and a visible "applied" line; the code is shown as its own line, never folded silently into the price (recall).
- Complaint pattern: codes that stack unpredictably, codes that work once for everyone then die with no explanation, guessable codes.
- Nigeria: codes are shared by WhatsApp-style forwarding and by employers handing out cards, so per-person limits and a total cap matter more than secrecy. Hardship must not be a self-declared discount (abuse, and a dignity risk); it flows only from an approved Access Fund grant (S72, OQ-323).
- Tarragon version: `discount_codes` are order-linked (a redemption row per order, released if the order is cancelled), capped in total and per person, expiring, optionally tied to one sponsor cohort (that is cohort pricing), rate-limited on failed tries, and every create or change is audited. One code per order. A refused code says only "this code cannot be used", never which rule failed. Hardship is a documented seam that returns nothing until S72 supplies grants. Legacy `promo_codes` are not touched.

## 20.5 Institutional price books (Omada Health)
- Best products: a contract states a per-member-per-month or per-enrolled-member rate by programme, effective-dated, with reporting against it (recall: Omada contracts are per participant; terms are not public).
- Complaint pattern (employer side): rate changes mid-contract with no version trail.
- Tarragon version: `price_books` per sponsor organisation (hmo, corporate, ngo) with immutable, effective-dated `price_book_entries` (a new entry closes the old one, like `prices`). A member is anyone in an active S38e sponsor cohort of that sponsor. The rate can only lower the list price and never below the partner or clinician cost; no capitation (I8): a rate is per covered life for a named item, never a pool.

## 20.6 Plain-language "what is included" page for every item (Nigeria-specific)
- Designed from the requirement: what you get, what it costs, what it does not include, no renewal, how to ask for a refund, in short sentences that read on a small screen and survive a forward on a shared phone.
- Tarragon version: a standalone route per item built from the existing `description_key` and `included_keys`, with a price block that comes from the same quote function as checkout, so the page and the charge cannot disagree. Linked from the checkout summary.

## 20.7 Two institutional products (Omada Health; Tarragon decision)
- Omada sells a protocol-led programme at scale and a higher-touch tier separately (recall). Tarragon already splits the same way: protocol-only programme per covered life; a human-heavy concierge coordinator service that stays OFF until staffing exists.
- Tarragon version: two catalogue kinds, `institutional_protocol` and `concierge_coordinator`, both `audience = institution`, priced only by a price book entry (no free text, no list price), never sold through patient checkout. The concierge item cannot be switched on without its own go-live guard (a staffing decision for the CMO, not an engineering switch).

## Quote function (`price_quote`, `price.changed`)
- Best products show a line-by-line total before payment (recall); the common failure is a total that is recalculated at checkout. Tarragon fixes this structurally: `create_order` calls the very function the quote calls, stores the full explanation and the price version on the order (INV-16), and the client may send the amount it was shown; a mismatch refuses the order instead of charging a different amount.
