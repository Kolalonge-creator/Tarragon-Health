# S52 research: AI health assistant, safety, transparency, memory and clinician review (7.8 to 7.13)

Date: 2026-10-07. Sources: the reference products named in spec B.7 as described in their public product pages and help centres, `docs/research/competitor-review-*.md` and `docs/research/S51-S55-review-and-build-plan.md`. Vendor figures are vendor-reported. Nothing was run against a competitor's product and screenshots were not reviewed.

## 7.8 Red flags before the model, crisis and emergency hand-off
- **Best products:** Wysa keeps an SOS control visible on every screen, detects crisis wording before the conversation continues, and hands over to local crisis lines and a safety plan. Its own published figure is that most flagged crisis messages were confirmed by the user.
- **Complaints:** a generic "call a helpline" card that does not work in the user's country, and a crisis screen that traps the user in a flow with no way out.
- **Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria:** Nigeria has no usable national crisis helpline, so a pasted overseas number is worse than none. Data and signal may be absent, so the emergency copy must be bundled in the app (INV-06). A phone number written into code is a number nobody reviews.
- **Premium Tarragon version:** an emergency button on every assistant screen. Emergency guidance points to the NEAREST HOSPITAL (patient's state and the facilities we hold), their own emergency contacts and an on-call page for self-harm (INV-05). Self-harm gets its own calmer copy, flagged PROPOSED until the CMO signs the wording. Any phone number comes from versioned configuration, never from code.

## 7.9 Visible limits, sources and a report-an-answer button
- **Best products:** short "what I can and cannot do" line near the input, answer sources under the answer, a one-tap report.
- **Complaints:** the report control buried in a menu, or asking for a long form.
- **Unsafe or unsuitable:** a report that goes nowhere. Reports must reach a clinician.
- **Premium Tarragon version:** report-an-answer on web (existing) and now on mobile, a limits panel on both ("I give general guidance from reviewed content; I do not diagnose, change doses or replace your care team"), and sources already shown under each answer (S51). Every report feeds the monthly review sample.

## 7.10 Re-engagement after silence and a silence signal to triage
- **Best products:** Lark and Noom send a gentle "pick up where you left off" after a gap.
- **Complaints:** guilt, streak shaming, and nudges that name the condition on the lock screen.
- **Unsafe or unsuitable:** treating silence as a clinical finding, or letting an assistant change treatment. Naming a condition in a notification (INV-07).
- **Premium Tarragon version:** for programme members only, after a configured silence period (PROPOSED config) write a rule event the triage engine can read. It is a signal, never a treatment change and never a model output. The re-engagement message is a keyed generic template ("It has been a little while").

## 7.11 Clarifying questions
- **Best products:** Lark asks one short follow-up when a message is too vague to answer safely.
- **Complaints:** an interrogation of five questions before any help.
- **Unsafe or unsuitable:** guessing at an unclear symptom or medicine question.
- **Premium Tarragon version:** a graph node that asks at most two questions when intent is under-specified, only once per topic, and never delays an emergency path. Evaluated with fixed cases.

## 7.12 Memory within consent
- **Best products:** Lark remembers goals and preferences and shows what it remembers.
- **Complaints:** memory that quietly stores sensitive facts, cannot be seen, or cannot be deleted. On a shared phone, remembered details leak.
- **Unsafe or unsuitable:** remembering clinical facts (the record is the source of truth); consent buried in terms.
- **Premium Tarragon version:** goals and preferences only, never a clinical fact. Off by default. A consent record is written before first use, modelled on scribe consents. The patient can view, edit, delete and export every item. The assistant never sees memory until consent is on.

## 7.13 Monthly clinician review of a sample
- **Best products:** safety teams at assistant vendors sample conversations for review; the useful part is that user reports are always included.
- **Complaints:** reviewers who can read everything, with no audit trail.
- **Unsafe or unsuitable:** widening who can read conversations. A purpose-built reader with a reason and an audit row is the safe shape.
- **Premium Tarragon version:** a monthly job selects a random sample plus every reported conversation, a review table records verdict, issue category and a linked incident, and the reviewer reads through one RPC that demands a reason and writes an audit row. The conversation read policy is not widened.
