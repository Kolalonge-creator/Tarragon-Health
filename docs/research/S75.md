# S75 research: Module 22, outcomes and population health analytics (completion)

Written 2026-10-07. S75 is mostly built (S38 to S38g, applied to production); this note covers what S75 adds. Sources are the earlier notes `docs/research/S38.md` and `docs/research/S70-S75-review-and-build-plan.md` plus the CMO pack `docs/plans/S70-S75-cmo-signoff-pack.md`. **Web access was not used in this session** (the machine was heavily loaded and the earlier notes already carry the verified material). Competitor product claims that earlier notes marked "recall" stay unverified here, and nothing below depends on one.

## 22.1 Baseline and follow-up for every pathway (Omada Health, Lark Health)
- Best products report change from a stated baseline at fixed follow-ups, and report the starting subgroup (for example people who began above target) separately. Omada's published hypertension work does this for systolic change; Lark reports a mean drop among those who reached the follow-up (a survivor figure; recall, unverified).
- Complaints and risks: survivor-only rates flatter a programme; one fixed "meaningful change" number is applied to everyone although estimates vary with starting severity (CMO pack C7, secondary sources); mental-health scores in a business report can expose someone in crisis.
- Not suitable here: weight targets for pregnant or under-18 members; HbA1c share-under-7% for people with frailty or pregnancy; any figure from a very small cohort in a small market where people are easy to re-identify.
- Decision: HbA1c change and share under 7%, weight loss of 5% or more, PHQ-9 and GAD-7 response and remission shown by starting severity band with a caveat; strict denominators with coverage beside every rate; PHQ-9 item 9 above zero or a score of 20+ is routed to the existing follow-up and never appears in a figure; the BP 140/90 basis stays (S75-1) with a printed note; figures stay hidden until the CMO signs the definitions.

## 22.2 Adherence, engagement, retention, programme completion (Medisafe; Nigeria-specific)
- Adherence apps publish no outcome-style definition for retention that I could verify. The consistent lesson from observational work (Lark, recall) is that engagement predicts outcome but is not an outcome.
- Risk: blending "stayed and logged" into a health score hides drop-out; completion defined by the product team looks better than completion defined by the clinical lead.
- Nigeria: shared phones and patchy data make "logged a reading" a weaker signal than elsewhere; membership length is annual, so "still a member at day 180" is mostly a billing fact.
- Decision: a separate family (retained, engaged in the last 30 days, care pack ran to its end). What counts as "complete" is a founder and CMO choice (OQ-383); the label says exactly what is counted.

## 22.3 Population risk stratification (Innovaccer, Health Catalyst, H2O.ai)
- Vendors sell risk tiers feeding care-gap lists; Obermeyer 2019 (Science) shows cost as a proxy for need under-serves people who spend less. Vendor rule counts are marketing claims (unverified).
- Risk: an opaque model used to deny or delay care; bias by sex, age or region going unnoticed.
- Decision (S75-4): stay deterministic with stored reasons; add a monthly frozen fairness report by sex, age band and region for the CMO, written once, with the CMO's review recorded separately. No ML before 90 days of real snapshots, and then only through the AI governance registry. Language is not a cut (English only).

## 22.4 Symptom-checker accuracy dashboard (Ada Health)
- Published validation of symptom tools compares against clinicians' final diagnoses; none of that exists here. What we hold is whether the clinician who handled a case agreed with the automatic grade.
- Risk: calling agreement "accuracy" invites a marketing claim the data cannot support.
- Decision (S75-3): renamed to "agreement" in the page, library, route and database function; no accuracy figure published until an outcome-linked study with a written design exists.

- Already built (S38c, S38d). Nothing added in S75.

## 22.6 Aggregate sponsor dashboards, minimum cohort (Omada Health)
- Statistical disclosure practice uses small floors (5 to 11) plus complementary suppression; the founder set 20 (30 for two attributes). The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 names no number (CMO pack C11), so counsel or the DPO still confirms (OQ-233).
- Finding: the rule was written out by hand in nine places and had already drifted (a different number for corporate and payer dashboards; a withheld cohort still printing "enrolled" counts; a triage grade withheld without its neighbour).
- Decision (S75-5): one database function family (`min_cell_floor`, `suppress_cell`, `suppress_difference`, `suppress_groups`) used by every surface, with a proof that scans the function bodies.

## 22.7 Escalation and follow-up rates, clinician response times (Nigeria-specific)
- The reliability dashboard (S36e) already shows what is waiting now, page acknowledgement and on-call cover. It has no task turnaround, no escalation or hand-back rate and no follow-up completion.
- Risk: per-clinician figures become a league table and are gamed; small tiers identify one person.
- Decision: rates by how urgent the task was, and by the tier of the clinician who finished it, only when at least three different clinicians stand behind a tier; no per-clinician figure anywhere.

## 22.8 Data-quality and missing-data indicators (Nigeria-specific)
- Offline sync, shared phones and manual entry make missing and late data the main threat to every outcome figure here; a late reading is never added to a past snapshot (S38).
- Decision: a standalone report of snapshot completeness by measure and day, reading sources, share waiting for a clinician, and device sync lag; counted in people, with the same suppression.

## 22.9 Exportable pilot and renewal reports (Omada Health)
- Omada-style client reports pair figures with a stated evaluation design (recall). The danger in a renewal conversation is a sentence that claims cause.
- Decision: an audited CSV and PDF built from the same rows; a fixed evaluation-design note; the database and the app each refuse to issue a file whose text claims cause (a sentence that states a limit is allowed), and a drift test keeps the two word lists identical.
