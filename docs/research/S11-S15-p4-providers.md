# S11-S15 package P4 (provider layer): research notes

Evidence tags: [V] vendor claim, [P] peer reviewed, [G] guideline or regulator, [NV] not verified (could not be checked from here; verify before relying). Searched 2026-10-13/14. Nothing is copied; what was adopted is adapted and the place it shows up is named.

## 1. Webhook inbox, replay, idempotency (R5)
- Providers deliver at least once. Retries, a slow handler, a lost 200 and a manual dashboard resend all produce duplicates, so dedupe on a unique key enforced by the database, not a check-then-insert. [V] (Hookdeck guidance, several Paystack integration guides; a Paystack dashboard article confirms events can be retried from the dashboard.) Paystack's retry schedule and window are quoted differently by different sources (hours to 72 hours) [NV]; the code does not depend on either.
- Paystack signs the raw body with HMAC-SHA512 in `x-paystack-signature`; the `transfer.reversed` event can follow a `transfer.success`, so a handler must update the existing transfer and only move it forward, never insert a new one. [V] Paystack carries no event id, so the key is event type plus the reference it is about. [V]
- Store the exact raw bytes, verify before parsing, compare in constant time, and keep the signature verdict and the original timestamp with the record, because a replay days later would fail a fresh-timestamp check if re-verified. [V]/inference (Svix glossary, Standard Webhooks 300 s tolerance).
- Queue-first: acknowledge fast, process from the stored record, dead-letter and replay. [V] (Hookdeck "webhooks at scale").
- Zoom: signature is `v0:<timestamp>:<body>`; endpoint must answer within about 3 seconds; three retries at about 5, 25 and 85 minutes. [V, forum and Nango docs; Zoom's own retry page not fetched, so the schedule is [NV]]. `x-zm-request-id` is reported stable across retries but not documented, and `x-zm-trackingid` was seen identical on different deliveries, so neither is used as the key. [NV]
- ADOPTED: `provider_events` (raw body append-only, signature verdict stored, unique key per provider, claim/complete/fail with a lease and backoff like the S10 bus, audited admin replay); Paystack key = event + reference (+ amount for a refund), Zoom key = event + meeting uuid + event_ts, Resend key = Svix message id. Shows up in `supabase/migrations/*_s14x_*`, `_shared/integrations/webhook-inbox.ts`, and the three webhook handlers.
- ADOPTED, differently by risk: Resend and Zoom adopt the inbox directly and fall back to the old direct handling if the inbox is unreachable (a missing table must never stop delivery outcomes being recorded). Paystack is behind `provider_inbox_paystack` (off) and, when on, answers 500 if it cannot store the event first ("stored first" is the point for money; Paystack retries).
- UNSUITABLE / NOT DONE: re-signing payloads for replay (the stored verdict is the authority; replay never re-verifies). A managed relay (Hookdeck, Svix) was not adopted: a new vendor, a new data processor for payment payloads, and a second place the customer email lives; the inbox is a few hundred lines in the database we already run. Revisit if volume or the on-call burden grows.

## 2. Settlement reconciliation (R6)
- Paystack lists settlements (`GET /settlement`, filters status, from, to, page, perPage; amounts in kobo; fields `total_amount`, `total_fees`, `settlement_date`) and the transactions inside one (`GET /settlement/:id/transactions`). [V] Third-party pages disagree on the transactions path (singular vs plural) and on field names for the net amount [NV]: the adapter reads only `total_amount`, `total_fees`, `reference`, `amount`, `fees`, `paid_at`, `status`, treats anything else as `bad_response`, and the first sandbox run is where the paths are confirmed.
- Stripe's transaction reconciliation sorts records into settled, in process (amount differs beyond a threshold), open (ours but not in settlement) and foreign (in settlement but not ours). [V] ADOPTED as the break types `missing_at_provider` (open), `missing_in_ledger` (foreign) and `amount_mismatch` (in process), plus `settlement_total_mismatch`. Stripe lets the difference threshold be configured; kobo are exact here, so the tolerance is zero and any difference opens a break.
- Stripe's receivables ageing buckets (30/60/90/120/over) are the model for break ageing; ours are 24h/72h/168h/over because settlement is T+1 and a break older than three days is a conversation with Paystack. [V]
- Modern Treasury's break model could not be found in public pages [NV]. The design follows the general rule in the brief: compare, queue, age, never auto-correct. Closing a break needs a note of at least ten characters and is audited; the page has no button that posts anything.
- Daily balance reconciliation with `GET /transfer/:id` for payouts is the recommended Paystack check [V]; transfers are not in the settlement report, so payout reconciliation stays on `payout_transfers` and `verifyTransfer`. Not built here (follow-up).

## 3. Payout reversal, maker-checker (R6)
- A reversal after success is a bank return of the money to our Paystack balance; the transfer row moves success to reversed and the books must move with it. [V] The payout state machine already allowed `succeeded -> reversed` (S31); it did not post to the ledger at all, because payouts had no GL posting. ADOPTED: a flagged posting on success (Dr 5200 Clinician fees, Cr 1000 Bank) and the exact opposite as a NEW entry on a later reversal, keyed by (payout, attempt, kind) so a replayed webhook posts nothing twice; posted entries are never deleted.
- Maker-checker for payouts: one person proposes a batch, a different person releases it; per-payout, per-clinician and per-batch limits apply at proposal, release and send. This is the compensating control for founder decision OQ-97 (Paystack transfer OTP stays OFF). The control is the standard four-eyes pattern used by bank payment platforms [NV, no single source cited]; nothing was copied.
- UNSUITABLE: with a single admin and the CMO as the only second approver, nothing can be released if the CMO is unavailable. That is deliberate for money and is written into the closeout.

## 4. Circuit breaker (R6)
- Consecutive failures open the breaker, a timer lets one probe through (half open), success closes it. Standard pattern. [NV, general engineering knowledge, no vendor source needed.]
- Agora offers a vendor-side fallback (high stream to low stream to audio only) as a subscriber option [V]. A Zoom developer-forum answer says the Video SDK does not drop video by itself and the app should act on network-quality events [V, forum]. Daily and 100ms behaviour could not be found [NV].
- ADOPTED: `provider_health` + `provider_health_v`, `provider_call_allowed` / `provider_record_result`, fail-open when the health store is unreachable, only outages count (a bad request does not), only checkout initialisation is guarded (a refund, a verify or a transfer must never be skipped by a breaker). No second provider, as decided. Email is wired as a wrapper but the notification sender still uses its own Resend code, so the email breaker has no caller yet (stated plainly in the closeout).

## 5. Audio fallback evidence (R7a)
- The ladder (video, audio only, phone) already exists in S21; the open item was where the thresholds sit. The harness drives the real `stepLadder` with synthetic networks and shows when audio-only triggers under the proposed values and under candidates. The network profiles are illustrative, not measured; the mapping from loss and bandwidth to the SDK's 0-5 level and to a video bitrate is an assumption that is written out in the code and is the first thing to replace with handset readings.
- Findings from the illustrative run (not evidence for a decision, but they show what to measure): a clean but high-latency link (900 ms) goes to audio only because of the 600 ms audio round-trip rule; a congested 4G link at 6% loss and 400 kbps stays on video under the proposed loss threshold of 10% and drops only if the threshold is 5%; burst loss of 8 seconds needs three bursts to trigger at 3 samples and 3 s spacing.

## 6. Speech gold set (R7b) and scribe review (Q18, Q19)
- AfriSpeech-200 (Olatunji et al., TACL 2023) and the Interspeech 2024 paper (Afonja et al., arXiv 2406.12387) benchmark accented African English including clinical entities: 120 accents, 19 systems, clinical-entity recall and medical WER reported separately, because a low overall WER can hide a worse error rate on drug names; fine-tuning on accented clinical speech improved medical WER by 25 to 34% relative. [P] A community Whisper-small model card reports 22.9% overall and 29.8% clinical WER after fine-tuning against 49 and 56% zero-shot [V, secondary, different split]. ADOPTED: score overall WER, drug-name error rate and number error rate separately, per accent tag and per speaker, and list every drug and number mismatch with its context. No pass mark is encoded (Q19): the CMO sets it.
- Ambient scribes (Nabla, Heidi, Abridge, Suki): consent is handled by script and policy and varies by jurisdiction; clinicians must review every note; none of the sources found shows a tool independently verifying medicines or doses, so that check stays with the clinician. [V, G (NHS England guidance quoted by a Heidi guide)] Whether Abridge or Suki flag medicine names or doses for extra checking could not be found [NV]. ADOPTED and made stricter than the vendors: recorded explicit consent per encounter, enforced in the scribe-draft function, in `attach_scribe_draft_to_note`, and now by database triggers on the two rows that carry a consent id (S23x); and the review screen marks every drug name and number (Q19) next to the existing confirm-each-section step.
- Nigeria: the drug list is Nigerian primary care (artemether-lumefantrine, Coartem, amodiaquine) not US-centric; a Nigerian accent set is exactly what US scribe vendors do not publish.
