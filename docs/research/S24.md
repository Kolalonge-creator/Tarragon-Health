# S24 research: prescriptions, referrals, care plan changes and titration proposals

Method: public guidance, regulator and law-firm summaries, and search snippets, read 2026-10-06. Search coverage was thin and US-only. Practo, Doctolib, Vezeeta, Kry, Okadoc, Clafiya, Ping An Good Doctor, Omada's own help centre, Lark, Noom, Oviva, Kaia, Sidekick, BeatO, Health2Sync, Glooko and Propeller returned nothing usable for the specific questions below; treat those as unresearched, not as having no policy. Nigerian regulator text (MDCN, PCN, NAFDAC) was read only through secondary summaries; anything resting on them is marked UNVERIFIED. Nothing here is copied; each line says what we adapt.

## 1. E-prescribing and a downloadable form usable at any pharmacy

**Content of a remote prescription.** India's Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 require the prescriber's name, qualifications, registration number, address and contact details; the patient's name, age, sex and contact details; drug names in capitals with formulation, dose, frequency and duration; date, time and place; and a signature. The doctor sends a signed copy (photo, scan or digital) by email or messaging (https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Telemedicine-Practice-Guidelines, https://www.mondaq.com/india/healthcare/921874/telemedicine-practice-guidelines). Whether generic names are mandatory there was not confirmed. Adapt: one fixed layout carrying MDCN number, patient name and age, generic name first, strength, form, quantity, duration, signature block, date and a verification code. This matches what a Nigerian pharmacist is likely to ask for, but no PCN checklist was found (UNVERIFIED).

**Nigeria rules.** Secondary summaries say a prescription issued after a teleconsultation touches PCN, NAFDAC and the Pharmaceutical Society (https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/healthcare/1464228/telemedicine-revolution-legal-implications-and-regulatory-compliance-for-healthcare-providers-in-nigeria), and that the MDCN 2022 telemedicine guideline sets identification, documentation, consent and referral duties (https://clinikehr.com/blog/mdcn-telemedicine-guidelines-nigeria-2026, a vendor blog, low quality). Prescription-only medicines may be supplied only under a licensed pharmacist's supervision (PCN Act, https://pcn.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Pharmacy-Council-of-Nigeria-Act-2022-publication.pdf). Whether a PDF or screenshot prescription is accepted by every pharmacy is UNVERIFIED; field reports suggest practice varies, so we cannot promise it. Adapt: say plainly on the form and in the app that the pharmacist decides, and give the pharmacist a way to verify.

**Verification, validity, repeats, cancel.** NHS England's electronic prescription service sends a prescription to a nominated pharmacy; a repeatable prescription must be first dispensed within 6 months and runs at most 12 months, with batch issues at set intervals (https://cpe.org.uk/national-pharmacy-services/essential-services/repeat-dispensing/). Surescripts and the NHS cancel and amend flows were not read in detail (UNVERIFIED). Adapt: a short verification code and a public verify page that shows only status (valid, expired, cancelled) and the issuing clinician, never the patient's condition; a validity period as a PROPOSED config value; amendment creates a new version and marks the old one superseded; cancellation is visible on the verify page. Repeats are not built in S24.

**Controlled and unsuitable items.** NAFDAC and national controlled-medicines policy restrict controlled substances (https://www.unodc.org/conig/uploads/documents/Approved_National_Policy_For_Controlled_Medicines_And_Its_Implementation_Strategies.pdf). UNSAFE: remote prescribing of controlled drugs. Adapt: the form refuses any item flagged controlled. A watermark-free PDF that is easy to edit invites forgery; the verify code is the control.

## 2. Safety checks at signing

**What platforms do.** Surescripts-style systems run allergy, interaction, duplicate-therapy and dose-range checks at the point of prescribing and require an override reason; Teladoc, Kry and K Health publish nothing on their own checks (UNVERIFIED). Adapt: run allergy, duplicate class, interaction, renal and pregnancy cautions and a dose range limit on the clinician's draft before the sign button enables; each alert shows its reason, and an override needs a typed reason that is stored and visible in the audit. Generic name first, with the brand optional.

**Hypertension classes.** WHO HEARTS and the Nigerian protocol favour a single-pill combination or simple stepwise classes: ARB or ACE inhibitor, calcium channel blocker, thiazide-like diuretic (https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/f062769d-f075-4a00-87af-0a2106e0bd04/content, https://resolvetosavelives.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/nigeria-hypertension-protocol-04.pdf; Nigerian snippet shows amlodipine first with losartan and hydrochlorothiazide, treat as indicative only). Adapt: the allowed drug list and classes come from the CMO-signed protocol, not code. Maximum days supply is a PROPOSED value.

**Unsafe.** A silent override of an alert; auto-suggesting a dose to the patient; ACE inhibitor or ARB for a patient who may be pregnant; checks that fail open when allergy data is missing (show "allergies not recorded" and block signing until the clinician confirms).

## 3. Referrals (specialist, lab, imaging, hospital, emergency)

**What platforms do.** Practo, Doctolib, Vezeeta, Okadoc and Kry referral flows were not readable (UNVERIFIED). Nigerian practice: under NHIA, the primary care provider is the gatekeeper and referral is meant to go primary to secondary to tertiary, but patients often self-refer to teaching hospitals, and about 60 percent of referrals are verbal (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6444603/, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331875842_Referral_Services_under_NHIS_-_Hospital_Based_Descriptive_Cross-Sectional_Sudy_in_Abuja_Nigeria; snippet-level). Adapt: a written letter with reason, urgency tier (routine, soon, urgent, emergency), a short clinical summary chosen by the clinician, and named attachments; the patient picks the facility from a list or names their own; the patient sees the letter and consents to each share before anything leaves.

**Closing the loop.** Status runs requested, sent, seen, report received, closed, with a chaser if no outcome arrives by a PROPOSED date. Emergency tier is a call-to-action to go now, not a queued letter. Cost and transport are shown as "ask the facility", not estimated.

**Unsafe.** Sharing the record without recorded consent; a referral that can stay open forever; sending reproductive-health data under a general consent (protected category rules apply); promising NHIA coverage we cannot confirm.

## 4. Care plan changes with signature

**What platforms do.** Omada, Lark, Twin Health and others describe clinician-supervised medication adjustment under protocol (https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/new-omada-care-program-cuts-down-pcp-visits-making-its-own-adjustments-patient-meds, https://web.twinhealth.com/); none published a patient-facing change flow, version history or "what changed" screen that we could read (UNVERIFIED for Noom, Oviva, Kaia, Sidekick, Dario, WellDoc, Glooko, BeatO, Health2Sync). Adapt: a change is a draft, then signed by a named clinician, then versioned; the patient sees a "what changed" summary (target, schedule, medicine) in plain words, with the old and new values side by side.

**Effective only after the patient confirms.** A medicine change takes effect when the patient confirms, not when signed. Reading-schedule and target changes can take effect on signing but are still shown. Avoid dark patterns: equal weight for "I understand" and "I have a question", no countdown, no pre-ticked box, declining is recorded and routes to the care team, never hidden. Unconfirmed after a PROPOSED number of hours triggers a follow-up and a care team task.

## 5. Titration proposals as drafts only

**Evaluator inputs.** Average home BP over 7 days (ISH 2020 treats home average of 135/85 or higher as the intensify threshold; https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2021/0615/p763.html), current medicines and doses, adherence, reported side effects, and potassium and creatinine only if available and recent. Home readings need a defined count and technique; ISH says to confirm with home or ambulatory readings because of white coat and masked patterns.

**Step tables.** WHO HEARTS-style protocols give stepwise doubling and adding a third class, review every 4 to 6 weeks (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41371-021-00612-6, https://resolvetosavelives.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/mp-a1-hypertension-protocol.pdf). The step table is CMO-owned, signed, versioned data. The evaluator reads the table and never contains drug names, doses or thresholds in code.

**Stop conditions (no proposal is made, a reason is shown to the clinician).** Open red or amber triage; pregnancy or possible pregnancy; fewer than the minimum readings in the window; adherence below the PROPOSED threshold (fix adherence first); a change inside the review window; unreliable or implausible readings; side effect reported; missing or stale K or creatinine where the step requires it; patient outside the approved protocol or protocol not active.

**Regulatory frame.** FDA's CDS guidance treats software as non-device only if the clinician can independently review the basis of the recommendation, in plain language, with missing or unexpected inputs shown (https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance). MHRA software-as-medical-device was not read (UNVERIFIED). This is US guidance, not Nigerian law; we use it as a design standard. Adapt: the draft shows inputs used, table step and version, the rule that fired, and what is unknown. A clinician can edit, reject with a reason, or sign.

**Unsafe.** Auto-applying; hiding the rationale; proposing outside the signed protocol; proposing on unreliable readings or with white coat or masked patterns unexcluded; a patient-visible proposal before signing (INV-11); an AI language model in the proposal path.

## 6. Patient confirmation, adherence follow-up, low-literacy settings

**Comprehension risk.** About 42 percent of low-literacy patients cannot follow common medicine instructions, and teach-back raised correct recall of purpose and side effects from about half to over 90 percent in one cardiac study (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7156054/, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41233965/; snippet-level). Adapt: after a medicine change, a one-question teach-back ("what will you take tonight?") with tap choices, not typing; a picture of the pill form if available; en and pcm text, short sentences, no jargon. Nigerian-language audio is out of scope by founder decision.

**Adherence and follow-up.** A check-in at a PROPOSED interval after confirmation asks "did you start it" and "any problem", with a one-tap route to the care team. Missed confirmation or a reported problem opens a care team task. Low data: text only by default, no images, works offline with a queued confirmation that syncs later.

**Notifications.** Push and email carry no condition, drug, reading or result (INV-07): "You have an update from your care team. Open the app." Nothing in the notification is itself the confirmation.

## Decisions this implies for Tarragon

- A prescription is a signed, versioned row with a fixed form layout; amend creates a new version and supersedes the old (INV-02).
- The downloadable form carries a verification code and a status-only verify page that never exposes the condition (INV-07).
- Signing is blocked until allergy, interaction, duplicate, renal, pregnancy and dose-range checks have run; every override needs a stored reason (INV-10).
- Allergy data missing means the clinician must confirm "none recorded" before signing; the check never fails open (INV-10).
- Controlled medicines cannot be put on the form; validity period and max days supply are PROPOSED config values (INV-12).
- Titration evaluator reads CMO-signed step tables from versioned config and holds no drug names or thresholds in code (INV-12).
- A titration proposal is a draft visible only to clinicians, always showing inputs, step, protocol version and unknowns; no language model is involved (INV-11, INV-16).
- Every stop condition produces a visible "no proposal because X" for the clinician, never a silent skip (INV-10).
- A medicine change takes effect only after patient confirmation; declining routes to the care team, with equal-weight buttons and no countdown (INV-02).
- Referral sharing needs per-share patient consent recorded before send; every referral has a status and a chaser so none stays open (INV-02, INV-10).
- Care plan changes store before and after values and render a plain-language "what changed" in en and pcm.
- Notifications about all of the above name nothing and are never the only interface (INV-07).

## Unsafe or unsuitable, do not build

- Auto-applying any titration or care plan change.
- A patient-visible proposal or draft before a clinician signs.
- A silent or reasonless override of a safety alert.
- Auto-suggesting a dose or medicine to a patient.
- Remote prescribing of controlled medicines.
- Hard-coded drug choices, doses or BP thresholds in the evaluator.
- Proposing on unreliable readings, pregnancy, open red or amber triage, or low adherence.
- Sharing records for a referral without recorded consent, or leaving a referral with no closed loop.
- Promising that every pharmacy or NHIA facility will accept the PDF, or showing a condition or drug name in a notification or on the verify page.
- Dark patterns on confirmation (pre-ticked, hidden decline, timers).
