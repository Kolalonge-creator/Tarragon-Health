# S33 research: BP course, audio lessons, guided breathing, content governance

Scope: public docs, help centres and published papers only. Ideas adapted, no text copied. Verified items cite a source in the last section; anything else is marked UNVERIFIED (from general knowledge of the products, not re-checked this session). Product behaviour of named apps (Noom, Headspace, Healthily and others) was not re-fetched this session and is UNVERIFIED unless stated.

## 1. Short structured courses (daily lessons under 5 minutes)

- Pattern across Noom, Headspace, Happify, Wysa, Big Health/HelloBetter (UNVERIFIED in detail): one fixed-length programme (weeks, not open-ended), one small lesson per day, a visible "day X of Y", and a closing action rather than a closing summary.
- Clinical digital programmes (Big Health, HelloBetter) are typically structured as sessions with homework between sessions, which suggests a lesson plus one real-world task per day works better than content alone (UNVERIFIED).
- End-of-lesson "what can I do next" should be one concrete, doable thing today (take a reading, check tablets are in the pill box, swap one salty ingredient), never a menu of five.
- Progress: show days completed and the next lesson; allow catch-up. Streaks help some users but punish the unwell and anyone with no data or power for a day.
- Completion: celebrate finishing the course with a plain summary of what the person now does differently, not a badge wall.
- Let people skip ahead and replay; do not lock lessons behind completion of the previous one when a reading is urgent.
- Adopt: 12 week-aligned lessons of 3 to 5 minutes, one action per lesson, resumable, catch-up friendly, streak shown as "days you did something", never as a loss counter.
- Avoid: loss-framed streak resets, guilt notifications, leaderboards, upsell inside a lesson, gamification that rewards logging a normal reading over an honest one.
- Nigeria note: assume intermittent data and shared phones; progress must sync later and work offline; reminders by in-app, push and email only, and no feature may need a message to succeed.

## 2. Hypertension education content

- A good BP course covers: why BP matters without symptoms; correct home measurement (rest 5 minutes, seated, back supported, arm at heart level, correct cuff size, no caffeine or smoking beforehand, same times daily, record every reading); what the numbers mean; salt and potassium; taking medicines daily and what to do if a dose is missed; side effects to report; alcohol and weight; physical activity; stress and sleep; herbal products; when to seek urgent care.
- Urgent-care lesson must use plain red-flag wording and route to the existing emergency safety net in the app, with no "wait and see" advice.
- Salt lesson should name local sources: seasoning cubes, stock powders, salted fish and meat, instant noodles, suya spice, processed snacks; potassium lesson should name local foods (UNVERIFIED which foods are best; needs dietitian review) and carry a caution that potassium advice differs for people with kidney disease or on certain medicines.
- Evidence on lesson length, spacing and knowledge checks: spacing and retrieval practice improve retention in education research generally; direct hypertension-app evidence is limited. UNVERIFIED for this specific population.
- Teach-back and knowledge checks: ask the learner to say or choose what they would do, then confirm or gently correct; one question per lesson, never graded as pass or fail.
- Health literacy: plain language, short sentences, one main message, a clear call to action. The CDC Clear Communication Index has 20 items scored to 100 with 90 or more treated as passing (verified, see sources). WHO health-literacy guidance supports the same direction (UNVERIFIED in detail).
- Common Nigerian myths to address directly (list is from general knowledge of local practice, UNVERIFIED as to prevalence; confirm with community testing): BP only matters when I feel dizzy or have a headache; I feel fine so I can stop tablets; bitter kola, garlic, ginger, zobo or herbal mixtures can replace tablets; BP drugs are a lifelong poison; hypertension is caused by spiritual attack or too much "blood"; seasoning cubes do not count as salt; only old or rich people get it; a single normal reading means cured.
- Myth format: state the belief kindly, say what is true, give the action. Do not repeat the myth as the headline of a shareable card.
- Adopt: myth-and-fact lessons, red-flag lesson, "what to do if you missed a tablet" lesson reviewed by your care team, local food examples.
- Avoid: fear framing, dosing advice in lessons, claiming a herb "is bad" or "is good" without clinical sign-off, numeric BP targets that differ from the patient's own care plan.
- Nigeria note: cuffs are often borrowed or the wrong size; include a short lesson on cuff size and on readings taken at pharmacies or chemists being logged with the setting.

## 3. Audio lessons

- Meditation apps (Insight Timer, Calm, Headspace) commonly offer download for offline use, speed or timer controls and background playback (UNVERIFIED in detail per app); Samsung Health guided audio similarly. Treat these as expected features.
- Voice and pacing: warm, unhurried, one speaker, short sentences, deliberate pauses after each key idea; target about 120 to 140 words per minute for health instruction (UNVERIFIED target, test it).
- Length: match the text lesson, 2 to 4 minutes; keep a separate 1 minute "just the action" cut.
- Always show a transcript alongside, with the current sentence highlighted; this serves deaf and hard-of-hearing users and people who read faster than they listen.
- Offline: download per lesson over wifi, show file size before download, resume interrupted downloads, offer a low-bitrate mono speech encoding; do not auto-download.
- Playback: 0.75x, 1x, 1.25x, skip back 10 seconds, sleep timer off by default; works with screen off and with earpiece or speaker.
- Low-literacy: audio first, picture cues, big play button, minimal text, voice-led navigation of "next lesson"; icons plus words.
- Pidgin audio: record by a native speaker, not text-to-speech, for the first release; TTS of Pidgin is likely to mispronounce and flatten tone (UNVERIFIED, test).
- Adopt: transcript always on, offline per-lesson download with size shown, 3-minute lessons, native-speaker recordings, speed controls.
- Avoid: background music that masks speech, auto-play next lesson, streaming-only audio, synthetic voice for medical safety wording.
- Nigeria note: Android-heavy and low-end devices; keep audio small, no autoplay video, and test on a low-RAM phone over a throttled connection. Accents vary: pilot Lagos, Hausa-influenced and Igbo-influenced speakers.

## 4. Guided slow breathing

- Evidence: a meta-analysis of 17 randomised trials found slow breathing lowered systolic BP by about 5.6 mmHg and diastolic by about 3.0 mmHg, and described it as a modest effect (verified, see sources). Typical protocols use 6 to 10 breaths per minute with or without a device; 6 per minute is the commonly studied pace (verified in the same source). Many trials used about 15 minutes a day (verified for at least one study).
- RESPeRATE is a device-based approach (UNVERIFIED regulatory status in Nigeria). Consumer apps (Oura, Samsung Health, Calm, Headspace, Wysa) offer breathing exercises as wellbeing tools, not BP treatment (UNVERIFIED per app).
- Pace: about 6 breaths a minute, roughly 4 seconds in and 6 seconds out; a longer exhale than inhale; breathe gently through the nose or lightly pursed lips, no forced deep breaths.
- Duration: start with 2 to 3 minutes, build toward about 10 to 15 minutes if comfortable; offer a 1 minute version.
- Stop and seek help if: dizziness or light-headedness, tingling around the mouth or hands, chest pain or tightness, new or worse breathlessness, palpitations that feel wrong. Extra caution (ask your care team first): lung disease such as asthma or COPD, heart rhythm problems, pregnancy complications, anxiety that is worsened by breath focus. Exact contraindication wording UNVERIFIED and needs clinician sign-off.
- Visual pacer: expanding and contracting circle or a filling bar, one colour, text cue "in" and "out" in the user's language, optional gentle haptic pulse at phase changes, sound optional and off by default; must work with screen reader and with reduced motion.
- Claims: say "a calm breathing exercise some people find helpful". Never say it lowers your BP, treats hypertension, or replaces tablets. Show a one-line reminder "keep taking your medicines and measuring as agreed with your care team".
- Never time the exercise to a BP reading; do not suggest taking a reading as proof it worked.
- Adopt: 6 per minute default with a slower and a gentler option, bar or circle pacer, haptics optional, safety card before first use, stop button always visible.
- Avoid: BP-lowering claims, streak pressure on breathing, breath-holds, hyperventilation-style breathing, requiring audio.
- Nigeria note: usable in noisy shared spaces without sound; do not frame as a substitute for medicine given cost and herbal-cure beliefs.

## 5. Understandability testing

- PEMAT (AHRQ) scores understandability and actionability, with separate print and audiovisual versions; 70% is the commonly used acceptability threshold (verified).
- CDC Clear Communication Index: 20 items, score out of 100, 90 or higher passes (verified).
- Teach-back, cloze test (learner fills in deleted words) and reading-level formulas (Flesch-Kincaid, targeting about grade 6 to 8) are common; reading-level formulas are built for English prose and say little about Pidgin or audio (UNVERIFIED for Nigerian English).
- Lightweight protocol, 10 to 15 community participants, one afternoon per language:
  1. Recruit adults with hypertension or at risk, mixed literacy, ages, gender, phone type; include 3 to 4 low-literacy participants; pay for time.
  2. Draft lessons pass PEMAT (two raters) and CCI before any participant sees them; fix below 70 and below 90.
  3. Individual session, 30 minutes, on the participant's own phone: play or read one lesson, then no looking back.
  4. Ask: what was the main message, in your own words; what would you do today; one hard word or phrase; one thing that felt wrong or unbelievable (cognitive interview, think-aloud).
  5. Score each: recalled the main message yes or no, named the right action yes or no, misunderstood anything unsafe yes or no.
  6. Pass rule: at least 80% recall the main message and the action, and zero unsafe misunderstandings; any unsafe misunderstanding means rewrite and retest with new participants.
  7. Log words that confuse, then update a glossary.
- In-app measures: lesson started vs completed, replay rate, drop-off second in audio, one-tap "was this clear" with a "no" follow-up reason, knowledge-check first-try correctness, next-day action self-report, speed setting usage, transcript use, offline download rate. Aggregate only; no individual dashboards for staff.
- Adopt: PEMAT plus CCI as a build gate, then the 10 to 15 person test, then in-app clarity signals.
- Avoid: using reading-grade scores alone, testing only with staff or students, leading questions.
- Nigeria note: run in at least English and Pidgin, with at least one non-Lagos site, and offer interviewer-read items so reading ability does not block participation.

## 6. Pidgin and English localisation

- Risks: Nigerian Pidgin has no single standard spelling and varies by region and age; word-for-word translation can change clinical meaning (for example "pressure" is understood differently from "blood pressure"); humour and idiom can trivialise a red flag; Pidgin may be heard as less authoritative or as insulting by some users.
- Review process: write plain English source first; native Pidgin writer drafts (not machine translation); second native speaker back-translates to English; clinician compares meaning; community test of 5 users per lesson; sign-off recorded.
- Maintain a glossary of approved terms (BP, tablet, reading, clinic, emergency) and one spelling convention.
- Hold back as English until native clinical review: urgent-care and red-flag wording, medicine names and any missed-dose or side-effect guidance, breathing contraindications, consent and legal text, anything with numbers or thresholds. Show English plus a clearly labelled "Pidgin coming" rather than an unreviewed version.
- Adopt: Pidgin for tone, motivation, myths and everyday actions first; English-with-audio for safety text until reviewed.
- Avoid: auto-translated Pidgin, mixing registers within a lesson, translating thresholds.
- Nigeria note: also expect Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo demand; plan the localisation pipeline so a third language reuses the same review steps. English-only platform stance stays unless the founder changes it (UNVERIFIED current position, see project rules).

## 7. Clinician-credited, reviewed-date governance

- Pattern (Healthily, Insight Timer clinician creators; UNVERIFIED in detail): visible author or reviewer, an updated date, listed sources, and a revision cycle.
- Required fields per lesson: reviewer name and registration, role, date of last clinical review, review-due date (for example 12 months, 6 for medicines content), source list with links, version, language version, and who recorded the Pidgin review.
- Render credit only from a real record (null-gated, same principle as the platform's reviewed-by rule): no review record, no "Reviewed by" label.
- Overdue content: show a quiet "being reviewed" state, move to a review queue, and hide medicine and red-flag lessons if overdue past a grace period.
- Changes after review: any edit to clinical text invalidates the review and re-queues it.
- Adopt: a content record with reviewer, sources, review date, review-due, language version and change log; a dashboard of overdue lessons for the Chief Medical Officer.
- Avoid: stock doctor photos, "doctor approved" without a named record, undated pages, reviewers who are creators only.
- Nigeria note: reviewers should be MDCN-registered; cite Nigerian guidelines where they exist and WHO or international hypertension guidelines otherwise (UNVERIFIED which Nigerian guideline is current).

## Design recommendations for Tarragon

- A 12-lesson, week-aligned BP course, 3 to 5 minutes each, resumable, with catch-up rather than punishment.
- Every lesson ends with one concrete action for today and one gentle teach-back question; no pass or fail.
- Audio first, transcript always visible, offline per-lesson download with size shown, 0.75x to 1.25x speed.
- Content order: why BP matters silently, measuring correctly, what numbers mean, medicines every day, salt and local foods, myths and herbals, stress and sleep, when to get urgent help, living with it.
- Myth lessons name the belief kindly, state the truth, give the action; urgent-care lesson routes to the existing emergency flow.
- Guided breathing as a separate calm tool at about 6 breaths per minute, longer exhale, 1 to 3 minute default, silent visual pacer with optional haptics, safety card first, stop always visible.
- Breathing is never claimed to lower BP or treat hypertension; always paired with "keep taking your medicines".
- Build gate: PEMAT at 70% or above and CCI at 90 or above, then a 10 to 15 person community test with the pass rule above.
- Pidgin for tone and everyday action, English plus audio for red flags, medicines and thresholds until native clinical review.
- Each lesson carries a null-gated reviewer credit, sources, review date and review-due; edits re-queue review.
- In-app clarity signals (one-tap "clear?" plus reason) and aggregate-only analytics; no staff dashboard per patient.
- Streaks framed as days you did something, never as loss; no feature depends on a notification succeeding.

## Unsafe or unsuitable for Nigeria

- Claiming breathing, apps or diet replace medicine, or implying herbal products are proven; either feeds the stop-tablets belief.
- Machine-translated Pidgin for red flags or medicine guidance.
- Streaming-only video or audio, autoplay, large downloads without size warning, heavy animation on low-end Android.
- Loss-framed streaks, shame messaging or fear framing in BP lessons.
- Numeric BP goals or salt and potassium targets in generic lessons that may conflict with a patient's individual plan or kidney status.
- "Doctor reviewed" labels with no review record, or the phrase "your doctor"; use "your care team".
- Relying on text-only lessons for low-literacy users, or reading-grade scores as proof of comprehension.
- Breathing exercises for people with unstable symptoms, chest pain or new breathlessness; breath-holding techniques.
- Using only Lagos, student or staff testers.
- Treating the unverified product-behaviour notes above as confirmed; re-check each app before citing it.
