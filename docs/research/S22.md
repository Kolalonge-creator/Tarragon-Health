# S22 research: written questions and clinical notes

Method: public help centres, published guidance and standards, read 2026-10-06. Search coverage was thin. Practo, Vezeeta, Doctolib, Clafiya, Okadoc, Helium Health, Wellahealth and most Nigerian players returned nothing usable; treat those as unresearched, not as having no policy. Claims marked (snippet) come from search snippets only. Nothing here is copied; each line says what we adapt.

## 15.2 Written (asynchronous) questions

**Submission.** Teladoc dermatology needs a description and at least three photos before a case is scheduled, and all exchange stays in an encrypted message centre; email or text only says a reply exists. Curology has a dedicated photo-guidance page. Practo allows images in chat. Babyl (Rwanda) used USSD, SMS and voice so a feature phone works. Adapt: a short structured form first, then free text, photo guidance before upload, the answer stays in the signed-in thread and the notification names nothing (INV-07). Unsuitable here: Babyl's phone-only design cannot carry photos, and Tarragon's channels are in-app, push and email only (F-02, INV-08).

**Response windows.** Paid dermatology and prescription products promise about 24 hours as an outer bound with a business-day caveat (Teladoc, MDLive, Hims, Ro; snippets for the last three). Free "ask a doctor" forms promise 24 to 48 hours (Practo, Vezeeta; snippets). Ping An Good Doctor users report slow doctor replies after a fast AI first pass. Adapt: publish 24 hours, count it from submission, show it to the patient before and after sending. Do not promise less than the rota can staff.

**Missed windows.** No platform read publishes an escalate, reassign or refund rule. Practo's FAQ offers only a general "contact support" refund. This is a gap Tarragon can own: the claim lapses back to the pool, the CMO is alerted, and the member's allowance is credited automatically.

**Follow-up.** Teladoc dermatology and Practo each give about 7 days of free follow-up in the same thread; Curology members get unlimited messaging. Adapt: 7 days of follow-up on the same question, not counted against the allowance. A clinician asking for more information is a first-class outcome, not a forced answer (Teladoc does this).

**Pricing.** Per-question pricing is rare; membership or bundled pricing dominates (K Health, Curology, Hims). Teladoc's price is not shown up front, a weakness to avoid. Tarragon: Member benefit with a monthly allowance (founder decision 2026-10-06, consistent with the 2026-10-05 membership pivot).

**Red flags.** No platform publishes its free-text red-flag logic. K Health and Babylon use AI triage plus clinician review. Tarragon's rule is stricter: a deterministic, offline screen on the device and again on the server before anything is sent, no language model in the path (INV-01, INV-06).

**Photo privacy.** Photos live inside the message thread and are part of the record (Curology, Teladoc). Nobody read documents how location metadata is handled. Adapt: strip metadata on the phone, store in a private bucket, serve only through an audited server route. No photos of intimate areas by default; that category is routed to a call.

**Nigeria and safety.** MDCN telemedicine guidance, read only through secondary sources, restricts diagnosis from asynchronous text alone and requires documented consent, records and a duty to refer. Founder decision 2026-10-06: **no text-based diagnosis, ever**; a patient who needs one is called. Data cost and power cuts: compress photos on the phone, keep an offline draft that survives a dead battery, resumable upload.

## 15.5 Clinical notes

**Signed means locked.** Jane and Healthie lock a note on signing; a later change is an addendum or amendment with a visible timestamp, author and reason; Healthie can unsign first, which we do not copy. CMS rules ask every correction to show date, author and reason and never to hide the original. Adapt: signed is immutable; an amendment is a new row linked to the original with a reason (the live `amends_note_id`).

**Status vocabulary.** FHIR `DocumentReference.docStatus`: preliminary, final, amended, entered-in-error. Map: draft to preliminary, signed to final, amended to amended; a retraction is entered-in-error and stays retrievable, flagged, never deleted.

**AI drafts.** Abridge, Nabla and Heidi all produce a draft the clinician reviews and signs. Adapt: any AI-authored note stays a draft, is marked as such, and the patient never sees it (INV-11). Drafting itself is S23.

**Patient access.** The US Cures Act releases notes to the patient without delay with narrow exceptions (preventing harm, privacy); the exception must be documented. Not Nigerian law, so a design pattern only. Adapt, per founder decision: the patient sees the published summary by default and can open the signed note after a clinician releases it, with a recorded withhold reason where it is not released. Protected categories (reproductive health and similar) stay withheld unless the CMO releases them.

**Corrections requested by the patient.** HIPAA's right to amend gives the provider 60 days to accept or deny, a written denial with reasons, and a statement of disagreement linked to the entry. The NDPA 2023 gives a correction right for inaccurate or misleading data; deadlines and clinical-record carve-outs are unconfirmed (counsel, OQ-153). Adapt: the patient's request is attached beside the note, a clinician responds, and nothing is deleted.

**Unsigned notes.** Hospitals commonly remind at 24 to 72 hours; no authoritative source found. The deadline is a PROPOSED value in versioned config.

**Failure modes designed against.** A patient sees a draft (enforced in row-level security, not app code); a clinician edits after the patient has read (blocked; an amendment is the only route and the patient is told); unsigned notes pile up (reminder, then the lead sees it).
