(fundraising / partnership prioritisation), not a build order — nothing here requires engineering
work on its own. Where it overlaps a *product* decision (e.g. how an NGO-funded cohort actually

## The core decision

**Do not spend significant effort applying directly to NGO-only grants as Tarragon Health Ltd.**
Most NGO-restricted grant programmes reject a commercial company at the eligibility-screening stage
before the application is even read on its merits — that's a structural mismatch (grantor wants a
non-profit/NGO legal entity), not a weakness in Tarragon's pitch. Chasing these is low-probability
effort that's better spent elsewhere.

**Do pursue NGOs — but not as a grant source. As one of four partnership roles instead:**

1. **Implementation partner** — an NGO that can lead a grant application (they hold NGO status,
   Tarragon can't), with Tarragon as the named technology/delivery partner inside their proposal.
2. **Customer** — an NGO buys Tarragon's screening, enrolment, and follow-up platform directly,
   the same way an employer or HMO would.
3. **Subcontracting route** — Tarragon delivers a defined slice of an NGO's *already-funded* NCD
   (non-communicable disease) programme, paid by the NGO out of a grant they already hold.
4. **Credibility partner** — an NGO relationship or state-government pilot becomes a reference /
   outcome-data source Tarragon can use for its own commercial and investor conversations.

## Funding priority order

Pursue these four paths in this order, not as parallel equal-weight bets:

1. **Commercial / impact-investor funding** — health-tech funds, angels, venture studios, corporate
   innovation funds. This is the fastest, least-restricted path and matches Tarragon's actual legal
   form (a commercial company).
2. **Grant funds explicitly open to companies / social enterprises** — not NGO-only programmes.
   **SFH CoElevate is the clearest current fit** (Society for Family Health's programme accepts
   companies/social enterprises, not just NGOs) — this is the one grant channel worth applying to
   directly right now, precisely because it doesn't have the NGO-only eligibility problem described
   above. Any other grant candidate should be screened for the same test before time is spent on it:
   *does this programme's eligibility criteria actually admit a commercial entity?*
3. **NGO/government partnership pilots** — structured so the NGO (or government programme) holds
   the grant and pays Tarragon for technology and delivery support, per the four partnership roles
   above. This is where NGO relationships pay off — as a paying counterparty, not a grantor.
4. **Employer/HMO revenue** — Tarragon already supports institutional health programmes commercially;
   this is existing, working revenue infrastructure, not a new channel to build.

## Outreach line for NGO/PHC conversations

> "Tarragon can provide the digital continuity-of-care layer for your hypertension and diabetes
> programme — enrolment, care-team workflows, remote monitoring, adherence follow-up and anonymised
> outcome reporting."

This is the pitch to lead with when approaching an NGO or primary-health-care (PHC) programme — it
positions Tarragon as infrastructure for a programme they already run or are funded to run, not as
a grant applicant competing with them.

## Practical target

**Build 3–5 real NGO/PHC partnerships and sell or subcontract Tarragon into their existing
programmes.** Do not chase NGO funding as if Tarragon were itself an NGO — the legal-entity mismatch
makes that a low-yield use of founder time. The partnerships are the goal; grant money flowing to
Tarragon directly is, at best, a rare exception (SFH CoElevate) rather than the default expectation.
