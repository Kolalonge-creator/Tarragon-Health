# Prescribing, take-anywhere (T2-11): clinical safety case and hazard log

**Status: v0.2 DRAFT, UNSIGNED. Prepared by the assistant on 2026-11-05 (v0.2 the same day, after the founder asked for HZ-RX-03, HZ-RX-05 and HZ-RX-06/11 to be closed before prescribing opens; see the change log) for the Chief Medical Officer, Dr Kola Longe, who is also the founder and the DPO and the sole reviewer of this platform (no second reader is required; the residual risk of having no independent check is stated once in HZ-RX-15 and gates nothing).** It becomes v1.0 only when the CMO has read it, corrected it, and recorded the attestation `clinical_safety_case_current` on the Prescribing guard; that platform record is the signature. Nothing here claims compliance with a standard or approval by any regulator (MDCN, PCN, NAFDAC or NDPC); the shape follows a DCB0129-style hazard log as a structure only. Ratings marked `(s)` are the assistant's suggestions and are the CMO's to change.

Guard this document supports: **`prescribing_enabled`** (a master switch, pressed by the CMO). After T2-11 (`20261104233059_t2_11_prescribing_take_anywhere.sql`, branch `feat/t2-prescribing-take-anywhere`, PR #1717, **not yet merged or applied live when this was written**) the master has three conditions: at least one active senior doctor with a verified, unexpired registration and a real credential number (`private.prescriber_ready_count()`, read from the data), this safety case (`clinical_safety_case_current`, attested by the CMO), and the CMO pressing the switch. **Do not attest against this document until T2-11 and T2-11b are both live**: before T2-11 the master still carries the five partner-pharmacy conditions and cannot be met, and before T2-11b (`20261105100015_t2_11b_prescribing_safety_gaps.sql`, branch `feat/t2-11b-prescribing-safety-gaps`, cut from #1717, not merged or applied when this was written) the controls this version describes for HZ-RX-03, HZ-RX-05 and HZ-RX-06/11 do not exist.

## 1. What the switch opens, and what it does not

**Opens:** signing a prescription, draft to signed (`private.enforce_prescription_rules`, which refuses `prescribing_guard_off` while the master is off for anyone but a test patient and a test clinician together). A signed prescription is projected into `public.medications` and is what the patient downloads as a PDF and takes to any pharmacy.

**Does not open:**
- **Partner pharmacy collection.** Choosing a partner, the pharmacist's lists, counter checks, supplies and questions all sit behind `private.pharmacy_collection_on()`, which after T2-11 needs the master **and** `private.pharmacy_partner_collection_ready()` (a choosable partner pharmacy, the collection rules confirmed, the pharmacy notification sender attested). Tarragon has no contracted pharmacy, so this stays closed whatever the master says, and the patient screen does not offer "Choose where to collect" (`public.partner_pharmacy_collection_available()`).
- **Controlled medicines.** Never prescribed on this platform, master or not (HZ-RX-04).

**Never behind the master (open today):** writing a draft, downloading the form for an already signed prescription, the public check at `/verify-rx/[token]` (`public.verify_prescription_public`), recording a supply from the QR (`public.record_prescription_supply_public`), the staff phone desk (`desk_verify_prescription`, `desk_record_prescription_supply`), and taking a prescription back from a pharmacy.

## 2. What the path does (read from the code and a local replay of every migration on 2026-11-05)

1. **Who may sign.** `public.prescribe_medication` (and `amend_medication`) refuse unless the caller is org staff (`private.is_org_staff`), holds prescribing authority (`private.has_prescribing_authority`: an active `clinical_staff` row at `senior_medical_officer` or `chief_medical_officer`) and has access to this patient (`private.clinician_has_patient_access`, the care-team tie). Since T2-11b the signing trigger also refuses a signer whose licence fails `private.prescriber_licence_ok` (verified, unexpired, a real credential number), with `prescriber_licence_not_verified`. The signer is stamped from the session, never from the request (`enforce_prescription_rules`: "a prescription can only be signed by the clinician acting").
2. **What must be written.** Quantity and a positive duration in days are required (`prescription_quantity_duration_required.sql`); dose, frequency, route, repeats, indication and instructions are carried. Signed content is frozen: a change needs a new, newly signed version (`amend_medication`), and the old one is superseded.
3. **Checks at signing.** `private.check_prescription_safety` over `private.prescription_safety_findings`: a controlled medicine is a hard stop with no override; an allergy name match, an empty allergy list and the same drug already active each stop the signing until the signer gives a reason (and, for an empty list, confirms the list was checked); the reason is stored on the prescription (`prescriptions.safety_checks`). `private.enforce_bp_prescribing_safety` refuses an ACE inhibitor, ARB or thiazide in a patient recorded as pregnant, and conflicting blood pressure classes.
4. **Interaction checks (T2-11b).** At signing, `private.prescription_safety_findings` now checks the new drug against the patient's current active medicines with the SIGNED interaction dataset (class pairs and same-class duplicates, read from `interactions` for the version whose content still hashes to the signed hash) and the active SIGNED ingredient rule set (`private.prescription_interaction_findings`). Drug classes come from the add check's own patterns, copied into `private.drug_class_patterns` and kept identical by `class-patterns-sql.test.ts`; ingredients come from the published brand map, then the catalogue, then the typed name. Contraindicated is a hard stop with no override; caution stops until the doctor gives a reason, which is stored on the prescription and written to the audit log (`prescription.safety_override`); info is shown and stored but does not stop. With no signed set active, nothing can be signed (`SAFETY_CHECK_UNAVAILABLE`). An allergen in the same class as the new drug (penicillin allergy, amoxicillin) stops until a reason is given (`allergy_class_match`). Before T2-11b: the signed interaction dataset v1 (85 rules, 43 class pairs and 42 duplicate groups, `interaction_dataset_versions`, signed by the CMO 2026-11-03, guard `interaction_check_enabled`) and the signed ingredient rule set v2 (350 BNF-cited rules, 79 contraindicated and 271 caution, `medicine_interaction_rule_sets`, signed 2026-11-03, guard `medicine_interaction_rules`) run in the **patient's own** add-a-medicine flow, as advice. On the clinician side, the chart's Medication safety panel (`MedicationSafetyPanel`, `loadMedicationSafety`, the `@tarragon/medicines` drug-safety rules) shows interactions, duplicate therapy, renal dosing and allergy findings across the current list, and after a clinician adds a medicine `assessMedicationSafetyBestEffort` turns any contraindicated finding into a `clinician_alerts` row. Those surfaces remain as a second look.
5. **What the patient gets.** `private.stamp_prescription_lifecycle` gives every clinician medicine an Rx number (`TRG-RX-YYYY-NNNNNN`), a six-character verification code, a 64-character public token for the QR, and an expiry (duration plus 30 days, or six months). The patient sees the Rx number, code and validity on the medicines list and downloads the PDF (`/api/patient/prescriptions/[id]/pdf`, mobile `/api/mobile/prescriptions/[id]/pdf`). The PDF prints the letterhead, patient name, patient number, date of birth, age and sex, the medicine, dose, route, frequency, duration, quantity, repeats and instructions, the prescriber's name, MDCN number and signature image, the date, the Rx number, the code and the QR. It refuses to issue for a medicine that is not clinician-sourced, stopped, superseded, expired, missing an identifier, controlled, or whose prescriber's registration is unverified or a placeholder (`buildPrescriptionPdfData`, `prescription-pdf-data.test.ts`). Every download writes an `audit_log` row; a failed audit write refuses the download.
6. **What the pharmacist does.** Scans the QR or opens the printed link (`/verify-rx/[token]`, no account). The check shows the status (active, expired, superseded, cancelled, controlled), the medicine and dose, supplies used and permitted, and the prescriber's credential only when the licence is verified. The pharmacist may record the supply there (name, pharmacist name and registration, attestation). Supplies are counted; a supply the patient says did not happen is excluded once disputed. Without the QR, the pharmacist types the Rx number and code into `/verify-rx` (T2-11b, `public.verify_prescription_by_code`), which opens the same page the QR opens; ten wrong codes for one Rx number in an hour lock that number for the hour, and the page also limits each connection. The pharmacy can still phone Tarragon, where staff check the number and code on the prescription desk.
