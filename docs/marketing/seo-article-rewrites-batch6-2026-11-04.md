practice (foot checks, insulin storage in Nigeria, the 15 g low-sugar treatment).

- `diabetic-foot-care-daily-habit` says a red, smelly or non-healing foot wound needs a doctor "that same week". The signed
  diabetes pathway treats an acute diabetic foot as an emergency, and batch 5 (approved) says **the same day**. Changed below.
- `diabetes-medications-explained` (batch 4, live) gives the low-sugar treatment as "three teaspoons of sugar in water". The
  signed pathway says **3 to 4 teaspoons**, and lists a tablespoon of honey; it also says not to use chocolate. Proposed fix:
  replace that paragraph with the pathway's wording (shown at the end of this document).

## 1. Just diagnosed with diabetes: what happens now

**Slug:** `diabetes-just-diagnosed`
**Title:** Just diagnosed with diabetes? What happens next, and what to ask
**Description:** What to expect after a new diabetes diagnosis: the tests you will have, the first steps in treatment, and the questions to ask at your first review.

### I have just been told I have diabetes. Is this an emergency?
Usually not. For most people with type 2 diabetes, a new diagnosis is a starting point, not an emergency. You now have information that lets you and your care team act early, which is far better than not knowing. The exception is someone who is very unwell: vomiting, very drowsy, breathing fast or deeply, or very thirsty with a lot of weight loss. That needs a hospital the same day.

### How is the diagnosis confirmed?
In someone without clear symptoms, diabetes is confirmed with a second abnormal result, for example a second HbA1c or fasting blood sugar. Your doctor will also decide which type you have, type 1, type 2, or diabetes in pregnancy, because each is treated differently. **[CHECK]**

### What tests will I have at the start?
Because type 2 diabetes can be present for years before it is found, the first review looks for any effects already present: **[CHECK against the pathway's baseline list]**
- HbA1c, to know your starting point;
- kidney blood tests (creatinine and eGFR) and a urine test for protein;
- cholesterol;
- blood pressure, weight and waist measurement;
- a foot examination, including feeling and pulses;
- an eye (retinal) examination, arranged early.

### What happens with treatment?
Most people with type 2 diabetes start with changes to food, activity and weight alongside metformin, the usual first medicine. If your sugar is very high or you have symptoms, your doctor may start more than one medicine, or insulin, from the beginning. Start any medicine as prescribed; you do not need to master your diet first. The two work together.

### What should I ask at my first review?
- What is my HbA1c, and what target are we aiming for?
- Which medicine am I starting, and what should I expect from it?
- Should I check my sugar at home, and if so, when?
- Which symptoms mean I should contact you before my next review?
- When is my next check, and what will it include?

### Do I need to stop eating my usual food?
No food is banned outright. Swallow, rice, yam and plantain can stay on your plate in smaller portions, with more vegetables and protein. Sugary drinks are the most useful thing to cut. Our article on eating with diabetes in a Nigerian kitchen has practical portions and swaps.

### What changes, and what stays the same?
You can still work, travel and enjoy food with family. What changes is a new habit of checking in on your health on a schedule, rather than only when something feels wrong. Most people with type 2 diabetes who follow their plan live long, full lives. The complications people fear come from years of uncontrolled sugar, not from the diagnosis.

### Where can I get support?
Learning about your diabetes makes a real difference to how well it is controlled. Your care team will explain your plan, and you can ask questions in the app between reviews. Family support helps too: share what you are learning with the people you eat with.

### Guidelines this article follows
Federal Ministry of Health, Nigeria: National Guidelines on the Prevention, Control and Management of Diabetes Mellitus. Diabetes Association of Nigeria: National Clinical Practice Guidelines for Diabetes Management in Nigeria. Tarragon's diabetes pathway. NICE guideline NG28: Type 2 diabetes in adults.
Where Nigerian national guidance and NICE differ, this article follows the Nigerian guideline, which is also what Tarragon's care team uses.

## 2. Diabetic foot care: the daily habit that prevents amputation

**Slug:** `diabetic-foot-care-daily-habit`
**Title:** Diabetic foot care: the 2-minute daily check that prevents amputation
**Description:** Why diabetes affects the feet, how to check your feet every day, the habits that protect them, and which foot problems need a doctor the same day.

### Why does diabetes affect the feet?
Years of raised blood sugar can reduce feeling in the feet, so a cut, blister or stone in your shoe may go unnoticed, and can slow how well small wounds heal. Most amputations in people with diabetes start with a small foot wound that was not noticed or treated in time. That is why daily checks matter so much.

### How do I check my feet every day?
Take two minutes each evening: **[CHECK]**
- Look at the top, the sole, the heel and between every toe. Use a mirror, or ask someone to look if you cannot see well.
- Look for cuts, blisters, cracks, swelling, redness, or a change in colour.
- Feel for any area that is hotter than the rest, or newly numb.
- Check inside your shoes for stones, nails or rough seams before you put them on.
You can log your daily foot check in the app.

### What protects my feet?
- Wash your feet daily in lukewarm water, and dry well, especially between the toes.
- Moisturise dry skin, but not between the toes.
- Cut nails straight across, not into the corners. If you cannot see or reach well, ask for help.
- Never walk barefoot, indoors or outdoors, including on hot ground or sand.
- Wear shoes that fit well and do not need breaking in, with socks that are not tight.
- Do not cut corns or hard skin yourself, or use corn plasters or chemicals; ask your care team.
- Keep feet away from hot water bottles, fires and hot sand.

### How often should a professional check my feet?
Your feet should be examined at diagnosis and at least once a year, including feeling (often with a thin nylon thread called a monofilament) and the pulses in your feet. If your feet are at higher risk, they are checked more often. **[CHECK]**

### Which foot problems need a doctor the same day?
Contact your care team the same day, or go to a hospital, if you have: **[CHECK: matches the signed acute diabetic foot rule]**
- a cut, blister or sore that is red, swollen, hot, smelly or leaking;
- redness spreading from a wound;
- a wound that is not starting to heal within a few days;
- a foot that has become suddenly swollen, hot or changed shape, even without pain;
- a toe or part of the foot turning dark or black.
Caught early, almost every diabetic foot problem can be treated. Delay is what turns a small wound into a serious one.

### What else helps keep feet healthy?
Keeping blood sugar in your target range, not smoking, and controlling blood pressure and cholesterol all protect the nerves and blood vessels in your feet.

### Guidelines this article follows
Federal Ministry of Health, Nigeria: National Guidelines on the Prevention, Control and Management of Diabetes Mellitus. Diabetes Association of Nigeria: National Clinical Practice Guidelines for Diabetes Management in Nigeria. Tarragon's diabetes pathway. NICE guideline NG19: Diabetic foot problems, prevention and management.
Where Nigerian national guidance and NICE differ, this article follows the Nigerian guideline, which is also what Tarragon's care team uses.

## 3. Starting insulin: what to expect

**Slug:** `starting-insulin-what-to-expect`
**Title:** Starting insulin: injections, storage in hot weather, and what to expect
**Description:** Why insulin is needed, the common types, how to inject, how to store insulin in Nigeria's heat or without power, and how to handle low sugar.

### Does needing insulin mean I have failed?
No. People with type 1 diabetes need insulin from the start, because the body makes little or none. In type 2 diabetes, the body's own insulin often falls over the years, so many people need insulin added later. It is not a sign that you did something wrong. It is the right tool for what your body needs now, and often the most effective way to protect it.

### What types of insulin are used in Nigeria?
- Soluble (regular) insulin: short-acting, taken before meals.
- NPH (isophane) insulin: intermediate-acting, gives background cover.
- Premixed 30/70 insulin: a mix of the two, usually taken twice a day before breakfast and the evening meal.
- Newer long-acting and rapid-acting insulins, where available and affordable.
Your doctor will choose the type and dose with you. People with type 1 diabetes usually take a background insulin plus insulin with meals. **[CHECK]**

### How do I inject insulin?
- Inject into the fat just under the skin, not into muscle: the stomach, thighs or upper arms.
- Change the spot each time within the same area, to stop lumps forming under the skin. Lumps can stop insulin working properly.
- Use a new needle as often as you can; do not share pens, syringes or needles.
- Most people describe the injection as a quick pinch, less uncomfortable than a finger-prick test.
Your care team will go through your first injection with you before you do it alone.

### How do I store insulin in Nigeria's heat?
Using the approach in Tarragon's diabetes pathway: **[CHECK]**
- Keep unopened insulin in a fridge at 2 to 8°C. Never freeze it, and never leave it in direct sun or a hot car.
- The pen or vial you are using can stay at room temperature, below about 25 to 30°C, for about a month.
- Without reliable power, keep insulin cool in a clay pot or wrapped in a wet cloth, away from sunlight.
- Throw insulin away if it looks clumped, frosted or discoloured, or if it is past its date.

### What is low blood sugar, and what do I do?
Insulin can make blood sugar fall too low. Signs include shaking, sweating, hunger, a fast heartbeat, confusion and irritability. If this happens: **[CHECK against the signed pathway]**
1. Take 15 g of fast sugar straight away: 3 to 4 teaspoons of sugar or glucose tablets in water, half a glass of a regular (not diet) soft drink, or a tablespoon of honey.
2. Wait 15 minutes and check again. If it is still low, repeat.
3. When you feel better, eat your next meal or a snack.
Do not use chocolate or fatty food to treat a low; they work too slowly. If someone is drowsy, confused or cannot swallow, do not give anything by mouth; get emergency help. Always carry something sugary.

### What if I am ill and not eating?
Never stop insulin in type 1 diabetes, even if you are not eating; this is the commonest cause of a dangerous condition called diabetic ketoacidosis. When you are unwell, check your sugar more often, keep drinking fluids, and contact your care team for advice on your doses. **[CHECK]**

### Will I always need insulin?
People with type 1 diabetes always need insulin. In type 2 diabetes, it depends on your own body and your other treatment; your doctor will review it with you. Many people find that, after the first few weeks, injecting becomes a routine they hardly think about.

NICE guidelines NG28: Type 2 diabetes in adults, and NG17: Type 1 diabetes in adults.

## 4. Cancer warning signs worth not ignoring

**Slug:** `cancer-warning-signs-worth-not-ignoring`
**Title:** Cancer warning signs: symptoms that need a doctor's check, not a wait
**Description:** Early signs of the cancers most common in Nigeria, including breast, cervical, prostate, bowel and liver cancer, and when to see a doctor.

### Do these signs mean I have cancer?
Usually not. Most lumps, coughs and changes in bowel habit have an ordinary cause. Knowing the signs is about getting checked rather than waiting, so that on the rare occasion it is cancer, it is found early, when treatment works best.

### Which general signs should I get checked?
See a doctor promptly if you have any of these, especially if they last more than three weeks or keep coming back: **[CHECK]**
- a new lump or swelling anywhere, including the breast, armpit, neck or testicle;
- unexplained weight loss;
- a cough, hoarseness or difficulty swallowing that lasts more than three weeks;
- coughing up blood;
- a sore, ulcer or wound that will not heal;
- a mole that changes size, shape or colour, or bleeds;
- unusual tiredness, night sweats or fevers without a clear cause.

### What are the early signs of breast cancer?
A new lump in the breast or armpit, a change in the size or shape of a breast, skin that dimples or looks like orange peel, a nipple that turns in or has discharge (especially blood), or a rash on the nipple. Breast cancer in Nigeria is often found late; checking your breasts and reporting any change early saves lives. Men can get breast cancer too.

### What are the warning signs of cervical cancer?
Bleeding after sex, between periods or after the menopause, unusual vaginal discharge, or pain during sex. Early cervical cancer often causes no symptoms at all, which is why regular screening matters, even if you feel well.

### What are the signs of prostate cancer?
Passing urine more often, especially at night; a weak stream or difficulty starting; feeling the bladder has not emptied; or blood in the urine. These are much more often caused by a harmless enlarged prostate, but they deserve a check, particularly for men over 45 of African ancestry, who have a higher risk.

### What are the signs of bowel cancer?
Blood in the stool or bleeding from the bottom, a change in bowel habit lasting more than three weeks (looser, more frequent, or constipation), stomach pain or a lump, or unexplained tiredness from anaemia.

### What are the signs of liver cancer?
Pain or a lump in the upper right side of the stomach, swelling of the stomach, yellow eyes or skin, and weight loss. Long-term hepatitis B is a major cause in Nigeria, so a hepatitis B test, and vaccination if you are not immune, is one of the best protections.

### What happens when I see a doctor?
Your doctor asks about your symptoms, examines you and decides whether you need a test, a scan or a referral to a specialist. Most of these visits end with reassurance. When cancer is found, finding it early gives you more treatment options and a better outcome.

Federal Ministry of Health, Nigeria: National Cancer Control Plan. Tarragon's annual health check pathway. NICE guideline NG12: Suspected cancer, recognition and referral.
