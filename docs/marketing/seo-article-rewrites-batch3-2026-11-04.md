## 1. What is a normal blood pressure, and when should you worry?

**Slug:** `normal-blood-pressure`
**Title:** What is a normal blood pressure? Ranges for adults, by age, and when to worry
**Description:** What the two numbers mean, the normal range for adults, whether it changes with age, what counts as high or low, and when to see a doctor.

### What do the two numbers mean?
A blood pressure reading has two numbers, written like 120/80 and said "120 over 80". The first, systolic, is the pressure in your arteries when your heart squeezes. The second, diastolic, is the pressure between beats, when the heart rests. Both matter, and either can be too high.

### What is a normal blood pressure for an adult?
As a widely used guide for adults, measured at rest in a clinic or pharmacy: **[CHECK ranges]**
- Below 130/85: normal.
- 130/85 to 139/89: high-normal. Worth checking again regularly and looking at salt, weight and activity.
- 140/90 or higher on repeated readings on different days: high blood pressure (hypertension).
Readings taken at home are usually a little lower, so at home an average of 135/85 or more is treated as high.

### Is 120/80 normal?
Yes. 120/80 is a healthy reading for an adult. It used to be described as the "ideal" figure, and it remains a good one.

### Does normal blood pressure change with age?
Blood pressure tends to rise with age because arteries stiffen, but the healthy range for adults does not change: a reading of 140/90 or more is high whether you are 30 or 70. In older adults, especially those who are frail, your doctor may agree a slightly different target with you, to avoid dizziness and falls. **[CHECK]**

### Why is one high reading not a diagnosis?
Blood pressure moves all day. Stress, caffeine, rushing, even the measurement itself (the "white-coat effect") can push a single reading up. That is why high blood pressure is diagnosed from a pattern: several readings taken at rest, on different days, often with a week of home readings.

### What counts as low blood pressure?
A reading below about 90/60 is usually described as low. **[CHECK]** Many healthy people, especially young, slim and active adults, have readings in this range and feel well; for them it is normal and needs no treatment. Low blood pressure matters when it causes symptoms such as dizziness or fainting, especially when standing up, blurred vision, or tiredness.

### What can cause low blood pressure, and what helps?
Common causes include not drinking enough fluid, standing up too quickly, some blood pressure and other medicines, heat, and long periods of bed rest. What often helps: drinking enough water, getting up slowly from lying or sitting, and asking your doctor to review your medicines. Do not add extra salt or stop a medicine without advice. Sudden low blood pressure with confusion, cold clammy skin, fast breathing or fainting can be a sign of a serious illness such as severe infection or blood loss, and needs a hospital straight away. **[CHECK]**

### Why does blood pressure matter so much in Nigeria?
High blood pressure is very common among Nigerian adults and is a leading cause of stroke, heart failure and kidney disease, yet it usually causes no symptoms at all. Feeling fine tells you nothing about your blood pressure. The only way to know is to measure it.

### When should I see a doctor?
- If your readings are 140/90 or above more than once: book a review. High blood pressure is very treatable.
- If a reading is 180/120 or above: rest 5 minutes and check again. If it is still that high, or at any reading you have chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness or numbness on one side, difficulty speaking, a sudden severe headache or blurred vision, go to the nearest hospital now.
- If you have never had your blood pressure checked: check it at a pharmacy, a laboratory or with a home monitor, then at least once a year.

## 2. Early signs of diabetes, and who should get tested

**Slug:** `diabetes-early-signs`
**Title:** Early signs of diabetes, the tests that confirm it, and who should get tested
**Description:** Early signs of diabetes in men and women, why many people have none, normal blood sugar ranges, and who should be tested.

### What are the early signs of diabetes?
The classic warning signs are:
- being unusually thirsty;
- passing urine more often than usual, especially at night;
- losing weight without trying;
- feeling tired much of the time;
- blurred vision;
- cuts and sores that heal slowly;
- repeated skin, gum or urine infections.
Any of these deserves a blood sugar test, not waiting to see.

### Are there early signs specific to women or men?
In women, repeated vaginal thrush (itching and discharge) or urine infections can be an early sign, as can diabetes in a past pregnancy. In men, difficulty getting or keeping an erection can be an early sign of raised sugar affecting blood vessels and nerves. These are common and treatable, and worth mentioning to a doctor, who will often check your sugar. **[CHECK]**

### Can you have diabetes without any symptoms?
Yes, and many people do. Type 2 diabetes builds slowly and the body adapts, so many people live with raised sugar for years before it is found, sometimes only when a complication appears. Feeling fine is not evidence that your sugar is fine. That is why people at risk are tested even without symptoms.

### Who should get tested even without symptoms?
Testing is worth it if any of these apply:
- a parent, brother or sister with diabetes;
- age over 40;
- excess weight, especially around the waist;
- high blood pressure or high cholesterol;
- diabetes in a past pregnancy, or a baby who weighed more than 4 kg at birth;
- polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS);
- a previous borderline sugar reading.
The more that apply, the stronger the case. **[CHECK]**

### Which tests show diabetes, and what are the normal ranges?
Any laboratory can do these. Nigerian laboratories often report sugar in mg/dL; some use mmol/L. **[CHECK all thresholds]**
- Fasting blood sugar (no food for 8 hours): normal is below 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L). 100 to 125 mg/dL (5.6 to 6.9 mmol/L) is prediabetes. 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or above suggests diabetes.
- HbA1c, the 3-month sugar test: below 5.7% is normal, 5.7% to 6.4% is prediabetes, and 6.5% or above suggests diabetes. No fasting is needed.
- Random blood sugar: 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or above, together with symptoms, suggests diabetes.
A diagnosis is usually confirmed with a second test unless the result is very high with clear symptoms.

### What do signs of type 1 diabetes look like?
Type 1 diabetes usually starts in children and young adults, and comes on quickly, over days or weeks: great thirst, passing a lot of urine, rapid weight loss and tiredness. If these are followed by vomiting, stomach pain, deep or fast breathing, a fruity smell on the breath or drowsiness, go to a hospital immediately; this can be diabetic ketoacidosis, an emergency. **[CHECK]**

### What if my result comes back high?
A raised result is a starting point, not a sentence. Prediabetes is often reversed with changes in food, movement and weight. Diabetes that is managed early and consistently is compatible with a long, full life. The complications come from years of unmanaged sugar, not from the diagnosis itself. Your doctor will explain your result and agree a plan with you.

## 3. Blood group and genotype: what they are, and why every Nigerian should know theirs

**Slug:** `blood-group-genotype-explained`
**Title:** Genotype and blood group: compatibility for marriage, explained simply
**Description:** The difference between genotype and blood group, a genotype compatibility chart for couples, and how to get a reliable test in Nigeria.

### What is the difference between genotype and blood group?
They are two different tests that answer different questions:
- Blood group (A, B, AB or O, plus rhesus positive or negative) tells you whose blood you can safely receive in a transfusion.
- Genotype (AA, AS, SS, AC, SC and others) tells you which type of haemoglobin you carry, including whether you carry the gene for sickle cell disease.
Knowing one tells you nothing about the other.

### What do AA, AS, SS, AC and SC mean?
- AA: the usual haemoglobin, no sickle gene.
- AS: sickle cell trait. You carry one sickle gene. Most people with AS are healthy and have no symptoms.
- SS: sickle cell disease (sickle cell anaemia).
- AC: you carry one haemoglobin C gene; usually no symptoms.
- SC: a form of sickle cell disease, often milder than SS but still a lifelong condition.
**[CHECK]**

### Which genotypes are compatible for marriage?
Every child gets one gene from each parent, so the chance of each outcome is the same in every pregnancy. For each pregnancy: **[CHECK percentages]**
- AA and AA: all children AA.
- AA and AS: each child has a 1 in 2 chance of AA and a 1 in 2 chance of AS. No child will have SS.
- AA and SS: every child will be AS. No child will have SS.
- AS and AS: each child has a 1 in 4 chance of AA, a 1 in 2 chance of AS, and a 1 in 4 chance of SS (sickle cell disease).
- AS and SS: each child has a 1 in 2 chance of AS and a 1 in 2 chance of SS.
- SS and SS: every child will have SS.
- AS and AC: each child has a 1 in 4 chance of SC, a form of sickle cell disease.
"Compatible" is a choice for each couple, not a rule. Knowing both genotypes before marriage or pregnancy turns a 1 in 4 chance into an informed decision rather than a discovery after birth.

### Is AS a disease?
No. AS (sickle cell trait) is not sickle cell disease. Most people with AS live completely normal lives. It matters mainly for family planning, when both partners carry a sickle gene. Rarely, people with AS can have problems with severe dehydration, extreme exertion or very high altitude, so drinking enough water during heavy exercise is sensible.

### Can my genotype change?
No. You are born with your genotype and it stays the same for life. If you have two results that disagree, one of them is wrong; this happens, usually from a laboratory error or from testing soon after a blood transfusion. Repeat the test at a reputable laboratory, ideally one that uses haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC, and keep the written result. **[CHECK]**

### Why does blood group matter in pregnancy?
If a mother is rhesus negative and her baby is rhesus positive, her body can make antibodies that affect a later pregnancy. This is preventable with an injection (anti-D) given at the right times, which is why your blood group and rhesus factor are checked early in pregnancy. **[CHECK]**

### How do I get tested in Nigeria?
Both are simple blood tests that most laboratories offer, with no fasting needed. Results usually come back within a day or two. If you have never been tested, or you are planning marriage or pregnancy and don't have a written result you trust, get tested together with your partner. On Tarragon, your care team can write the request for you, and you take it to a laboratory you choose.

### What if we are both AS?
You are not alone; it is common in Nigeria. Talk to a doctor together before or early in pregnancy. They can explain the options, which in some centres include testing a pregnancy for sickle cell disease early on, and what support is available if a child has sickle cell disease. **[CHECK]**

## 4. Lower blood pressure naturally: the changes that work

**Slug:** `lower-blood-pressure-naturally`
**Title:** How to lower blood pressure naturally: changes that work, and how much they help
**Description:** Salt, food, weight, exercise, alcohol, sleep and stress: the natural changes that lower blood pressure, and what they cannot replace.

### Can you lower blood pressure without medicine?
Yes, often by a useful amount. For people with mildly raised blood pressure, lifestyle changes alone are sometimes enough. For most people with high blood pressure, they work alongside medicine and can mean needing fewer tablets or lower doses. Never stop a prescribed medicine on your own; blood pressure rises again quietly.

### 1. Eat less salt, especially the salt you can't see
Most salt comes from seasoning cubes, instant noodles, processed and tinned foods, bread and snacks, not from the salt spoon. Halving seasoning cubes often does more than avoiding table salt. Cook from fresh ingredients, flavour food with onions, garlic, ginger, pepper, scent leaf and curry leaf, and taste before you add salt. Most adults should keep to less than one level teaspoon of salt a day in total. **[CHECK]**

### 2. Eat more vegetables, fruit and beans
A diet rich in vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains, with less fried and processed food, lowers blood pressure on its own. Potassium in beans, plantain, leafy vegetables and fruit helps balance salt. If you have kidney disease or take certain blood pressure medicines, ask your doctor before greatly increasing potassium. **[CHECK]**

### 3. Lose some weight if you carry extra
Blood pressure tracks body weight closely. Even a 5% weight loss, about 4 kg for someone who weighs 80 kg, lowers readings for most people. Slow and steady weight loss lasts better than a crash diet.

### 4. Move most days
About 30 minutes of brisk walking, or similar activity, on most days lowers blood pressure even without weight loss. It needs no gym: a regular route and a habit. If you have not been active, start with shorter walks and build up.

### 5. Drink less alcohol, and stop smoking
Regular heavy drinking raises blood pressure; cutting down brings it back down. Smoking does not cause long-term high blood pressure on its own, but it adds greatly to the risk of heart attack and stroke, so stopping protects your heart and arteries. **[CHECK]**

### 6. Sleep and stress
Short or broken sleep pushes blood pressure up over time. Loud snoring with gasping or choking at night can be sleep apnoea, which raises blood pressure and is worth raising with a doctor. Ongoing stress raises blood pressure too; regular activity, time with people you trust and enough rest all help.

### Do zobo, garlic or herbal remedies lower blood pressure?
Unsweetened zobo (hibiscus) and garlic may lower blood pressure slightly in some studies, but the effect is small and varies, and sweetened zobo adds sugar. Herbal mixtures vary in strength from bottle to bottle and some interact with medicines. They can be part of a healthy diet, not a replacement for proven treatment. Tell your doctor about any herbal remedy you take. **[CHECK]**

### How quickly will my blood pressure come down?
Changes in salt and activity can lower readings within a few weeks; weight loss lowers them steadily as the weight comes off. Check your blood pressure at home while you make changes. Seeing your own numbers fall is the strongest motivation there is, and it shows your doctor whether your medicine can be adjusted.
